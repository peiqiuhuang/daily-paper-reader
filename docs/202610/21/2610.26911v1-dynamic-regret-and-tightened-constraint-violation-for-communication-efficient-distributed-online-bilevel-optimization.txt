# Dynamic Regret and Tightened Constraint Violation for Communication-Efficient Distributed Online Bilevel Optimization 

Fan Li, Xinlei Yi, Guanghui Wen, Yang Shi and Tao Yang 

Abstract —This paper investigates the distributed online bilevel optimization problem with time-varying inequality constraints. In this setting, a multi-agent network is confronted with ahierarchical decision-making task characterized by coupling between the inner and outer optimization levels. We first propose a communication-efficient single-loop distributed online primal– dual algorithm based on communication compression, which tracks both the inner-level solution and the Hessian-inverse-vector product using only one update per-round. We show that this algorithm achieves a dynamic network regret bound of 

O(T max {θ1,1−θ1} + T θ1 PT + H1,T + E1,T ), where θ1 ∈ (0 , 1) ,

PT is the outer-level path variation, H1,T is the inner-level path variation, and E1,T is the gradient path variation alongside a network cumulative constraint violation bound of O(T 1−θ1/2),which is further reduced to O(T 1−θ1 ) under Slater’s condition. Furthermore, to eliminate the dependence of regret on the inner-level and gradient path variation, we propose a novel adaptive multi-loop distributed online algorithm to control the inner-level approximation accuracy at each round. By dynamically adjusting the number of inner-level updates based on a gradient-norm tolerance condition, this algorithm yields an improved dynamic network regret bound of O(T max {θ1,1−θ1} + T θ1 PT )

which depends solely on the outer-level path variation while maintaining the same network cumulative constraint violation bound. Finally, numerical simulations verify the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms. 

Index Terms —Bilevel Optimization; communication compres-sion; distributed algorithms; time-varying constraints 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Bilevel optimization has recently garnered extensive at-tention in the fields of machine learning and control as a rigorous mathematical paradigm for characterizing hierarchical decision making. Many complex tasks in reality, such as meta-learning [1], hyperparameter optimization [2], and reinforce-ment learning [3] often exhibit a natural bilevel hierarchical structure. Driven by the explosive growth of data and distributed com-putational resources, extending classical centralized bilevel optimization to distributed settings has become a compelling

Specifically, the authors of [4] propose the MA-DSBO algorithm utilizing the Hessian-inverse-vector prod-uct (HvP) technique without explicit computation of full Hessian or Jacobian matrices. The authors of [5] develop the decentralized alternating gradient method by leveraging the penalty function method. To eliminate inner computational loops, the authors of [6] present a single-loop personalized distributed algorithm that characterizes the impact of data heterogeneity. Furthermore, the authors of [7] combine time scale separation techniques to design a Hessian-free and single-loop algorithm for nonconvex-strongly convex settings. However, the aforementioned algorithms rely on static time-invariant cost functions, making them unsuitable for real-world applications in highly dynamic environments. For example, hierarchical resource allocation determines local allocations under an upper-level control parameter [8], while time-varying demands and resource availability motivate online costs and constraints [9]. To address the case of dynamic environments, significant strides have been made in online bilevel optimization [10]– [13]. For example, pioneer work [10] formulates the online bilevel optimization framework to achieve sublinear regrets. Furthermore, the authors of [11] establish the online algorithm in the nonconvex-strongly convex setting with the window-averaging strategy, which aggregates information over a recent time window to mitigate temporal fluctuations. The authors of [12] utilize the Lagrangian reformulation to recast the online bilevel problem into single-level constrained optimization, thereby developing algorithms relying exclusively on first-order information. The authors of [13] develop novel efficient algorithms for online bilevel optimization that attain optimal regret bounds by dynamically adjusting inner iterations to track subproblem optimum drift and employing the window-averaging strategy. In many networked applications, however, the data and computational resources involved in bilevel decision making are naturally distributed among multiple agents and vary over time. These settings motivate the study of distributed online bilevel optimization. Existing bilevel optimization algorithms typically rely on Neumann series or conjugate gradient meth-ods to approximate the Hessian inverse matrices with high precision [14]–[16]. These approaches inevitably introduce computationally expensive nested loops for approximating the inner-level solution and the Hessian inverse matrices at each iteration. It is also not yet clear whether the approaches in [6], [7] based on single-loop update mechanisms to obviate these arXiv:2610.26911v1 [math.OC] 17 Oct 2026 XX, NO. XX, XXXX 2026 2

computational loops can be generalized to online settings. Moreover, unlike single-level online optimization [17], the dynamic network regret in online bilevel optimization typically depends on inner-level and gradient path variation [10]–[13]. These schemes fail to guarantee sublinear regret when se-vere environmental changes cause significant inner-level path variation. This motivates us to explore novel approaches to eliminate this dependence on inner-level path variation. Practical physical systems are frequently subjected to com-plex time-varying inequality constraints, for example, dynam-ically varying capacity, power, and resource constraints in networked systems. To evaluate constraint satisfaction in on-line settings, cumulative constraint violation, which indepen-dently penalizes instantaneous violations, is widely adopted in constrained online optimization [18], [19]. This metric is stricter than the conventional long-term constraint-violation metric in [20]. As a standard constraint qualification, Slater’s condition postulates the existence of a strictly feasible interior point [21]. Leveraging this feasibility margin, the authors of [18] improve the long-term constraint violation by re-placing the original constraint functions with non-negative clipped versions. However, as pointed out in [19], this clipping operation destroys the strict feasibility margin. While the authors of [19] successfully tighten the bound in distributed single-level convex optimization by directly maximizing the regularized Lagrangian, extending this advancement to online bilevel optimization is nontrivial. Under the bilevel nested structure, the primal–dual updates dynamically couple with the continuous drift of the inner-level optimal trajectories. Most existing studies on online bilevel optimization such as [10]– [12] are confined to unconstrained settings or simple feasible domains. It remains unclear whether sublinear cumulative constraint violation and corresponding bound reductions under Slater’s condition can be achieved in online bilevel optimiza-tion with time-varying inequality constraints. As the scale of the network expands, the prohibitive commu-nication overhead rapidly emerges as the primary bottleneck to the overall efficiency of the distributed algorithm. Com-munication compression serves as an effective approach to alleviate this bottleneck, which has been extensively studied in single-level optimization. For instance, the authors of [22] develop an error compensation strategy to achieve linear convergence, while [23] and [24] establish a unified framework accommodating both relative and absolute compression errors. Furthermore, the authors of [25] propose a distributed online gradient descent algorithm with communication compression attaining an O(√T ) regret bound. [26] and [27] respectively extend compression mechanisms to the push-sum protocol and the bandit feedback setting. However, directly extending these compression schemes to online bilevel optimization is nontriv-ial. Since both the inner-level solution and the Hessian-inverse-vector product are tracked based on the current outer-level iterate, perturbations induced by compression in the outer-level iterate also affect these tracking processes. Consequently, the resulting compression errors propagate into the hypergradient approximation rather than remaining only in the consensus dynamics. It remains unclear how to design communication-efficient online bilevel optimization algorithms with commu-nication compression. In this paper, we consider the distributed online bilevel optimization problem with a convex outer-level objective, strongly convex inner-level problems, and time-varying in-equality constraints. The main contributions are outlined as follows. (i) We propose two distributed online primal–dual algo-rithms with communication compression by combin-ing inner-level solution tracking with iterative HvP ap-proximation. Different from existing distributed bilevel algorithms [4]–[7], which are primarily developed for static environments, and centralized online bilevel al-gorithms [10]–[13], our framework simultaneously ac-counts for distributed implementation, online variations, time-varying inequality constraints, and communication compression. In particular, the first algorithm retains a single-loop architecture with one inner-level update and one HvP tracking step per round, while the sec-ond algorithm utilizes an adaptive multi-loop tracking mechanism to eliminate the regret dependence on inner-level path variation. Compared with centralized online bilevel methods [11], [13], [28], the proposed frame-work additionally accounts for network disagreement, compressed communication, and time-varying inequal-ity constraints. Compared with distributed constrained online methods [19], [29], the bilevel structure further requires tracking both the time-varying inner solution and the HvP approximation, whose errors are coupled with the distributed primal dynamics. (ii) We show in Theorem 1 that the single-loop algorithm establishes an O(T max {θ1,1−θ1} +T θ1 PT +H1,T +E1,T )

dynamic network regret bound and an O(T 1−θ1/2) net-work cumulative constraint violation bound, which is dependent on the outer-level path variation PT , the inner-level path variation H1,T and the gradient path variation E1,T . Moreover, the network cumulative con-straint violation bound is further reduced to O(T 1−θ1 )

under Slater’s condition. The tightening is achieved by retaining the original constraints in the regularized Lagrangian, thereby preserving the negative feasibility margin supplied by the common Slater point. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to achieve such an improvement for distributed online bilevel optimization. (iii) We show in Theorem 2 that the adaptive inner-loop algorithm establishes an improved O(T max {θ1,1−θ1} +

T θ1 PT ) dynamic network regret bound which depends solely on the outer-level path variation. The resulting regret bound no longer depends explicitly on the inner-level and gradient path variations H1,T and E1,T . Dif-ferent from [10], [11] that utilize a prefixed number of inner gradient steps, our adaptive algorithm dynam-ically adjusts the number of inner-level updates based on a gradient-norm tolerance condition. Remark 3: Theorems 1 and 2 establish a fundamental trade-off between computational efficiency and robustness against environmental non-stationarity. Algorithm 1 benefits from a single-loop structure, demanding one inner-level update and HvP tracking step per iteration. While this ensures a minimal computational burden, its dynamic network regret inevitably depends on the inner-level path variation, yielding the bound 

O(T max {θ1,1−θ1} + T θ1 PT + H1,T + E1,T ). In highly volatile environments, drastic environmental changes can cause H1,T 

and E1,T to grow linearly, thereby precluding a sublinear regret guarantee. This theoretical limitation necessitates the de-sign of Algorithm 2. Nonetheless, this mathemat-ical decoupling is achieved at the expense of an increased computational overhead. To satisfy the tolerance condition, the adaptive mechanism dynamically invokes multiple inner iterations per-round, which accumulates a larger number of gradient and HvP evaluations over the horizon. This paper investigated the distributed online bilevel op-timization problem subject to time-varying inequality con-straints. We proposed two communication-efficient primal– dual algorithms. First, a single-loop distributed algorithm was developed by integrating an iterative approximation approach with communication compression. We analyzed its dynamic network regret alongside the network cumulative constraint vi-olation that is tightened under Slater’s condition. Furthermore, to eliminate the regret’s dependence on the inner-level and gradient path variation, we introduced a novel adaptive multi-loop algorithm. By dynamically adjusting the number of inner-level updates based on a gradient-norm tolerance condition, this algorithm achieved an improved dynamic network regret bound that relies solely on the outer-level path variation. Fu-ture research will explore extending these frameworks to fully asynchronous communication protocols and online bilevel optimization under zeroth-order feedback. APPENDIX
