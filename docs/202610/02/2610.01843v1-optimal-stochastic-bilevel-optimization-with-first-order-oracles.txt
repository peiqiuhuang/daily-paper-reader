Abstract 

We study nonconvex–strongly-convex bilevel optimization under a stochastic first-order oracle. We introduce MRT-FD, a single-loop first-order method that simultaneously tracks the upper-level variable, the lower-level solution, and the auxiliary response arising from implicit differentiation of the hyperobjective. MRT-FD performs one update of each variable per iteration and approximates the second-order derivative actions using order-p finite differences. For any fixed finite smoothness order p ≥ 1 in the lower-level variable, MRT-FD finds an ε-stationary point using O(ε−4−2/p ) stochastic gradient queries. We also prove a matching Ω( ε−4−2/p ) oracle lower bound. The lower-bound construction starts from a hard nonconvex minimization chain with a stronger stochastic oracle, and lifts it to a bilevel problem through a sinusoidal coupling with a scalar lower-level variable. Consequently, the dependence on ε is optimal for every fixed finite p, closing the upper–lower complexity gap in this stochastic first-order oracle setting.

This improvement raises a fundamental question. Existing lower bounds do not characterize how the complexity of a first-order stochastic bilevel oracle depends on the available smoothness order p. It remains unclear whether the exponent 4+2 /p reflects an intrinsic information-theoretic barrier. 

What is the optimal complexity of stochastic NC–SC bilevel optimization under a first-order oracle, and how does higher-order smoothness in the lower variable change this complexity? 

We answer the question above by sharpening both the upper and lower complexity bounds. 

A single-loop first-order algorithm. We develop Moving-Target Tracking with Finite Differences (MRT-FD), a single-loop method that requires order-p smoothness only for g with respect to the lower variable y, for any p ≥ 1. We introduce the auxiliary vector z⋆(x) as the unique solution of 

H(x, y ⋆(x)) z⋆(x) = ∇yf (x, y ⋆(x)) .

MRT-FD maintains yk and zk to track the moving targets y⋆(xk) and z⋆(xk) while simultaneously updating the outer variable xk. It uses order-p finite differences to reconstruct the required second-order derivative actions from first-order queries, while projection keeps zk bounded and controls the resulting finite-difference errors. Our analysis couples objective descent with the tracking errors ∥yk − y⋆(xk)∥2 and ∥zk − z⋆(xk)∥2 through a moving-target Lyapunov function that jointly controls target motion, finite-difference bias, and stochastic variance. For fixed 

p, it achieves O(ϵ−4−2/p ) complexity, matching the ϵ dependence of Chen et al. (2026b) while removing the logarithmic factor. 

Lower bound for finite smoothness order p. We establish a matching lower bound under the same assumptions and oracle model. The construction embeds a hard nonconvex chain into a bilevel problem with a scalar lower-level variable through a sinusoidal coupling. Setting a coupling scale to δ = ϵ1/p preserves the required order-p smoothness while keeping the resulting hyperobjective close to the hard chain. We then construct a stochastic oracle in which informative observations occur only through a rare Bernoulli event with probability 

ϖ = Θ( δ2ϵ2). A random-rotation argument limits progress along the hidden chain for arbitrary adaptive randomized algorithms. Combining a chain of length T = Θ( ϵ−2) with the reveal probability gives the lower bound of Ω( T /ϖ ) = Ω( ϵ−4−2/p ). 

1.1 Related Work 

Bilevel optimization with second-order information. Classical bilevel methods compute the hypergradient using second-order information. Iterative differentiation (ITD) differentiates through a finite lower-level trajectory (Domke, 2012; Shaban et al., 2019; Bolte et al., 2021), whereas approximate implicit differentiation (AID) approximates the inverse-Hessian–gradient product through linear-system or Neumann-type solvers (Pedregosa, 2016; Ghadimi and Wang, 2018; Grazzi et al., 2020; Ji et al., 2021). In stochastic NC–SC bilevel optimization, methods such as stocBiO (Ji et al., 2021) and SUSTAIN (Khanduri et al., 2021) construct hypergradient estimators using Hessian- and Jacobian-vector products. SOBA/SABA (Dagréou et al., 2022) and MA-SOBA (Chen et al., 2024b) instead track the associated linear-system solution jointly with the lower and upper-level variables. These methods are structurally related to MRT-FD through linear-system tracking, but require second-order derivative information. 2First-order bilevel optimization. Fully first-order methods often rely on value-function or penalty reformulations, including BOME (Liu et al., 2022), penalty-based bilevel gradient methods (Shen and Chen, 2023), and F 2SA (Kwon et al., 2023). Subsequent work improves the first-order oracle complexity through refined penalty and two-timescale schemes (Kwon et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025). More recently, SGHA (Gu et al., 2026b) develops a single-loop method based on a constrained reformulation of lower-level stationarity and finite-difference approximations, achieving O(ϵ−6) complexity, but requires additional assumptions in the boundedness. FdeHBO (Yang et al., 2023) is the closest structural comparison to MRT-FD: it tracks the auxiliary linear-system solution using projection and finite-difference Hessian-vector approxi-mations. Its guarantees, however, rely on stronger stochastic assumptions, including stochastic smoothness and unbiased stochastic Hessian estimates with bounded variance. FdeHBO uses variance reduction and reuses the same sample across multiple query points, whereas MRT-FD uses fresh independent samples and order-p finite differences. Their analyses track different auxiliary errors: FdeHBO uses the current lower-level linear-system solution, while we directly control ∥zk − z⋆(xk)∥2.Chen et al. (2026b) show that pth-order smoothness in the lower variable can improve the complexity from ˜O(ϵ−6) to ˜O(ϵ−4−2/p ). Their F 2SA-p method uses high-order finite differences within a penalty formulation over perturbed lower-level problems, and assumes higher-order smoothness of both f and g in y. In contrast, our additional order-p smoothness condition is imposed only on g.

Lower bounds. Ji and Liang (2023) established lower complexity bounds for convex–strongly-convex and strongly-convex–strongly-convex bilevel problems, while Dagréou et al. (2024) derived matching lower bounds for finite-sum bilevel empirical-risk minimization. Beyond the strongly-convex lower-level setting, Chen et al. (2024a) showed hardness results for finding stationary points of the hyperobjective when the lower-level problem is only convex. For stochastic NC– SC bilevel optimization, Kwon et al. (2024) establish lower bounds under a y⋆-aware oracle, where unbiased bounded-variance stochastic gradients are guaranteed only locally around y⋆(x), making their setting not directly comparable to ours. More recently, Chen et al. (2026a) prove an Ω( κ4.5ϵ−4) lower bound for NC–SC bilevel optimization, where κ denotes the condition number, while concurrent work by Gu et al. (2026a) establishes an Ω( κ8ϵ−6) lower bound. Our result complements these works by characterizing the effect of higher-order smoothness in the lower variable: for a fixed p, we prove a Ω( ϵ−4−2/p ) lower bound.

# 4 Matching Lower Bound under First-Order Oracles 

We first recall a hard instance construction for stochastic nonconvex minimization and establish its lower bound under a stronger oracle notion. We then lift this construction to a bilevel instance and show that the bilevel oracle call can be simulated by the oracle in the minimization hard instance.
