Abstract 

Modern text-to-video (T2V) models generate visually com-pelling videos, yet meaning-preserving changes in prompt wording can substantially alter semantic realization and over-all quality. Existing prompt rewriting modifies the inference input, whereas conventional post-training constructs super-vision from one prompt formulation at a time; neither ex-plicitly optimizes reliability across equivalent forms. We in-troduce VARI (Verified Alignment over Representations of 

Intent), a group-structured framework that treats automati-cally verified formulations of the same visual intent as a shared supervision unit. VARI builds matched form–seed rollout pools, cross-scores each video against all forms, and applies a wording-independent structured intent audit to identify valid realizations and hard semantic failures. Group-Consistent Distillation (GCD) aligns every verified form with inter-changeable valid realizations, while Form-Contrastive Flow-DPO (FC-DPO) learns group-level preferences from audited positive–negative pairs, prioritizing same-seed, different-form contrasts. On HunyuanVideo-1.5, VARI attains the high-est T2V-CompBench, VBench, and StoryEval scores among the evaluated methods. On prompt-form extensions of T2V-CompBench and VBench, it improves weakest-form align-ment over the strongest prompt-level baselines by 2.45 and 3.55 points and reduces cross-form drop by 30.5% and 32.2%, respectively, while preserving visual quality. Human evalu-ation, natural-caption controls, prompt-enhancement exper-iments, diversity analyses, and transfer to LTX-2.3 further support the robustness and transferability of these gains, while VARI requires no additional inference-time module. 

## Introduction 

Large text-to-video generators based on diffusion or flow matching now achieve strong visual fidelity, motion quality, and instruction following (Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020; Lip-man et al. 2023; Kong et al. 2024). Yet robust semantic re-alization remains challenging: meaning-preserving changes in prompt wording can alter entity counts, spatial relations, action bindings, event realization, or overall generation qual-ity. Such variation is distinct from desirable visual diver-sity, since appearance, composition, and motion details may change while the core visual intent should remain invariant.

For example, consider “A boy reading in front of a bench” and “A bench is positioned behind a boy who is reading.” They express the same spatial relation, yet a generator may realize it under one wording and violate it under the other. Inference-time prompt rewriting can improve a selected input, but it does not train the generator itself to behave reli-ably across equivalent formulations. Reward- and preference-based post-training instead samples multiple rollouts for a prompt, scores them with automatic rewards, and learns from preferred and rejected examples (Liu et al. 2025b; Rafailov et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2026; Liu et al. 2025a; Xue et al. 2025). However, supervision constructed from one formu-lation at a time cannot distinguish intent-level robustness from wording-specific success. Semantic-preserving prompt forms provide complementary linguistic evidence for con-structing more reliable supervision. We introduce VARI (Verified Alignment over 

Representations of Intent), a group-structured intent-alignment framework that treats automatically verified prompt forms as a shared supervision unit. For each visual intent z, we construct P(z) = {p0, p 1, . . . , p K }, generate a matched form–seed rollout pool, and cross-score every candidate against all forms. A generation seed determines the initial sampling noise; reusing it across forms controls stochastic variation, so same-seed differences more directly expose wording sensitivity. Because equivalent forms share one structured semantic target, score variation for a fixed video is treated as scoring-view dispersion , rather than form-dependent ground truth. A frozen, wording-independent structured intent auditor checks atomic entities, counts, relations, action roles, and temporal commitments without observing prompt wording. The audit determines semantic type, while cross-form evidence ranks candidates within type, yielding reliable positives and visually plausible hard negatives. As illustrated in Figure 1, Group-Consistent Distillation 

(GCD) trains every verified form on interchangeable valid re-alizations, whereas Form-Contrastive Flow-DPO (FC-DPO) optimizes audited success–failure preferences under every form, prioritizing same-seed, different-form contrasts. The resulting generator requires no additional inference-time module. Our main contributions are threefold: 

• Verified prompt-form groups as supervision units. We define prompt-form reliability over verified formulations of the same visually testable intent and use these groups, paired with shared seeds, as the supervision unit for T2V post-training. 

• Audited cross-form supervision. We develop a compiler that cross-scores every rollout against all verified forms and uses a wording-independent structured intent audit to separate semantic validity from hardness ranking, yield-ing group-consistent positives and audited hard negatives. 

• Complementary group-level objectives. We introduce GCD, which trains every verified form on interchangeable valid realizations, and FC-DPO, which optimizes audited success–failure preferences under every form while pri-oritizing same-seed, different-form comparisons. 

## Related Work 

Diffusion, flow, and video generators. Modern visual generators build on denoising diffusion, latent diffusion, flow matching, rectified flows, diffusion transformers, and classifier-free guidance (Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020; Rom-bach et al. 2022; Lipman et al. 2023; Liu, Gong, and Liu 2023; Peebles and Xie 2022; Ho and Salimans 2022). Video generation has progressed from early pixel- and latent-space diffusion systems, cascaded spatial–temporal upsampling, and motion adaptation (Ho et al. 2022a,b; Singer et al. 2022; Blattmann et al. 2023a; Wang et al. 2023; Guo et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2023; Kondratyuk et al. 2023; Bar-Tal et al. 2024) to large transformer- and flow-based backbones with stronger video latent spaces, including HunyuanVideo-1.5, LTX-2.3, and Wan2.2 (Blattmann et al. 2023b; Chen et al. 2024; Ma et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2024; Polyak et al. 2024; Kong et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2025a; HaCo-hen et al. 2025; Lightricks 2026; Wan Team 2025a,b). VARI operates after pretraining and reorganizes supervision for an existing video DiT rather than modifying its backbone. 

Preference and reward-based alignment. Generative-model alignment includes direct preference objectives, policy-gradient fine-tuning, reward backpropagation, and reward-model-free denoising preference learning (Rafailov et al. 2023; Black et al. 2023; Wallace et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2023). Image-generation studies have developed pref-erence datasets, reward models, and input-side prompt op-timization (Kirstain et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2023b; Hao et al. 2023). For video, VADER, VideoDPO, VideoReward, OnlineVPO, DenseDPO, DanceGRPO, and VisionReward improve reward learning, preference construc-tion, temporal granularity, or on-policy optimization (Prab-hudesai et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2025b,a; Zhang et al. 2026; Wu et al. 2025b; Xue et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2026). Recent LocalDPO, Diffusion-APO, and Dual-IPO further study lo-calized supervision, trajectory-aware updates, and iterative reward–generator alignment (Huang et al. 2026; Zhu et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2026). VARI instead structures supervision across verified linguistic realizations of one visual intent. Main comparisons use VideoDPO and OnlineVPO because both admit matched HunyuanVideo-1.5 data and generation budgets; the newer methods change the underlying data or optimization pipeline. 

## The VARI Framework 

Semantic-Preserving Prompt-Form Groups 

Let z denote a visual intent and let p0 be its original prompt. A verified prompt-form group 

P(z) = {p0, p 1, . . . , p K } (1) contains variants preserving the same structured target Tz ,a set of atomic, visually testable commitments over objects, actions, attributes, relations, roles, and temporal order; un-constrained appearance may vary. Gemini 3.5 Flash gen-erates ten training rewrites, Qwen3.6-27B verifies critical fields, and filtering retains four forms spanning lexical, syn-tactic, and caption-style variation; the roles are exchanged for benchmark groups. Human audit validates 190/200 training and 98/100 benchmark pairs, and 97/100 complete bench-mark groups preserve Tz . Removing the three flagged groups changes headline robustness metrics by at most 0.11 points. Five forms and four shared seeds are adopted from held-out scale diagnostics, which show diminishing gains beyond five forms; details are in the supplement. Each intent uses a seed set Sz shared across all verified forms, so form comparisons reuse the same initial sampling noises. The complete rollout pool is 

X (z) = {Gθroll (pi, s ) : pi ∈ P (z), s ∈ S z }. (2) Here Gθroll is the frozen rollout checkpoint used to construct supervision, and s indexes the initial sampling noise. Mining uses the full within-intent form–seed pool; candidates are never paired across intents.

## Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

Backbone and training pool. We use HunyuanVideo-1.5 (Wu et al. 2025a) with a unified 480p, 16:9, 121-frame, 24-fps, 50-step protocol. The training pool contains 5,000 independently constructed source intents balanced across at-tributes, relations, actions, interactions, numeracy, and multi-event composition; each has four verified rewrites and four shared seeds, yielding 100,000 rollouts. All trainable meth-ods use matched groups and generation budgets. Offline methods share the same pool, whereas OnlineVPO performs on-policy refresh under the same total budget. Main com-parisons are Base, VideoDPO, OnlineVPO, GCD, and full VARI, with additional controls in the ablation. 

Evaluation principle. Mining scores are not reused for reporting. Benchmark results are recomputed with the offi-cial VBench, T2V-CompBench, and StoryEval evaluator im-plementations. VBench and T2V-CompBench use the same 480p, 16:9, 121-frame, 24-fps, 50-step generation configu-ration for every system; StoryEval follows its official 10s set-ting. These extensions introduce no learned evaluator: they reuse the official per-video scores unchanged and add only verified prompt-form grouping and intent-level aggregation, yielding VBench-R and T2V-CompBench-R. 

Prompt-form robustness protocol. For each of the 1,400 T2V-CompBench prompts, we construct five verified forms. VBench-R uses matched official semantic dimensions; each rewritten form inherits the source category and structured target, and only the generation-side wording changes. Data independence and natural-caption controls. 

Source intents are split before rewriting, so every form of an intent remains in one partition. VBench, T2V-CompBench, StoryEval, VATEX, and MSR-VTT prompts are excluded from training, calibration, and checkpoint selection. Normal-ized exact matching, lexical retrieval, and frozen sentence-embedding retrieval followed by manual review find no shared visual intent; training and benchmark groups also use disjoint rewrite instructions and verifier instances. The sup-plement reports the full overlap audit and held-out VATEX (Wang et al. 2019) and MSR-VTT (Xu et al. 2016) controls scored by VisionReward (Xu et al. 2026). VATEX/MSR-VTT weakest-form scores improve from 61.92/59.24 for On-lineVPO to 65.09/62.34 for VARI. 

Audit, coverage, and statistics. The protocol is frozen before mining, and only the union of the validation-fixed pos-itive and negative shortlists is audited. Across 5,000 intents, 87.4% yield an audited positive, 64.6% an audited negative, and 61.4% a preference pair; 71.7% of retained pairs are same-seed, different-form. VideoDPO, OnlineVPO, GCD, and VARI use three matched training seeds; full deviations and source-intent paired tests are reported in the supplement. 

Compute efficiency. All trainable methods share the 100,000-video rollout budget. VARI adds one-time, par-allelizable offline cross-scoring, shortlist auditing, and a second optimization stage, giving modest overhead over VideoDPO and the same cost regime as OnlineVPO, while leaving per-video inference cost unchanged; details are in the supplement.

## Conclusion 

We present VARI, a group-structured intent-alignment framework for robust text-to-video post-training. VARI treats verified formulations of the same visual intent as a shared supervision unit, separates semantic validity from hardness ranking through a wording-independent audit, and trains the generator with GCD and FC-DPO. The two stages play com-plementary roles: GCD shares interchangeable valid real-izations across forms, while FC-DPO sharpens the pref-erence boundary against audited semantic failures. Across HunyuanVideo-1.5 and LTX-2.3, VARI improves standard alignment and weakest-form reliability, reduces cross-form drop and mixed-success failures, and preserves visual quality, seed diversity, and sensitivity to genuine semantic changes. These results show that robustness to meaning-preserving prompt variation can be learned directly at the generator level, complementing inference-time prompt rewriting with-out adding deployment-time modules. ## Prompt-Form Groups and Semantic Preservation 

Visual intents and admissible forms 

A visual intent z is represented by the unordered form set 

P(z) = {p0, p 1, . . . , p K } and a structured target Tz . The tar-get records entities and counts, attributes and states, actions and participant roles, interactions, spatial relations, temporal order, and explicit scene or camera constraints. The forms may differ lexically and syntactically but must preserve ev-ery field in Tz ; the group is therefore an equivalence class of visually testable content, not loosely related captions. Training source intents are instantiated from a de-terministic, non-benchmark structured schema balanced across attributes, relations, actions, interactions, numer-acy, and multi-event composition. Gemini 3.5 Flash (gemini-3.5-flash ) generates ten candidates per source with a capped refill for malformed outputs. Qwen3.6-27B independently verifies every candidate against the source-derived Tz . For benchmark extensions, generator and verifier roles are exchanged and their prompts are disjoint from the training compiler. The endpoint IDs, access dates, and decoding settings are frozen in the released configura-tion. 

Semantic-preserving prompt-form rewriting template 

Input and goal. Given one T2V prompt, produce independently usable, linguistically diverse rewrites of the same visual event. 

Preserve. Every entity and count, attribute/state, action and par-ticipant role, interaction, spatial relation, temporal order, scene constraint, and explicit camera/style/lighting/duration/text re-quirement. 

Reject. Any added, removed, weakened, strengthened, disam-biguated, or reversed visual commitment. 

Permitted variation. Lexical substitution, clause reordering, role-preserving voice conversion, caption-like phrasing, and con-cise or expanded syntax without visual change. 

Output. Return the source, each rewrite, and its transformation-family label in structured form. 

The accepted transformation families are summarized in Table 6. They are used for audit and balance; the labels are not provided to the generator during training. 

Family Allowed surface variation 

Lexical Synonyms and local wording changes without semantic strengthening Syntactic Clause reordering, attachment changes, or role-preserving voice conversion Caption-like Natural descriptive-caption syntax with the same commitments Concise Removal of linguistic redundancy while retaining every visual constraint Relation-focused Reordering that foregrounds an existing spatial or interaction relation Action-focused Reordering that foregrounds an existing action or event sequence

Structured equivalence verification 

A rewrite is retained only when its relation to the source is unambiguous. Verification is field-wise rather than based on a single similarity score. This avoids accepting a fluent paraphrase that silently changes a count, swaps semantic roles, or modifies a spatial relation. 

Visual-intent equivalence verification template 

Task. Compare an original text-to-video prompt with one can-didate rewrite. Judge semantic preservation only; do not judge writing style or predicted video quality. 

Check independently. Entities and exact counts; attributes and states; actions; agent–patient roles; interactions; spa-tial relations; temporal order; scene constraints; explicit camera/style/lighting/duration/text constraints; and added, re-moved, or altered commitments. 

Decision rule. Reject if any critical field differs, if a new visual commitment is introduced, if a required commitment disap-pears, or if a previously explicit relation becomes ambiguous. Treat an uncertain critical field as rejection rather than guess-ing. 

Output structure. Return a status for every field, lists of added/removed/changed commitments, a final accept/reject decision, and a concise reason. 

Illustrative audit example 

For the source intent “A dog jumps over a low wooden fence in a grassy field,” a rewrite may move the setting to the beginning or use a role-preserving paraphrase such as “In a grassy field, a dog clears a short wooden fence.” A rewrite is rejected if it introduces a tracking camera, changes the fence height, adds a person, replaces over with through , or weakens the jump into generic movement. This example illustrates the field-wise verification policy.

Complementarity of the two stages 

Two distinct controls    

> GCD controls conditional disagreement around positives sam-pled from a valid support set. It contains no rejected sample and does not establish a preference boundary. FC-DPO or-ders positives above independently audited negatives under every form; matched seeds reduce stochastic confounding. The stages therefore address complementary failure modes: shared positive support first, explicit rejection of verified hard failures second.

The complete supervision-construction and two-stage opti-mization flow is summarized in Algorithm 1 of the main pa-per. ## Auxiliary Natural-Caption Controls 

Verified VATEX multi-caption diagnostic 

VATEX provides multiple English captions for the same source video, making it a useful auxiliary test bed for natural-caption grouping (Wang et al. 2019). We first collect candi-date caption groups from the same video and then apply the same semantic-preservation verification used by VARI: re-tained captions must agree on the main entities, participant roles, principal action, core interaction or spatial relation, and temporal scope. Groups that describe different moments or omit a required relation are discarded. The verification retains 619 VATEX intent groups, all disjoint from the VARI training intents and checkpoint-selection data. ## Human and Qualitative Evaluation Protocols 

Human comparison 

Human evaluation follows a blinded pairwise design on 200 held-out intent groups. Annotators receive the visual in-tent, the verified prompt forms, and anonymized video sets from two methods. Method identity and left/right placement are hidden and randomized. Separate questions assess in-tent faithfulness, consistency across equivalent forms, visual quality, and overall preference. Each comparison is judged by three independent annotators; ties are retained rather than forced into a directional choice. Win, tie, and loss are raw percentages over all judgments and therefore sum to 100%. Decisive-win rates excluding ties are reported only as an auxiliary statistic. 

Human-evaluation questions     

> Intent faithfulness. Which set more accurately depicts the required entities, actions, attributes, and relations?
> Prompt-form consistency. Which set preserves the same in-tended content more consistently across the equivalent forms?
> Visual quality. Which set has better perceptual quality, tem-poral coherence, and fewer visible artifacts?
> Overall preference. Considering faithfulness, consistency, and quality together, which set is preferred?

For the comparison with OnlineVPO, Table 32 reports integer outcomes and the percentages derived from them. The decisive-win rates excluding ties are 69.84% for intent faithfulness, 73.11% for cross-form consistency, 63.93% for visual quality, and 70.24% overall. Confidence intervals use intent-cluster bootstrap; Krippendorff’s α is computed on the three-way win/tie/loss labels.

Qualitative cases and video comparison protocol 

The main paper reports two held-out T2V-CompBench source prompts, one from spatial relationships and one from action binding. Each source is paired with one verified semantic-preserving form. The left/right prompt pairs are: 

• Spatial relation: “A boy reading in front of a bench.” and “A bench is positioned behind a boy who is reading.” 

• Action binding: “A man is reading a newspaper on a park bench, while a squirrel is climbing a tree.” and “While a squirrel climbs a tree, a man reads a newspaper on a park bench.” All compared methods use the same prompt string and infer-ence configuration within each row. Spatial-relation case. Across both forms, Base and VideoDPO place the reader on the bench, replacing the re-quested in-front-of /behind relation with a frequent reading-on-a-bench configuration. One baseline clip additionally du-plicates the reader, but the shared failure is the relation change rather than the mere presence of a counting artifact. The two VARI clips preserve the intended relation throughout the sequence while allowing an unconstrained posture change: the boy sits on the grass in one form and stands in front of the bench in the other. The complete clips also expose tem-poral behavior hidden by the three-frame layout. Baseline book pages can flip repeatedly or change geometry abruptly, whereas the VARI outputs maintain a more coherent reading interaction; the seated result contains a brief localized page turn and the standing result remains stable without changing the spatial arrangement. 

Action-binding case. The baseline videos display several ways in which a partially correct scene can still violate the compositional intent. The man–newspaper event is of-ten present, but squirrels may be duplicated, appear on the bench rather than the tree, or fail to maintain a single upward trajectory; one clip instead duplicates the reader. The two VARI clips preserve both assignments over time: the man continues reading and the squirrel remains on the tree while moving upward. The full clips also show stable newspaper handling with limited page motion, rather than a still-frame coincidence, and make the climbing progression visible from low to higher positions on the trunk. Checkpoint selection precedes qualitative inspection. We then select held-out groups with clear weakest-form improve-ments and interpretable failure modes; the quantitative con-clusions are based on the full benchmark results.
