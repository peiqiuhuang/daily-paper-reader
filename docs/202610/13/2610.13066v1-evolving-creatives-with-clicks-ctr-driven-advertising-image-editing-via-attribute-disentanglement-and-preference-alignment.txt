Abstract 

The optimization objective for e-commerce advertising cre-atives has shifted from aesthetic quality to online business metrics like click-through rate (CTR). Recent methods gener-ate appealing ad images from product images by constructing CTR-based reward signals and refining prompt generation via Reinforcement Learning. Such paradigms equally penalize all attributes within an underperforming image, even though some are actually beneficial to clicks. In this work, we propose Evolving Creatives with Clicks (ECC), which reformulates creative optimization as fine-grained editing rather than gen-eration from scratch. ECC selectively refines attributes that hurt CTR while preserving those that contribute positively. Specifically, faced with a rich attribute space across mas-sive images, ECC first identifies 28 attributes significantly correlated with CTR via ANOVA and Random Forest regres-sion. Based on attribute contrasts between images with dis-parate CTRs, we instruction-tune a prompt model to generate editing suggestions, then refine it with real click preferences since reference-derived suggestions are not uniformly benefi-cial. Furthermore, we introduce Consistent Preference Opti-mization (CPO) as ECC’s preference optimization strategy, which enforces consistency across semantically equivalent shuffled suggestions to mitigate DPO overfitting and improve preference generalization. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ECC significantly improves the CTR of ad images, out-performing state-of-the-art models by a substantial margin. The complete data pipeline, including attribute extraction and analysis, the resulting dataset, and source code will be released upon acceptance. 

Introduction 

Advertising creatives, especially advertising images (ad im-ages), serve as a key lever to capture user clicks (Mishra et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2021). Early works primarily focused on enhancing the aesthetics or controlla-bility of generated ad images (Li et al. 2023; Du et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024, 2025a). However, such improvements rarely translate into click-through rate (CTR) gains, as the criteria correlate loosely with online business performance.

The most prominent paradigm leverages CTR-derived rewards to fine-tune a prompt model via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al. 2023), thereby generating prompts that yield better-performing images. Despite achieving cer-tain CTR improvements, this paradigm has three major lim-itations. First, as shown in Fig. 1(a), DPO treats prompts de-scribing the entire ad image as positive or negative samples, causing all visual attributes in an underperforming image to be penalized equally, even though some may contribute posi-tively to CTR. Such erroneous signals are difficult to recover because promising visual ideas may appear only sparsely in the vast design space. Second, ad images in practice originate from diverse production pipelines, while a unified framework that generates prompts and renders images from product im-ages can hardly preserve or reproduce existing high-quality creatives. Finally, DPO is prone to overfitting when pref-erence supervision is limited (Lin et al. 2024; Chowdhury, Kini, and Natarajan 2024). Although expanding the pref-erence dataset can alleviate this issue, collecting additional samples from real online feedback requires substantial image arXiv:2610.13066v1 [cs.CV] 9 Oct 2026 generation, traffic, and experimentation time, making over-fitting reduction and data acquisition cost difficult to balance. To address the first two limitations, we propose Evolving Creatives with Clicks (ECC), which reformulates creative optimization as fine-grained editing of existing ad images rather than generation from scratch. As shown in Fig. 1(b), ECC selectively refines attributes that hurt CTR while pre-serving those that contribute positively. We define 51 visual attribute dimensions based on large-scale e-commerce data and expert knowledge, and provide candidate values for most attributes to reduce ambiguity and avoid overly dispersed descriptions. We then leverage MLLMs to extract attribute values from online ad images and identify 28 CTR-sensitive attributes through statistical analysis. These attributes trans-form the vast editing space into a structured set of actionable dimensions, enabling more targeted CTR optimization. Based on the selected attributes, ECC adopts a two-stage learning strategy. In the first stage, we construct image pairs with disparate CTRs and derive attribute-level editing schemes. Since high-CTR creatives may still contain sub-optimal attributes, the resulting suggestions are not always beneficial. We therefore randomly sample a subset of oper-ations for each training instance to train the prompt model to generate diverse executable editing suggestions. In the second stage, the instruction-tuned prompt model generates multiple suggestions for existing ad images, which are exe-cuted to produce new creatives. Since accurately predicting CTR from images remains challenging, with prior CTR re-ward models achieving only about 58% accuracy (Chen et al. 2025b), we deploy these creatives online rather than relying on a learned reward model. Preference pairs are then con-structed from real exposure and click feedback to distinguish effective suggestions and further refine the prompt model toward genuine CTR gains. To mitigate DPO overfitting without further expanding the preference dataset, we propose Consistent Preference Optimization (CPO) as the preference optimization strategy of ECC. Inspired by invariance learning in self-supervised learning (Chen et al. 2020), we identify that shuffling in-dependent operations does not affect the edited image, so the original and shuffled chosen editing suggestions should receive consistent preferences. CPO therefore introduces a lightweight consistency objective into DPO, encouraging the prompt model to focus on editing semantics rather than super-ficial ordering patterns and improving generalization without additional online data. Overall, our contributions are as fol-lows: 

• CTR-Aware Attribute Mining and Dataset Construc-tion Pipeline. We develop a unified pipeline for CTR-aware attribute mining and editing-suggestion data con-struction. By extracting 51 visual attributes from over 550,000 online ad images and identifying 28 CTR-sensitive dimensions through MLLM-based extraction and statistical analysis, we construct the first large-scale CTR-driven editing-suggestion datasets for instruction tuning and reinforcement learning. 

• ECC: The First CTR-Driven Advertising Image Edit-ing Framework. We propose ECC, the first framework to formulate ad image editing from a CTR-oriented per-spective. Guided by CTR-sensitive attributes, it learns executable editing suggestions through instruction fine-tuning and aligns them with online click preferences through reinforcement learning. 

• Consistent Preference Optimization Strategy. We pro-pose Consistent Preference Optimization (CPO), which augments DPO with consistency regularization between chosen responses and their semantics-preserving shuffled variants, addressing the overfitting problem in preference learning. Large-scale online experiments show that ECC achieves the highest CTR gain among strong MLLM-based baselines. 

Related Work 

E-commerce ad image Optimization 

Prior work on ad image optimization primarily focuses on visual quality and controllability. Methods such as Au-toCO (Chen et al. 2021), PAID (Chen et al. 2025a), retrieval-assisted advertising image generation (Ku et al. 2023), and instruction-guided editing models (Brooks, Holynski, and Efros 2023; Zhang et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024; Hui et al. 2024) improve visual appeal, composition, product presen-tation, or editing flexibility, but do not explicitly model user behavior. CTR-driven methods leverage online interaction signals for creative refinement and ranking (Mishra et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2021; Lin et al. 2022) and generation (Wei et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2025b). Rank-ing methods only select existing creatives, while generation-based methods often reuse fixed product foregrounds, limit-ing foreground diversity and creative variation. ECC instead edits existing creatives using real CTR feedback, enabling more flexible and diverse optimization. 

CTR Attribution and Visual Factor Analysis 

CTR prediction methods such as DeepFM (Guo et al. 2017) and xDeepFM (Lian et al. 2018) model feature interactions, while later studies incorporate visual information to improve prediction accuracy (Ge et al. 2018). However, these models often lack interpretability and provide limited insight into which visual factors drive CTR differences. Visual factor analysis commonly relies on statistical signif-icance and feature importance. ANOVA (Fisher 1970) evalu-ates whether different attribute values lead to significant CTR differences, while Random Forest regression (Breiman 2001) captures nonlinear relationships and estimates attribute im-portance. Building on these methods, we identify CTR-sensitive visual attributes and use them to guide editing-oriented data construction.

Methods 

Our workflow begins by extracting visual attributes from large-scale online ad images and identifying CTR-sensitive dimensions through statistical analysis, which are then used to construct instruction-tuning data. The instruction-tuned model generates executable editing suggestions that are ap-plied to source images for online deployment, and the re-sulting exposure and click feedback are used to construct preference data. Finally, CPO introduces consistency reg-ularization over semantics-preserving shuffles to explicitly learn order-invariant preferences and mitigate overfitting. 

CTR-Aware Visual Attribute Mining 

As shown in Fig. 2(a), we collect over 550K online ad im-ages and use Qwen3-VL-32B (Bai et al. 2025a) to extract 51 predefined visual attributes, including composition and copywriting, among others. Most attributes use constrained candidate values for consistent extraction, while Scene Type and Visual Style permit open-ended descriptions. We then analyze the extracted attribute values and their distributions, pre-excluding 14 attributes with unstable extraction, subjec-tive or redundant semantics, or weak attribution signals, and retain 37 candidates for statistical analysis. To identify visual attributes associated with click-through rate (CTR), we combine ANOVA significance with Ran-dom Forest feature importance, as illustrated in Fig. 2(b.1). To reduce product-level differences in popularity and user demand, CTR is first normalized within each product. For ANOVA (Fisher 1970), samples are grouped by their ex-tracted attribute values, and the between-group and within-group variations in normalized CTR are compared. The re-sulting F -statistic is converted into a p-value under the cor-responding F distribution, where a smaller value indicates a stronger association with CTR. Since ANOVA evaluates each attribute independently, we further train a Random For-est regressor (Breiman 2001) to predict normalized CTR and estimate attribute contributions under multi-attribute in-teractions. Categorical attributes are converted into one-hot features, and the contribution of each split is measured by the weighted reduction in mean squared error: 

∆MSE = MSE( D) − |D L||D| MSE( DL) − |D R||D| MSE( DR), (1) 

where DL and DR denote the subsets produced by the split. Importance scores of one-hot branches for each attribute are summed and normalized as attribute-level contributions. The final selection considers ANOVA significance, Ran-dom Forest contribution, distribution reliability, semantic re-dundancy, and practical editability. Most attributes follow the two quantitative signals, with a few boundary cases adjusted accordingly. Instruction-Guided Editing Learning 

As shown in Fig. 3(a), we perform instruction tuning us-ing the CTR-driven editing-suggestion dataset constructed based on the 28 selected attributes. Given a source ad image 

Ir and an instruction q, the prompt model learns to gener-ate executable editing suggestions for visual factors such as composition, copywriting, and background. Since the attribute-guided supervision provides only an initial editing signal and not every operation necessarily improves CTR, we introduce Stochastic Editing Operation Sampling. For each training instance, 1–3 operations are randomly sampled from the complete suggestion and con-catenated as the target response. This exposes the model to diverse operation combinations while leaving subsequent online preference optimization to identify modifications that genuinely improve CTR. Datasets 

To support the two training stages, we construct an instruction-tuning dataset for learning executable editing suggestions and an online preference dataset for reinforce-ment optimization. The former provides the model with an initial capability to identify and describe actionable modifi-cations, while the latter further aligns these suggestions with real user click preferences. As shown in Fig. 2(c), we first group sufficiently ex-posed creatives by product and construct image pairs with clear CTR differences. For each pair, the lower-CTR im-age is treated as the editing target, while the higher-CTR image serves as a reference that provides potentially benefi-cial visual cues. Guided by the 28 selected attributes, GPT-5.5 (OpenAI 2026) compares the two creatives and generates executable editing suggestions covering visual factors such as composition, copywriting, product presentation, and back-ground design. The reference image and extracted attribute values are used only during data construction and are not included in the model input. Each instruction-tuning sample therefore consists of the lower-CTR creative and an editing instruction as input, with the generated editing suggestion as the target response. After data filtering and quality control, this process produces 101,699 training samples. After instruction tuning, the model generates multiple can-didate editing suggestions for each source image. These sug-gestions are executed by the image-editing backend to pro-duce corresponding creatives, which are then reviewed and deployed online to collect real exposure and click feedback. For the same source image, suggestions that lead to higher and lower post-editing CTRs are selected as chosen and re-jected responses, respectively. Each preference sample con-tains the same source image and instruction as input, together with one chosen suggestion and one rejected suggestion, en-suring that the preference signal reflects differences in editing strategies rather than input conditions. This process produces 68,704 preference pairs. The structures of both datasets are shown in Fig. 2(d). 

Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

Implementation Details. We use Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as the base multimodal model for generating editing sugges-tions. All models are trained using the SWIFT (Zhao et al. 2024) framework on 4 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. For instruction-guided editing learning, we apply LoRA to all linear layers with rank 8 and alpha 32, and train the model for 3 epochs with a learning rate of 1×10 −4. For preference optimization, we initialize from the instruction-tuned adapter and optimize using the proposed CPO objective. This stage uses LoRA training for 4 epochs with a learning rate of 5 × 10 −5, and the regularization weight λ is set to 1. 

Online Evaluation Protocol. To evaluate the practical ef-fectiveness of the generated editing suggestions, we conduct a one-week online experiment on a large-scale e-commerce platform covering 44 product categories. For all compared methods, Gemini 3 Pro Image Preview (Google 2025) applies the generated suggestions to the original images, and the re-sulting creatives are deployed online. Before deployment, all edited images are reviewed by professional practitioners to ensure visual quality and foreground–background compati-bility. Their real exposure and click feedback are then used to determine the CTR effects of different editing suggestions. The experiment accumulates approximately 20 million im-pressions, providing reliable feedback for both preference construction and online evaluation. 

Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate each method using the aggregate click-through rate (CTR) gain between edited and original advertising creatives:

Ablation Study 

Ablation on Preference Optimization. Tab. 6 compares standard DPO, partial and full response-order shuffling aug-mentation, and the proposed Consistent Preference Opti-mization Strategy. DPO brings only limited CTR improve-ment, indicating that directly fitting offline preference pairs is insufficient for learning robust editing preferences. Although shuffled-response augmentation slightly improves CTR gain by exposing the model to different operation orders, it re-mains much weaker than CPO. CPO explicitly constrains se-mantically equivalent editing suggestions to receive consis-tent preferences, helping the model focus on editing seman-tics rather than superficial ordering patterns. This provides a more direct way to encode task-specific invariance than simply expanding the training data with shuffled variants. As a result, ECC achieves the highest CTR gain of 6.86%, validating the proposed preference optimization design.

Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose ECC, a CTR-driven framework that improves existing ad images through fine-grained edit-ing rather than generation from scratch. Using large-scale online data and statistical analysis, ECC identifies 28 CTR-sensitive attributes from 51 predefined dimensions and uses them to construct actionable editing supervision for targeted creative refinement. It first learns to generate executable edit-ing suggestions through instruction tuning and is then further refined through preference optimization based on real click feedback. We further propose Consistent Preference Opti-mization (CPO) as ECC’s preference optimization strategy, augmenting DPO with a consistency regularizer to mitigate overfitting to spurious ordering patterns and improve pref-erence generalization. Large-scale online experiments show that ECC outperforms strong MLLM-based baselines and achieves the highest aggregate CTR gain across diverse prod-uct categories and advertising scenarios, demonstrating the effectiveness and practical value of fine-grained attribute editing for creative optimization.

Analysis of Variance. Random Forest Regression. We further employ a random forest regressor to evaluate the contributions of visual attributes under a multivariate setting. Categorical attribute values are first converted into binary indicator features, such that each possible attribute value is represented by an individual feature branch. A random forest is composed of multiple regression trees. Each tree recursively partitions the feature space according to visual attribute values, with the objective of grouping images that have similar normalized CTR values. At each internal node, the model selects a feature and a splitting rule that produce the largest reduction in regression error. In this way, regression trees can model nonlinear relationships and conditional dependencies between visual attributes and CTR without requiring a predefined functional form. Different regression trees are constructed from randomized samples and feature subsets. Their predictions are aggregated to obtain the final CTR prediction, reducing the instability and overfitting of a single decision tree. The importance of each feature branch is calculated according to the cumulative reduction in regression impurity produced by splits using that branch across all trees.
