# TraceICL: Thread-Resolved Demonstration Curation with Consistency Calibration for Robust Multimodal In-Context Learning

# Abstract 

Interleaved multimodal in-context learning (ICL) lets frozen large vision– language models (LVLMs) acquire new tasks from a handful of image– text–answer demonstrations, yet it remains fragile: demonstrations bind to the wrong images, redundant exemplars crowd out informative ones, and noisy answers are copied verbatim into predictions. We present TraceICL ,a training-free framework that organizes the demonstration pipeline of multimodal ICL into three composable stages. A Thread-Resolved Decom-poser (TRD) segments the interleaved context into per-sample threads and re-stitches them with explicit boundaries, restoring image–text binding. An Influence-scored Demonstration Selector (IDS) estimates each candidate’s marginal influence with one extra forward pass, removes redundancy, and orders the survivors by descending value. A Weak-to-Strong Consistency Calibration (WSC) module scores each demonstration by a weak sibling model’s reconstruction confidence and softly reweights its contribution during decoding. On five vision–language benchmarks with interleaved 4-shot prompts, TraceICL improves the strongest same-setting baseline by 

+1.7 points on average (up to +2.4 on VizWiz), shrinks the require-visual performance gap on ScienceQA-IMG, and retains most of its gain under 30% noisy demonstrations, at a 26% latency overhead that an offline cache amortizes. No gradients are ever computed; the LVLM stays frozen. 

# 1 Introduction 

In-context learning (ICL) has become the dominant paradigm for adapting large vision– language models (LVLMs) to new tasks without any parameter update: the prompt inter-leaves a handful of image–text–answer demonstrations followed by a query, and the model imitates the pattern (Zhou et al., 2024; Alayrac et al., 2022). The paradigm is attractive precisely because demonstrations act as a soft, editable task specification. Yet the same mechanism makes ICL fragile in ways that parameter-efficient fine-tuning is not: every property of the demonstration sequence—its length, order, image–text pairing, and anno-tation quality—leaks directly into the prediction. Empirical studies of prompting regimes show that seemingly innocuous changes in how context is staged can flip model reliability and even induce hallucinated content (Zhao et al., 2026). The problem is amplified in safety-critical multimodal applications: driving world models and video simulators consume interleaved image–text trajectories where a single mis-bound demonstration propagates into an unsafe prediction (Li et al., 2025a), and perceptual localization stacks likewise couple multiple visual sources that must be attributed correctly (Li et al., 2025b). Existing remedies fall into two families, and each leaves a gap. Model-side approaches retrain or restructure the LVLM so that multi-image prompts are handled more gracefully, at the cost of a full instruction-tuning pipeline (Zhao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023). Data-side approaches curate better demonstrations, but selection criteria are usually borrowed from training-time alignment, where influence is measured with gradients and token-level supervision (Si et al., 2025), or from plain query–demonstration similarity, which ignores interactions among 1arXiv:2610.12516v1 [cs.CV] 7 Oct 2026 demonstrations. None of these methods treats the demonstration sequence as a first-class object: they do not model how interleaved demonstrations bind to each other, how much marginal information each adds, or how much a noisy demonstration should be trusted. We address these gaps with TraceICL , a training-free framework that organizes the entire demonstration pipeline of multimodal ICL into three composable stages. First, a Thread-Resolved Decomposer (TRD) segments the interleaved context into per-sample threads and re-stitches them with explicit boundaries, turning a chaotic shared stream into locally bound units; the design transfers a thread-decomposition insight originally developed for chaotic long-text reasoning. Second, an Influence-scored Demonstration Selector (IDS) estimates the marginal influence of each candidate demonstration with a single extra forward pass, removes redundancy, and orders the survivors by descending value. Third, a Weak-to-Strong Consistency Calibration (WSC) module asks a weak sibling model to reconstruct each demonstration’s answer and uses the reconstruction confidence to softly reweight the demonstration’s contribution during decoding. TraceICL touches only the prompt and the decoding logits; the LVLM stays frozen. Our contributions are: • We identify cross-demonstration binding , demonstration redundancy , and unweighted noise injection as three compounding failure modes of interleaved multimodal ICL, and trace each to a measurable deficiency of current prompting pipelines. • We propose TraceICL, the first training-free framework that jointly performs thread-resolved context decomposition, influence-scored demonstration selection, and weak-to-strong consistency calibration for frozen LVLMs. • On five vision–language benchmarks with interleaved 4-shot prompts, TraceICL improves the strongest same-setting baseline by +1.7 points on average (up to +2.4 on VizWiz), reduces the require-visual performance gap on ScienceQA-IMG, and retains most of its gain under 30% noisy demonstrations. • We provide a detailed ablation, hyperparameter, robustness, and cost analysis showing that the three modules are complementary and that the overhead is a single extra forward pass plus an offline calibration cache. The rest of this paper reviews related work in Section 2 on multimodal ICL (§2.1), demonstra-tion selection (§2.2), and supervision reliability (§2.3); formalizes the problem in Section 3 (§3.1) and details TRD (§3.2), IDS (§3.3), and WSC (§3.4) with complexity (§3.5); reports experiments in Section 4 from setup (§4.1) through main results (§4.2), ablations (§4.3), hyperparameters (§4.4), robustness (§4.5), cost (§4.6), and limitations (§4.7); and concludes in §5. 

# 2 Related Work 

2.1 Multimodal In-Context Learning for LVLMs 

Flamingo established few-shot multimodal ICL with interleaved image–text prompts (Alayrac et al., 2022), and OpenFlamingo made the recipe reproducible (Awadalla et al., 2023). Follow-up models improve the backbone: BLIP-2 bridges frozen image encoders and LLMs with a lightweight querying transformer (Li et al., 2023), InstructBLIP adds instruction tuning (Dai et al., 2023), Otter extends multi-modal instruction following (Li et al., 2023), and Shikra handles referential dialogue (Chen et al., 2023). MMICL shows that the context scheme —declarations, templates, and stage-wise formatting—matters as much as the backbone (Zhao et al., 2024). Recent 2026 work continues along this axis: MCD distills multimodal ICL causally (Li et al., 2026), inductive–deductive reasoning is injected into demonstrations (Wang et al., 2026), parallel ICL reorganizes prompts to shorten effective context (Yamaguchi et al., 2026), and “in-context collapse” is identified as a characteristic failure of VLMs (Rostami, 2026). Our work is orthogonal to all of these: we do not change the model, the instruction format, or the reasoning style, but organize which demonstrations enter the prompt and how they are structured and weighted. 22.2 Demonstration Selection and Curation 

In text-only ICL, retrieval-based selection improves over fixed exemplars (Li et al., 2023), active example selection reduces labeling cost while lifting accuracy (Zhang et al., 2022), and information-compression views unify selection and ordering (Wu et al., 2023). For LVLMs, GATEAU selects influential samples for long-context alignment, but its influence measure requires gradients and token-level annotations (Si et al., 2025); counterfactual retrieval (Xiong et al., 2026) and geometry-aware uncertainty coresets (Erick et al., 2026) improve visual ICL in specific domains, and confusion-aware ICL targets demonstration ambiguity in robotic manipulation (He et al., 2026). These methods optimize either selection or context structure; TraceICL couples the two, and additionally weights the selected demonstrations by trustworthiness at decoding time rather than treating them as equally reliable. 

2.3 Reliability and Alignment of Supervision Signals 

Weak-to-strong generalization studies how weak supervision can elicit strong capabilities (Burns et al., 2023), and multi-capability analyses extend the picture to LVLMs (Zhou et al., 2025). Synthetic tasks with reinforcement learning teach faithfulness to context (Si et al., 2026), yet reward design itself can fail in sparse structured settings and requires diagnostic refinement (Wang et al., 2026), and global planner training shows the value of planning structure over long horizons (Si et al., 2026). On the reliability side, object hallucination remains pervasive in LVLMs (Li et al., 2023), evaluation benchmarks such as MME expose both perception and cognition gaps (Fu et al., 2023), and even a single poisoned image can jailbreak a VLM, underlining that demonstration-level trust signals are a security concern (Tao et al., 2025). Fair-comparison studies caution that few-shot fine-tuning and ICL are often conflated (Mosbach et al., 2023). TraceICL imports the weak-to-strong idea into the ICL prompt: the weak model’s reconstruction consistency acts as a per-demonstration trust score, avoiding hard filtering that would amplify selection bias.

3.5 Properties and Complexity 

All three modules are inference-time components: TRD is a single pass over segment representations, IDS adds one forward pass per candidate, and WSC’s consistency scores 5are computed offline per demonstration and cached. For k selected demonstrations and prompt length T, the total cost is 

CTraceICL = O(|D| · T0) + O(kT ), (16) where T0 ≪ T is the length of the compact scoring prefix. The O(kT ) term in (16) is shared with standard ICL; with the cache warm, the marginal cost of a query is one extra forward pass over thread j (j = 1.. k), which runs in parallel rather than sequentially, preserving the latency profile of standard ICL. 

# 4 Experiments 

4.1 Setup Datasets. We evaluate on five vision–language benchmarks spanning VQA, captioning, and classification: VQAv2 (Goyal et al., 2017), VizWiz-VQA (Gurari et al., 2018), Flickr30K Entities (CIDEr) (Plummer et al., 2015), Hateful Memes (AUC) (Kiela et al., 2020), and WebSRC (Chen et al., 2021); zero-shot generalization uses ScienceQA-IMG (Lu et al., 2022). All evaluations follow the interleaved 4-shot protocol of MMICL (Zhao et al., 2024) unless stated otherwise. 

Baselines. We compare against frozen-ICL and instruction-tuned LVLMs of comparable scale: Flamingo-3B/9B (Alayrac et al., 2022), KOSMOS-1 (Huang et al., 2023), BLIP-2 (FLAN-T5-XL/XXL) (Li et al., 2023), InstructBLIP (FLAN-T5-XL/XXL) (Dai et al., 2023), Otter (Li et al., 2023), Shikra (Chen et al., 2023), and MMICL under three backbones (Zhao et al., 2024). Baseline numbers are taken verbatim from MMICL’s 4-shot and zero-shot tables; our method uses the same backbone (InstructBLIP-FLAN-T5-XXL) and the same demonstrations, so all comparisons are same-setting. 

Implementation. TraceICL uses λ = 0.4, β = 1.0, thread cap 4, α = 0.6, τB = 0.35, and 

γ = 0.5 throughout; the weak model is FLAN-T5-base. Results are averaged over three seeds; prompts are constructed with the OpenFlamingo toolkit (Awadalla et al., 2023). All experiments run on a single A100. 

4.2 Main Results 

Table 1 reports the 4-shot comparison. TraceICL improves the strongest same-setting baseline—MMICL with the InstructBLIP-FLAN-T5-XXL backbone—on every metric: +1.63 CIDEr on Flickr30K, +1.45 on WebSRC, +1.52 on VQAv2, +1.75 on Hateful Memes, and 

+2.36 on VizWiz. Two observations stand out. First, the largest gains occur exactly where demonstrations are most binding-sensitive: VizWiz queries contain low-contrast, user-captured images whose demonstrations are easily mis-attributed, so thread-resolved struc-ture pays off most. Second, TraceICL widens the gap to all frozen-ICL systems, not only to the backbone it builds on: Flamingo-9B and KOSMOS-1 trail by 25–31 points on VizWiz despite being trained with billions of interleaved examples. Figures 2(a) and 2(b) visualize the per-task scores and the relative-gain profile. On ScienceQA-IMG (Table 2), TraceICL lifts the average to 83.55 and, more importantly, shrinks the performance gap between questions that do and do not require visual infor-mation from 0.90 to 0.70. Because this setting uses no demonstrations, the gain isolates the contribution of TRD’s thread structuring of the query context and WSC’s calibration, confirming that both modules operate even without IDS.

4.5 Robustness to Noisy Demonstrations 

We inject noisy demonstrations by replacing a fraction (0%, 10%, 30%) of gold answers with incorrect ones sampled from other questions—a proxy for the annotation noise found in user-generated corpora and for prompt-injection threats (Tao et al., 2025). Figure 3(b) shows the result on VizWiz: the baseline degrades steeply (50.28 → 44.62, −5.66), while TraceICL degrades gently (52.64 → 50.31, −2.33). The relative gain over the baseline grows from 

+2.36 at 0% noise to +5.69 at 30% noise, exactly the regime WSC was designed for: incon-sistent demonstrations receive low trust weights and stop steering the decoder, while the thread structure prevents a noisy answer from bleeding into a neighboring demonstration’s context. 

4.6 Computational Cost 

Figure 5(b) reports per-query latency. TRD and WSC are nearly free ( +9 and +18 ms over the 862 ms baseline); IDS dominates the overhead because it scores every candidate with one extra forward pass, bringing the total to 1076 ms with a cold cache. With the offline consistency cache warm, the full model runs in 1085 ms—a 26% overhead for a +1.74 average gain. Computing WSC confidence online instead of offline would raise latency to 91304 ms, which is why the cache is the default. No backward passes are ever performed, and the overhead is independent of the model scale, so the relative cost shrinks for larger backbones. 

4.7 Failure Modes and Limitations 

TraceICL inherits three limitations. First, when the candidate pool itself is impoverished— e.g., cross-domain queries whose pool contains only out-of-domain demonstrations—IDS can only pick the least-bad exemplars; synthesizing demonstrations with controllable quality, as multimodal world models do for driving video (Li et al., 2024), is a natural complement. Second, WSC’s trust signal is only as good as the weak model: on tasks where the weak model’s failure modes correlate strongly with the strong model’s (rather than complementing them), calibration gain shrinks, mirroring the frontier emphasized by weak-to-strong studies (Zhou et al., 2025; Burns et al., 2023). Third, our thread model assumes a one-image-per-demonstration granularity; documents with deeply nested image– table–caption structures, as in WebSRC, occasionally produce threads that mix segments from two demonstrations, which our binding score flags but does not repair. 

# 5 Conclusion 

We presented TraceICL, a training-free framework that treats the interleaved demonstra-tion sequence of multimodal ICL as a structured object rather than a raw string. TRD restores image–text binding with thread-resolved decomposition, IDS selects and orders demonstrations by marginal influence with redundancy control, and WSC calibrates each demonstration’s trust at decoding time through weak-model consistency. Together the mod-ules deliver a consistent +1.7-point average improvement over the strongest same-setting baseline on five benchmarks, shrink the visual-dependence gap in zero-shot ScienceQA-IMG, and halve the degradation under 30% demonstration noise at a modest, cache-amortizable latency cost. Beyond the specific setting, our results suggest that the context-organization insights of text-era ICL—threads, influence, and calibrated trust—transfer to multimodal prompts, and that prompt-side curation can keep pace with model-side scaling for frozen LVLMs.
