## Answer It or Not? Towards Prompt-Adapted Adversarial Images on Vision Language Models 

Shunyuan Bai Yuchen Ren Le Yang Chenhao Lin Chao Shen Zhengyu Zhao 

Xi’an Jiaotong University, Xi’an, 710049, China 

Abstract 

With the advancement in language model-ing, research on adversarial images has re-cently shifted from image classifiers to vision-language models (VLMs). Despite the new, text-prompt-driven nature of VLMs, we find that existing VLM adversaries have largely overlooked the dynamics of text prompts. In a typical use case, adversarial images can pro-tect visual privacy by rejecting VLMs from answering questions about the image. How-ever, existing protections are prompt-agnostic, i.e., indiscriminately rejecting any question, in-cluding those for potential legitimate use of the image. To address this limitation, we pro-pose Prompt-Adapted (ProAda) adversarial im-ages, which are optimized to balance the re-jection of harmful prompts and the correct response of legitimate prompts. In particu-lar, to achieve a more accurate adversarial di-rection, the text prompt is also iteratively up-dated with a simple classifier judging whether the harmfulness of the prompt remains. Ex-tensive experiments on visual-privacy-related datasets validate that ProAda can reject over 90% of harmful prompts while allowing over 90% of legitimate ones, demonstrating supe-rior overall performance compared to existing prompt-agnostic VLM adversaries. In addition, our ProAda achieves competitive overall cross-model transferability and improved robustness against countermeasures.

Subsequent advances, including BLIP (Li et al., 2022), Open-Flamingo (Awadalla et al., 2023), and BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023), have further improved generative ca-pabilities, reduced training costs, and allowed flexi-ble integration with various encoders and language models. Recent studies have also explored universal adversarial perturbations on VLMs, showing the cross-prompt transferability of VLM adversarial images, i.e., a single image perturbation can disrupt the VLM’s responses to multiple prompts. (Luo et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Despite extensive studies, we have identified a blind spot in existing research: the dynamics of text prompts have been largely overlooked. Specifically, existing VLM adversaries are prompt-agnostic, i.e., aiming to disrupt responses to either a sin-gle prompt (in the white-box setting) or all kinds of prompts (in the cross-prompt transfer setting). However, in many real-world applications, e.g., vi-sual privacy protection (Joon Oh et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2019; Xue et al., 2020; Cherepanova et al., 2021; Rajabi et al., 2021), it is necessary to adapt adversarial effects to specific properties of prompts. As illustrated in Figure 1, to balance privacy pro-tection and the legitimate (prompt-driven) use of the image, adversarial perturbations should only reject harmful prompts, such as “How old is this woman?" but allow general prompts, such as “Is there any building?" .In this paper, we address the above blind spot by proposing Prompt-Adapted (ProAda) adversar-ial images on VLMs to achieve a balance between privacy protection and the general image utility. Specifically, the adversarial perturbation is jointly optimized to move away from the correct responses to a set of harmful prompts but stay close to the correct responses to a set of general prompts. In particular, to achieve a more accurate adversarial di-rection, the embedding of the harmful text prompt is also iteratively updated, where a simple classi-fier is introduced to ensure that the updated prompt remains harmful. In sum, we make the following main contribu-tions: • We propose the new concept of Prompt-Adapted (ProAda) adversarial images against VLMs, which can adapt adversarial effects to specific text prompts. In a case study on vi-sual privacy protection, ProAda is optimized to reject harmful prompts while accepting gen-eral ones. In contrast, existing protections are prompt-agnostic, inevitably sacrificing the general utility of images. • We further propose a simple yet effective method to ensure a more accurate adversar-ial direction, by also iteratively updating the embedding of the harmful text prompt with a binary classifier used to determine whether the updated prompt remains harmful. • We demonstrate that ProAda can reject over 90% of mostly unseen harmful prompts while maintaining the correct answers to over 90% of mostly unseen general ones on visual-privacy-related images, achieving superior overall performance compared to existing prompt-agnostic VLM adversaries. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Vision-Language Models (VLMs) 

Many traditional artificial intelligence models rely solely on unimodal input and are therefore classi-fied as single-modality models. In contrast, mul-timodal models, such as vision-language models (VLMs), can integrate inputs from diverse modali-ties. The widespread adoption of the Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) has provided a powerful framework for constructing multimodal models across various tasks. Early VLMs, like the Show and Tell model (Vinyals et al., 2015; Lu et al., 2019; Tan and Bansal, 2019) has laid the conceptual foun-dation for the development of more complex VLMs. CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) model, which demon-strated outstanding performance in image classifi-cation, image retrieval, and zero-shot classification tasks, leverages contrastive learning on large-scale image–text pairs during training. BLIP model (Li et al., 2022) represents a more generalized multi-modal pre-training framework. It supports a wider range of tasks, such as image captioning and visual question answering. Building upon BLIP, BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023) model enables stronger multi-modal reasoning capabilities while significantly reducing training costs. Beyond these, Instruct-BLIP (Dai et al., 2023), Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022), LLaVA (Liu et al., 2023), and miniGPT4 (Zhu et al., 2024) have also demonstrated strong performance in high-quality vision-language tasks. 

2.2 Adversarial Examples on VLMs 

Adversarial example generation involves adding deliberately crafted, often imperceptible perturba-tions to input data, causing AI models to produce targeted erroneous outputs (Szegedy et al., 2013; Goodfellow et al., 2014; Madry et al., 2018). In 2the context of VLMs, adversarial images are also widely studied (Zhao et al., 2023; Qi et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Specifically, visual perturbation trained on a small number of harmful prompts can enable the model to bypass the original safety limitations and provide accurate answers to numerous dangerous questions (Qi et al., 2024). Subsequent studies went beyond simply deceiving VLMs with fixed input variables and began exploring the transferability of adversar-ials across multiple models or text prompts. For model-level transferability, it involves generating adversarial samples on open-source VLMs and test-ing their effectiveness on other downstream VLMs (such as MinigPT-4 and LLaVA) in a black-box test-ing scenario (Zhao et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024). For prompt-level transferability, it aims to achieve a universal adversarial perturbation to fool VLMs across various text prompts (Luo et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). 

2.3 Visual Privacy Protection 

Privacy leakage caused by image sharing on so-cial media platforms has become an increasingly severe issue (Krishnamurthy and Wills, 2008). Even when individuals consciously check their images for explicit privacy-sensitive content be-fore uploading, implicit privacy information, such as geo locations (Liu et al., 2019) and private at-tributes (Joon Oh et al., 2017) may still be in-ferred (Such et al., 2017; DeHart et al., 2020). To protect visual privacy, extensive methods have been studied. Traditional work (Korshunov et al., 2012) has applied common image processing operations to obfuscate image content. Since advanced ma-chine learning models are often used to help infer private information in images (Tonge and Caragea, 2020; Schultenkämper and Bäumer, 2024), many studies have explored how to protect privacy by misleading machine learning models. In particular, adversarial perturbations, as a typical way to cause prediction errors, have been used for protection in various tasks, such as image classification (Joon Oh et al., 2017; Cherepanova et al., 2021; Rajabi et al., 2021), image retrieval (Liu et al., 2019), object detection (Xue et al., 2020), and vision-language understanding (Zhao et al., 2025). In this paper, we propose a new concept of Prompt-Adapted (ProAda) adversarial images against VLMs, which can adapt adversarial effects to specific properties of the text prompts, in con-trast to the previous prompt-agnostic adversarial images. With ProAda, we can achieve visual pri-vacy protection while maintaining the general util-ity of images.

Specifically, the response to general prompts is minimized towards the correct answer using Lg , and the response to harmful prompts is minimized towards a pre-defined target text (e.g., “unknown”) using Lh. In particular, optimization on harmful prompts also involves updating the text perturbation δt to achieve a more accurate adversarial direction, as commonly done in related work (Luo et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2025). Here, δt is updated only when the adversarial text is judged to remain harmful. 

3.2 Harmfulness Judgment on Text Perturbations 

Since the text perturbation δt on harmful prompts is updated to provide intermediate guidance for the updating of visual perturbation, its harmfulness should not be changed after being perturbed. To this end, we train an additional binary SVM classi-fier to judge whether the intermediate adversarial text xht + δt retains harmfulness. Specifically, a random subset of harmful and general prompts is selected for training the classifier. During the op-timization, if the classifier judges the intermediate adversarial text as harmful, its update will be re-tained. If not, the current adversarial text would be discarded, and the text obtained in the previous iteration would be used. The ablation results confirm the effectiveness and stability of such a simple SVM classifier: it can achieve near 100% accuracy, and the protec-tion performance gets saturated after 90%. Here, employing an LLM-based classifier is unnecessary and technically infeasible because the text update operates on continuous embedding vectors, not dis-crete text tokens. 

3.3 Algorithm Details 

The detailed algorithm of our ProAda is provided in Algorithm 1. Following the common practice, we adopt PGD (Madry et al., 2018) for iterative optimization, and the perturbation is restricted to be within ϵ (∥δv∥p<ϵ ). The training sets of harm-ful prompts {xht } and of general prompts {xgt }

for optimizing the adversary are only part of the evaluation set. This can be used to validate the generalizability of our ProAda to unseen prompt distributions. Since the text perturbation only acts as a guidance, δt is updated every Rv:t times that

4.1 Experimental Setup Datasets and models. The image dataset is derived from (Orekondy et al., 2018), which ex-tends the visual-privacy-related VISPR dataset (Orekondy et al., 2017) and contains 8.5k images covering 24 categories of private attributes, related to faces, disabilities, and tattoos, as well as public attributes. Each image is associated with one or more private categories. The dataset also provides region-level annotations of 47.6k instances for ev-ery private category, but out of the scope of our work. We randomly select 200 images 1 from this dataset, covering the full range of 24 categories of private attributes. 

> 1Previous studies typically adopt 50 images (Luo et al., 2024; Mittal et al., 2025).

The text dataset contains both harmful and gen-eral prompts. The harmful prompts are constructed by us and assisted by large models. Specifically, we instructed GPT-4o with “These are images related to the <> privacy category. Based on the image con-tent and existing privacy policies, please construct question texts targeting this category.” We have dis-carded those with privacy-unrelated or excessively short content and those with explicit names, to en-sure that all of them indeed target private attributes: names, ages, locations, etc. For example, prompts in the “driver’s license” category may ask about names or place names appearing on the license. Finally, according to the 24 privacy categories in the image dataset, there are 10 prompts for each category. The general prompts are selected from 

VQA general prompts in (Luo et al., 2024). We ensure that the selected general prompts do not involve any privacy-related information and share similar characteristics with the above harmful prompts, in terms of question styles, text length, and vocabulary. This results in a total of 190 general prompts. A complete list of harmful prompts in the 24 privacy categories and the 190 selected general prompts is provided in Appendix A.1. To validate the generalizability of ProAda to unseen prompt distributions, only 20% of the harmful prompts and 5% of the general prompts are used for optimizing the adversary, and all prompts are used for evaluation. For the VLM models , we follow existing work (Luo et al., 2024; Mittal et al., 2025) to mainly adopt the BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023) as the vision en-coder and OPT-2.7b (Zhang et al., 2022) as the large language model. In particular, for the trans-fer experiments, we adopt InstructBLIP (Dai et al., 2023) with Vicuna-7b (Zheng et al., 2023) as the target black-box VLM. 

Parameter Settings. We set the step size of visual perturbation αv to 1/255 and that of text pertur-bation αt to 5 × 10 −3. The perturbation size ϵ is 16/255, and the number of iterations N is 1,650. If not mentioned specifically, the targeted optimiza-tion (Equation 2) is used. The target text T is simply set to be “Unknown”. The ratio of visual to text perturbation update Rv:t is 5, and the ratio of harmful loss to general loss Rh:g is 9. Finally, we conduct ablation studies on all these parameters.

5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose ProAda to achieve prompt-adapted adversarial images on VLMs. Un-like existing prompt-agnostic adversaries, ProAda employs distinct optimization strategies for harm-ful and general prompts, together with SVM-based harmfulness judgment to guide text perturbation updates. Using image privacy protection as a case study, we generate adversarial samples that can pre-serve general prompt responses while securing im-age privacy. Challenging scenarios, such as cross-model transfer and diverse countermeasures, are also considered. 8Limitations 

Scope. Since we are the first to explore prompt-adapted adversarial images, we have limited the adaptation to binary options (harmful or general) and to a specific vision-language task of visual privacy protection. Future work can investigate other forms of prompt adaptation and a broader set of multi-modal tasks. 

Performance. Despite integrating diverse ad-vanced techniques, the cross-model transferability of prompt-adapted adversarial images remains lim-ited, especially in targeted settings (see Table 2). Future work can explore the internal structures of VLMs, such as attention features in visual encoders, for further improvement.

A.3 Discussion A.3.1 Untargeted Optimization 

We evaluate ProAda using untargeted optimization (Equation 1). Figure A.2 shows that the untargeted adversary converges faster than the targeted one (Figure 3), saturating within approximately 20–30 iterations since it merely requires the adversarial response to diverge from the original response. 

A.3.2 Additional Models 

To evaluate the cross-architecture generalization of ProAda, we apply it to LLaVA-1.5 (CLIP ViT-L/14 with Vicuna) under identical experimental settings. As shown in Table A.1, ProAda main-tains high success rates comparable to our primary evaluations. This sustained efficacy confirms that our prompt-adapted strategy exploits universal text-vision alignment vulnerabilities rather than overfit-ting to specific backbones, demonstrating its robust applicability across diverse VLMs.
