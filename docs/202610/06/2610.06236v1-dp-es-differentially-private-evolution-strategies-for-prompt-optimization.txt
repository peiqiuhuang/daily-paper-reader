Abstract 

Token-level differentially private (DP) prompt optimization methods such as DP-OPT can be-come unstable under tight privacy budgets: on GSM8K, DP-OPT obtains 49 .5±28 .5% across 30 runs, and a logged search trajectory re-veals prompt-template drift and noise-sensitive irreversible choices. We diagnose these as structural consequences of greedy token-by-token construction over privately aggregated counts. We then propose DP-ES (Differen-tially Private Evolution Strategies), a struc-turally cleaner alternative that maintains a population of full prompts, mutates them via LLM calls that never access the private dataset, and spends privacy only on sampled-Gaussian evaluation; deterministic or Gumbel-smoothed selection is post-processing. Under a con-servative (ε≤1.0, δ =10 −5) guarantee, DP-ES achieves 88.1% on GSM8K (+38.6 pp over DP-OPT, ∼9× lower std), 99.7% on MedQA ,

73.5% on BANKING77 , and 86.8% on Al-paca . It is also 2.5 × faster in wall-clock time and uses 3.3 × fewer logged private-data call groups than DP-OPT. Selection and population ablations, implementation-level noise checks, and a 200-profile exact-match memorization stress test complement the formal guarantee. 

Scope. Our experiments establish optimization robustness under DP noise, especially where prompt structure is critical; end-to-end vali-dation on genuinely sensitive, non-saturated deployment data remains future work. Code: github.com/StephCpa/dp-es. 

1 Introduction 

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demon-strated remarkable capabilities through in-context learning (Brown et al., 2020; Ouyang et al., 2022), yet their performance heavily relies on carefully crafted prompts (Liu et al., 2023). Automatic prompt optimization (Yuksekgonul et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024b) can bridge this gap, but when the optimization data are sensitive (e.g., patient records, financial logs), the optimizer can memo-rize and expose private information, as evidenced by recent membership inference and extraction attacks (Wang et al., 2023; Shanmugarasa et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2023). For instance, a naive op-timizer might generate a prompt like “Remember how patient #12847 had atrial fibrillation...” , di-rectly violating privacy. Any optimizer intended for such settings must therefore satisfy formal Dif-ferential Privacy (DP) guarantees (Dwork et al., 2006)—and, crucially, the optimization mechanism itself must remain robust under the noise that DP introduces, which is the question we focus on in this work. 

The Challenge: Task Complexity and Privacy Budgets. Applying DP to prompt optimization is non-trivial due to the discrete nature of text. Re-cent work like DP-OPT (Hong et al., 2024) uses histogram-based token generation with the expo-nential mechanism. Other approaches apply DP to in-context learning via private few-shot genera-tion or nearest neighbor retrieval (Tang et al., 2024; Koskela et al., 2025), but they rely on additional retrieval or embedding machinery. While DP-OPT is effective on simple tasks, it is unstable on our complex-reasoning setting: across 30 GSM8K runs it obtains 49 .5±28 .5% , and trajectory analysis re-veals prompt-template drift and noise-sensitive ir-reversible decisions (Appendix C.2). This fragility is consistent with token-level myopia and non-backtracking search under tight privacy constraints. 

Private Evolution Perspective. Private Evolu-tion (PE) is a training-free DP paradigm for data synthesis via APIs (Lin et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025). Its core advantage is structural: only low-sensitivity evaluation touches private data, while exploration/generation and se-lection over privatized scores are post-processing. This yields a decoupling between exploration and privacy expenditure—increasing the number or strength of mutations improves search without con-arXiv:2610.06236v1 [cs.CR] 5 Oct 2026 suming additional privacy budget. DP-ES is a prompt-optimization instantiation of this PE princi-ple. 

Our Contribution: DP-ES. We frame this work as a diagnostic and methodological contribution for DP prompt optimization: we systematically charac-terize the instability mechanisms of token-level DP construction under tight privacy budgets and pro-pose Differentially Private Evolution Strategies (DP-ES) , a structurally cleaner alternative. Unlike methods that build prompts token by token from privately aggregated counts, DP-ES maintains a population of full prompts that evolve over time. The key design choice is to decouple exploration from privacy consumption: using LLMs to gen-erate semantic mutations consumes zero privacy budget (by the post-processing property), allowing the scarce privacy budget to be allocated strictly to sampled-Gaussian evaluation. Our contributions are: 1. Instability diagnosis: A 30-run analysis of DP-OPT on GSM8K, complemented by a concrete logged trajectory that exposes prompt-template drift and noise-sensitive irreversible selection in token-level construction (Section 5). 2. Method: A structurally cleaner evolution-based DP prompt optimizer with privacy-free mutations, sampled-Gaussian scoring under RDP accounting, and deterministic or Gumbel-smoothed post-processing selection. 3. Empirical robustness: ∼9× lower standard de-viation than DP-OPT on GSM8K, plus strong or competitive results on MedQA, BANK-ING77, and Alpaca under a conservative 

(ε≤1, δ =10 −5) guarantee. 4. Efficiency: 2.5 × faster wall-clock runtime and 3.3 × fewer logged private-data call groups than DP-OPT on GSM8K, with an explicit generation-vs-evaluation cost decomposition. 5. Privacy evidence: A reproducible RDP ac-counting calculation and noise-distribution checks, plus a 200-profile synthetic exact-match stress test (0 PII strings found for DP-ES vs. 40 for an adversarial non-DP control). 

Scope of Empirical Evidence. Our experiments establish DP-ES as a structurally more robust DP prompt optimizer than token-level alternatives, es-pecially on tasks where prompt structure is critical. They do not by themselves validate deployment on genuinely sensitive, non-saturated datasets. The formal (ε, δ )-DP guarantee bounds record-level leakage independently of the benchmark, while the implementation checks and exact-match stress test provide complementary diagnostics. We make these scope boundaries explicit in the Limitations section. 

2 Related Work 

Prompt Optimization. Gradient-free methods such as APE (Zhou et al., 2023) and OPRO (Yang et al., 2024b) search over discrete prompt can-didates using LLM-generated variations, while gradient-based approaches like TextGrad (Yuksek-gonul et al., 2024) use LLM feedback as differ-entiable signals. Both families optimize prompts effectively but assume unrestricted access to train-ing data with no privacy guarantees (Edemacu and Wu, 2025), making them unsuitable for sensitive domains such as healthcare, finance, and education where data protection is legally mandated. 

Differential Privacy in Machine Learning. DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and PATE (Papernot et al., 2018) provide formal (ε, δ )-DP guarantees for continuous model parameters during training. DP-LoRA (Liu et al., 2024b; Frery et al., 2025) extends this to parameter-efficient fine-tuning of LLMs, achieving strong performance with reduced memory overhead. However, all these methods require access to model weights, precluding their use with black-box API-only LLMs—the dominant deployment mode for state-of-the-art models such as GPT-4 and DeepSeek. 

DP Prompt Optimization. For discrete prompts over black-box LLMs, DP-OPT (Hong et al., 2024) uses histogram-based token generation with the exponential mechanism, constructing prompts to-ken by token from privately aggregated counts. While effective on classification and factual QA, this greedy token-level approach is unstable in our GSM8K experiments and can exhibit prompt-template drift and noise-sensitive irreversible deci-sions (Section 5).

Private Evolution. Private Evolution (PE) (Lin et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025) is a training-free DP framework originally devel-oped for data synthesis via APIs. Its key struc-tural advantage is that only a low-sensitivity eval-uation step touches private data, while API-based generation is pure post-processing. This creates a favorable decoupling: exploration can be ar-bitrarily rich without consuming privacy budget. DP-ES adapts this principle from data synthesis to prompt optimization, inheriting the favorable privacy-exploration trade-off while introducing population-based search over the discrete prompt space. 

Evolution Strategies and DP. Evolution strate-gies (ES) (Rechenberg, 1973) are derivative-free algorithms that maintain and evolve populations of candidate solutions. Prior non-private prompt optimizers, including EvoPrompt (Guo et al., 2024) and PromptBreeder (Fernando et al., 2023), already use LLM-driven mutation and population-based selection to optimize discrete prompts. DP-ES builds on this evolutionary-prompt-optimization line while introducing record-level DP through sampled-Gaussian scoring and post-processing se-lection. The population-based design provides natu-ral robustness to noise-induced regressions—a crit-ical advantage under tight privacy budgets where evaluation noise is substantial. 

Privacy Auditing. Empirical privacy attacks and audits can reveal implementation errors but can-not certify DP from a finite number of trials (Nasr et al., 2021; Jagielski et al., 2020). We therefore base our guarantee on mechanism-level RDP ac-counting and use noise checks and an exact-match memorization stress test only as complementary diagnostics (Section 6).

Efficiency. 6 Privacy Guarantees 

DP-ES inherits formal (ε, δ )-DP from a Decou-pled DP Evolution view: mutations operate on public or previously privatized prompts, and only sampled-Gaussian evaluation accesses D. RDP accounting composes at most T K score releases; both deterministic and Gumbel-smoothed selection are post-processing. The worst reported bound is 

(ε=0 .71 , δ =10 −5), which we conservatively state as ε ≤ 1. Full derivation and reproducible accoun-tant settings are in Appendix B. 

Implementation Diagnostics. We record 1,000 injected-noise samples and verify that their stan-dardized distribution is consistent with the con-figured Gaussian mechanism via a Kolmogorov– Smirnov test. We also compare final-prompt be-havior on a small set of neighboring datasets as a debugging diagnostic. Because finite output sam-ples cannot certify DP or reliably estimate privacy loss in this high-dimensional discrete space, we do not interpret this diagnostic as an empirical ε; the guarantee comes from the mechanism-level RDP accountant. 

Synthetic Privacy Stress Test. To illustrate di-rect memorization risk, we construct 200 syn-thetic customer profiles containing 1,200 sensitive strings (six per profile: name, phone number, email, full address, street-level address fragment, and ac-count ID). Figure 4 reports exact and near-verbatim matching in final prompts: no listed string is found for DP-ES, while an intentionally adversarial non-DP control—which is explicitly given raw records and instructed to embed a directory—contains 40 strings (3.33%). This is a targeted sanity check, not a semantic-leakage attack or a substitute for the formal guarantee. 

7 Conclusion 

We presented a diagnostic and methodological con-tribution for differentially private prompt optimiza-tion. On the diagnostic side, a 30-run analysis shows substantial DP-OPT instability on GSM8K, while a logged trajectory exposes prompt-template drift and noise-sensitive irreversible decisions in token-level construction. On the methodological side, we introduced DP-ES , a structurally cleaner evolution-based alternative that maintains a popu-lation of full prompts, mutates them without expos-ing private records, and restricts privacy expendi-ture to sampled-Gaussian scoring; selection over privatized scores is post-processing. Across four tasks—GSM8K (88.1%), MedQA (99.7%), BANKING77 (73.5%), and Alpaca (86.8%, LLM-judge)—DP-ES matches or exceeds DP-OPT under a conservative ε ≤ 1 guar-antee, with ∼9× lower variance on GSM8K, 2.5 × faster wall-clock runtime, and 3.3 × fewer logged private-data call groups. Deterministic and Gumbel-smoothed selection have comparable util-ity and identical privacy guarantees because both post-process privatized scores. Noise-distribution checks and a 200-profile exact-match memoriza-tion stress test provide complementary diagnostics alongside the formal RDP accounting. 

Scope. The strongest evidence we provide is for 

optimization robustness under DP noise on bench-marks where prompt structure matters , with the largest gains on reasoning-heavy tasks where token-level construction is most brittle. End-to-end val-idation on genuinely sensitive, non-saturated de-ployment data—which would require benchmarks combining record-level sensitivity with non-trivial semantic reasoning—remains future work and is the most direct extension of this contribution.

Empirical Scope: No Sensitive-Data Deploy-ment Validation. Our experiments establish that DP-ES is a structurally more robust DP prompt optimizer than token-level alternatives on bench-marks where prompt structure matters. They do 

not validate end-to-end deployment on genuinely sensitive, non-saturated datasets (e.g., real clini-cal records, financial logs). Such validation would require benchmarks that combine record-level sen-sitivity with non-trivial semantic reasoning, which the current public benchmark landscape does not provide. The formal (ε, δ )-DP guarantee, by con-struction, bounds record-level leakage independent of the benchmark, but a direct sensitive-data de-ployment study remains future work. 

MedQA Ceiling Effect. DP-ES reaches 99.7% on the 200-question MedQA subset, with the Non-DP baseline saturating at 100.0 ±0.0. This near-ceiling performance limits the discriminative power of MedQA for studying privacy-utility trade-offs at ε=1 .0; the 6.2 pp improvement over DP-OPT on this saturated regime is informative but should not be over-interpreted as evidence for a non-trivial privacy-utility frontier on medical QA more broadly. A non-saturated medical benchmark (e.g., a harder open-ended clinical reasoning task) would provide stronger evidence. 

Privacy Attack Coverage. The 200-profile syn-thetic memorization stress test (Section 6) mea-sures direct or near-verbatim sensitive-string leak-age against an intentionally adversarial non-DP control. It does not test paraphrased memorization, rare-attribute disclosure, record reconstruction, or membership inference. Moreover, the mutation LLM can reproduce information already present in its parametric memory; DP-ES bounds the incre-mental influence of the private optimization dataset, not memorization inherited from pretraining. The formal (ε, δ )-DP guarantee is attack-agnostic, but a direct empirical attack suite comparing DP-ES against DP-OPT under matched bounds would pro-vide useful complementary evidence and remains future work. 

Efficiency Reporting Scope. The cost decompo-sition by call purpose (Appendix C.1, Table 7) is from a representative single-seed run on a 200-sample validation set. Aggregate wall-clock num-bers across the full 1,319-sample test set are re-ported separately (Table 6); token-level and call-purpose splits at full aggregate scale are not yet released. We report the most granular reproducible numbers we have and treat aggregate token-level reporting as future work. 

Task Diversity. We evaluate on four tasks (GSM8K, MedQA, BANKING77, Alpaca) cov-ering math reasoning, medical QA, intent clas-sification, and instruction-following. Additional benchmarks (e.g., summarization, code generation, open-ended clinical reasoning) would further char-acterize cross-domain generalization. 

Task-Dependent Advantage. DP-ES’s strongest gains over DP-OPT are on complex reasoning tasks (GSM8K: +38.6 pp, MedQA: +6.2 pp). On well-structured classification (BANKING77) and instruction-following (Alpaca), both meth-ods perform comparably, suggesting that DP-ES’s population-based exploration is most beneficial when the prompt search space is large and struc-tured prompts are critical. 

Privacy Budget Sweeps. Our verified experiments establish the common conservative bound ε ≤ 1

rather than a densely sampled privacy–utility fron-tier. Re-running all methods with noise recalibrated to several exact RDP targets would better charac-terize the trade-off. 

Theoretical Analysis of Task Complexity. We em-pirically observe that task complexity is a key deter-minant of the success of DP optimization. A formal theoretical framework linking prompt search space characteristics to privacy budget requirements is still needed. 

Future Work. Beyond the scope items above, promising directions include adaptive population sizing, multi-objective optimization (utility, pri-vacy, stability), and federated DP-ES for distributed sensitive data settings. 

Ethics Statement 

This work develops differentially private (DP) prompt-optimization mechanisms motivated by set-tings where the underlying optimization data may be sensitive. Our empirical evaluation is conducted on public benchmarks; the considerations below concern both this study and any downstream use of the released method. 

Privacy Scope: DP-ES provides formal ( ε, δ )-DP guarantees for prompt optimization under correct implementation and accounting. The reported im-plementation diagnostics and exact-match stress test do not certify DP or cover downstream deploy-ment risks beyond the mechanism. 

Data Use: We use public benchmarks and do not collect new personal data. Applications to real sensitive data should follow institutional review, access control, and data governance requirements. 

Dual-Use Concerns: As with any optimization technique, DP-ES could be misused to refine harm-ful applications. Responsible deployment should include access control, monitoring, and safety fil-tering. 

Transparency and Reproducibility: We provide code and privacy accounting details to enable veri-fication of claims and independent audits.

Proof sketch. Step 1 (Privacy source). At each round t, only Qt touches D. Each candidate score uses a uniformly sampled batch of size b, clips per-record utility to [0 , 1] , averages it, and adds Gaussian noise with standard deviation z/b .

Step 2 (Post-processing). Selection sees only pri-vatized scores; mutation sees only public or previ-ously privatized prompts. Neither operation adds privacy loss. 

Step 3 (RDP composition). We apply the without-replacement subsampling bound of Wang et al. (2019) to each score release, add RDP across at most T K adaptive releases, and convert to (ε, δ )-DP. Post-processing then gives the same guarantee for the released final prompt. 

Intuition. Private data influence the search only through noisy scalar scores. Every later operation— ranking, randomized smoothing, LLM mutation, and final choice—uses already privatized outputs.

B.4 Threat Model and Assumptions 

Our privacy guarantees assume: 1. Trusted curator: The party running DP-ES has access to the private dataset D but releases only the final prompt with DP guarantees. 2. Record-level privacy: The privacy unit is one data record (xi, y i). Group privacy (protect-ing multiple records per individual) requires scaling ε by group size. 3. Trusted computation boundary: The cura-tor and any model endpoint used for private-data evaluation are trusted processors. The re-leased prompt is protected; DP does not hide API inputs from the provider receiving them. 4. Mutation isolation: The mutation LLM re-ceives only a parent prompt, never records or record-derived text feedback. PII memo-rized independently in the pretrained model’s parameters is outside this record-level threat model and should be handled by provider safe-guards. 5. Post-processing safety: The released prompt may be used by downstream applications with-out additional loss about D, although new downstream data require their own privacy analysis. These assumptions are standard in the DP litera-ture and match those of DP-OPT and other DP-ML methods. 

B.5 Implementation Diagnostics 

We log the exact noise draws used by the scorer. For both GSM8K and MedQA, 1,000 standardized samples are consistent with the configured Gaus-sian distribution under a Kolmogorov–Smirnov check. We also rerun the optimizer on five neigh-boring datasets as a debugging exercise. The re-sulting nearest-neighbor output-distance statistic is degenerate on near-ceiling MedQA and is not a calibrated likelihood-ratio estimator; accordingly, we do not report it as an observed privacy parame-ter. These checks can catch missing noise or gross data-flow errors, but only the accountant in Table 5 establishes the formal guarantee.

C.2 DP-OPT Stability and Trajectory Analysis 

Across 30 GSM8K runs, DP-OPT achieves 

49 .5±28 .5% , whereas DP-ES achieves 

88 .1±3.2% . We use these distributional statistics directly rather than imposing a post-hoc accuracy threshold to label runs as failures. Table 8 sum-marizes the aggregate statistics and representative trajectory diagnostic. The main-text visualization (Figure 3) reproduces an actual DP-OPT trajectory and illustrates two mechanisms consistent with the observed instability: prompt-template drift during token-level construction and irreversible propagation of noisy ranking decisions. 

Root Cause Analysis: 1. Token-Level Myopia: DP-OPT generates prompts token by token using a histogram-based ex-ponential mechanism. At each position, it votes on the next token across data partitions. This greedy process lacks global coherence checking, allowing it to: • Omit critical placeholders like {question} 

during generation • Create syntactically valid but semantically meaningless prompts • Drift toward generic text unrelated to the task 

2. Non-Backtracking Search: Once a token is selected, DP-OPT cannot revise it. If early tokens lead to a poor prompt structure, the algorithm is permanently stuck. This contrasts with DP-ES, which maintains a diverse population and can re-cover from individual poor candidates. 

3. Sample-Based Evaluation Noise: DP-OPT evaluates candidates on small samples (typically 10-20 examples). A prompt that performs well by chance on this sample may be selected and prop-agated, even if it generalizes poorly. DP-ES miti-gates this through population diversity—multiple independent candidates reduce the risk of overfit-ting to evaluation noise. 

Noise-sensitive selection. DP noise can reverse the ordering of candidates with similar utility. DP-OPT commits to each token-level decision without backtracking, so an early rank inversion can per-manently redirect construction. DP-ES does not eliminate rank inversions; instead, its population re-tains multiple complete hypotheses and can recover in later rounds. 

Why DP-ES Improves Stability: 

• Prompt-level generation: Mutations oper-ate on complete prompts, preserving global structure • Population diversity: Even if one candidate is degraded by noise, others can compensate • Privacy-free exploration: Mutations don’t consume privacy budget, allowing extensive search This analysis offers a mechanism-level explana-tion consistent with the 38.6 pp mean performance gap and the substantially lower variance of DP-ES on GSM8K.

C.5 Practitioner Hyperparameter Guide 

Start with K = 6 , T = 3 and recompute the RDP bound whenever K, T , b, n, or z changes. In-creasing KT increases both evaluation cost and composed privacy loss; reducing K narrows explo-ration, while reducing T limits iterative refinement.

D Proofs 

D.1 Proof of Proposition 1 

By the post-processing property of differential pri-vacy (Dwork and Roth, 2014), a function of public inputs or previously privatized outputs adds no pri-vacy loss. Mutate receives only a parent prompt and public randomness, never D or record-derived feedback; it is therefore post-processing. 

D.2 Proof of Proposition 2 

Because mutation and candidate generation do not access D, they incur zero additional privacy loss. Therefore, for any number of mutations, pri-vacy loss is determined entirely by the sampled-Gaussian score releases. 

D.3 Proof of Theorem 3 

Mutations consume zero budget (Proposi-tion 1). Each executed iteration releases K

sampled-Gaussian scores. Selection and subsequent mutation are post-processing, so the final prompt inherits the same (ε, δ ) guarantee.
