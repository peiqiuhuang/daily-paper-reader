# A Bregman inertial iteratively regularized extragradient method for bilevel variational inequality problems 

## Kangming Chen 1, Atsushi Hori 2, Ellen H. Fukuda 3

Abstract 

In this paper, we consider bilevel variational inequality problems, where the feasible set is the solution set of another variational inequality. We propose a Bregman inertial regularized extragradient method for solving these problems. By performing the inertial extrapolation in the dual space, the proposed method aligns the inertial step with the structure of the Bregman three-point identity. We also derive explicit non-asymptotic bounds for the generalized gap function. More precisely, using diminishing regularization, we establish convergence rates of 

O(1 /k 1−b) for the optimality gap and O(1 /k b) for the feasibility gap with 0 <b < 1. For constant regularization parameter η > 0, we establish an O(1 /k )

rate for the optimality gap and an O(1 /k ) + O(η) bound for the feasibility gap. Furthermore, numerical experiments on traffic networks and multi-portfolio Nash equilibrium problems demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework. 

Keywords: Variational inequalities, bilevel optimization, inertial acceleration, Bregman divergence, dual extrapolation

We consider the following (bilevel) variational inequality problem: Find x ∈ Q such that ⟨H(x), z − x⟩ ≥ 0 for all z ∈ Q, (VIP( H,Q)) where Q is the solution set of the following variational inequality: 

Q := {x ∈ X | ⟨ F (x), y − x⟩ ≥ 0 for all y ∈ X}. (VIP( F ,X)) Problem (VIP( H,Q)) arises naturally in equilibrium selection. For instance, consider a noncooperative game whose Nash equilibria are characterized as the solution set of a variational inequality (VIP( F ,X)). When the game admits multiple equilibria, it is often desirable to select one satisfying an additional criterion, such as maximizing social welfare; see, e.g., (Samadi and Yousefian 2025, Subsection 1.1). Such a sec-ondary selection objective can be modeled through another operator H. This leads to the problem of finding a solution of upper-level variational inequality (VIP( H,Q)). Therefore, bilevel variational inequalities provide a natural framework for equilibrium selection in game theory. Regarding methods, a classical approach to solving variational inequality problems is the extragradient method in Korpelevich (1976). For problem (VIP( H,Q)), the corresponding update is given by 

yk = PQ(xk − λkH(xk)) ,xk+1 = PQ(xk − λkH(yk)) , (1) where λk > 0 is the stepsize, and PQ(·) denotes the orthogonal projection onto Q.However, the hierarchical structure of (VIP( F ,X)) makes PQ(·) difficult to compute since Q itself is a solution set. To address this issue, a regularization-based strategy is often employed, e.g., Samadi and Yousefian (2025); the method approximates the bilevel structure using a regularized operator F + ηkH over the feasible set X, whose projection PX (·) is assumed to be easy to compute. The regularized extragradient update is given by 

yk = PX (xk − λk(F (xk) + ηkH(xk))) ,xk+1 = PX (xk − λk(F (yk) + ηkH(yk))) , (2) where ηk is a regularization parameter. Recently, Samadi and Yousefian (2025) established iteration complexity bounds for (2) in terms of the dual gap function (Defi-nition 1) under suitable conditions on λk and ηk. Further developments on hierarchical variational inequalities include regularization frameworks and extragradient meth-ods with complexity guarantees (see, e.g., Cortild et al. (2025); Dvurechensky et al. (2025)), as well as stochastic variance-reduced extragradient methods (Dvurechensky et al. (2026)). To accelerate convergence, inertial techniques have been incorporated into algo-rithms for monotone inclusions and variational inequalities since the seminal work 2of Alvarez and Attouch (2001). In standard Euclidean settings, Alves et al. (2025) introduced an inertial effect in the current iterate xk. This yields the extrapolated base point:

For this reason, we generalize the inertial regularized extragradient method by replacing the Euclidean projection with a Bregman proximal mapping and by constructing the inertial extrapolation in the corresponding dual space. Note that Bregman divergence is generated by a strongly convex function ω that can be selected according to both the geometry of the feasible set and the structure of the problem. Indeed, the Bregman three-point identity and the associated proximal analysis are expressed in terms of gradient differences ∇ω(x) − ∇ ω(y).Thus, unlike standard approaches that perform linear extrapolation in the primal space as in (3), we construct the extrapolated point by aggregating the inertial extrap-olation in the dual space induced by the distance-generating function ω. (2015); Nesterov (2007, 2009); Wang et al. (2024). # 6 Conclusions 

We proposed a Bregman inertial regularized extragradient method for bilevel varia-tional inequalities with inertial extrapolation in the dual space. We show that this framework preserves Bregman three-point identities, which allows the method to pro-vide non-asymptotic complexity bounds under standard monotonicity and Lipschitz assumptions. Our simple numerical experiments show that using Bregman divergence 23 Fig. 6 Convergence of the infeasibility ϕ(¯ yk ) for the traffic network equilibrium problem.
