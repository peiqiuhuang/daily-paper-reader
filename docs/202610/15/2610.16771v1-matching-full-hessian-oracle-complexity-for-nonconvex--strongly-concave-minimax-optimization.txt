Abstract 

We characterize the oracle complexity of finding first-order stationary points in smooth nonconvex–strongly-concave (NC–SC) minimax optimization when each query provides the full Hessian in addition to the function value and gradient. For Φ( x) = max y f (x, y ), assume that f has an L-Lipschitz gradient, a ρ-Lipschitz Hessian, and is μ-strongly concave in y. Let 

κ = L/μ , let ∆ denote the initial value gap, and define r = ρκ 3/2ε/L 2.We establish matching deterministic upper bounds and randomized lower bounds, up to logarithmic factors and additive initialization costs. When the leading term dominates initialization, the resulting minimax complexity is

Despite the stronger oracle, the optimal complexity exhibits a nontrivial dependence on accuracy, conditioning, and Hessian smoothness. In particular, these regimes exhibit accuracy dependencies ε−3/2, ε−12 /7, and ε−2, respectively. The upper bounds combine second-order regularity estimates for a saddle Moreau envelope with certified accelerated reduced-dual solves and amortized warm starts. The lower bounds combine an all-derivative lift with a serial composition of private-handle constructions, using hard instances chosen independently of the algorithm’s realized random seed. The resulting characterization quantifies improvements over the classical first-order benchmark under gradient smoothness alone and identifies the regime in which that benchmark is recovered.

Contributions. The paper makes four contributions. • We characterize the bundled full-Hessian minimax query complexity by a single three-phase function A1Ψκ(r), with matching deterministic upper and randomized lower bounds up to logarithmic and initialization terms. • For the upper bound, we introduce a saddle Moreau envelope whose Hessian is O(ρκ 3/2)-Lipschitz, but whose proximal saddle can be solved through a reduced dual with O(ρ)-Lipschitz Hessian. Eliminating the primal block before accelerating the dual is essential: the eliminated block has condition number at most three, and the reduced-dual contraction cost is ˜O(1 + ( ρD/μ )2/7), where D is an upper bound on the initial distance to the reduced-dual minimizer. 2• We amortize warm-start distances rather than paying their worst-case value at every outer step. # 2 Related Work 

Table 1 summarizes the most relevant oracle-complexity results and highlights the distinction between the present work and existing first- and second-order methods. 

First-order NC–SC complexity. Lin, Jin, and Jordan [1] used accelerated proximal-point ideas to obtain a near-optimal ˜O(∆ L√κ ε −2) gradient bound for value-function stationarity. Zhang et al. [2] established the corresponding Ω(∆ L√κ ε −2) lower bound in the general smooth setting and gave an acceleration scheme matching it up to logarithms. Li et al. [3] independently proved a √κ ε −2 deterministic first-order lower bound under their normalization. These results identify the large-r endpoint of our frontier, but they neither charge nor exploit a full-Hessian query. 

Higher smoothness and second-order information. Wang, Zhang, and Zhang [4] showed that a first-order method can exploit a Lipschitz Hessian of Φ, obtaining an ε−7/4 upper dependence and an ε−12 /7 lower dependence under the source’s normalization. Their oracle remains first order and their smoothness assumption is imposed directly on the value function. Luo, Li, and Chen [5] studied a different target: an approximate second-order stationary point of Φ. Their Minimax Cubic Newton method uses second-order oracle calls with O(κ3/2√ρ ε −3/2)dependence. Our target is first-order stationarity, our Hessian-Lipschitz assumption is on the joint function f , and the goal is a matching minimax query characterization. Subsequent trust-region, gradient-norm-regularized, homogeneous, and single-loop Newton schemes retain an ˜O(ε−3/2) iteration dependence for approximate second-order stationarity, while addressing subproblem structure, Hessian- vector implementation, or the inner-loop bottleneck in different ways [6–9]. These are algorithmic upper bounds for the stronger SOSP target; none gives a matching bundled-oracle lower bound for the FOSP criterion studied here. 

Second-order acceleration and lower-bound tools. The upper proof uses the large-step accelerated hybrid proximal extragradient framework of Monteiro and Svaiter [11] and the implementable high-order line-search machinery of Jiang, Wang, and Zhang [12]. The high-accuracy lower branch starts from the randomized all-derivative stationary-point lower bound of Carmon et al. [10]. # 6 Discussion 

The theorem shows that Hessian access helps only after its smoothness is measured relative to the target accuracy and the saddle condition number. The intermediate exponent 12 /7 is not an isolated property of an outer method; it is the balance between an O(A1) envelope trajectory and a distance-sensitive 2 /7-power inner contraction. The matching lower packet reproduces the same balance through the relation q7/2 ≍ r.Several boundaries remain. The oracle is exact and bundles value, gradient, and the full joint Hessian at one unit cost; separate gradient/Hessian costs, Hessian-vector products, stochastic or finite-sum oracles, and finite- dimension refinements require different characterizations. The result hides logarithmic factors and records a separate initialization term I0. Finally, the proof treats the three general results listed after Theorem 4.1 as external interfaces. All problem-specific reductions and randomized quantifier arguments are included below.
