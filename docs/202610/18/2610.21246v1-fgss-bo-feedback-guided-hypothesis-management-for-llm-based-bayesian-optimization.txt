Abstract 

Large language model (LLM)-guided Bayesian optimization (BO) uses past observations to guide search, but proposed directions and their revision can remain implicit. We propose Feedback-Guided Search State (FGSS), a struc-tured hypothesis-management interface con-necting language-based guidance to observed evidence, executable parameter interventions, and comparative support updates. An LLM re-flector proposes and revises hypotheses; a deter-ministic curator validates them and maintains identities and test records. The evolving state guides candidate construction and priorities. In-stantiated on LLAMBO, FGSS-BO retains its surrogate and default expected improvement selection, with a bounded testing policy. On two retrospective protocol/media tasks with nearest-neighbor response oracles, it attains higher sample mean final best and search AUC than the matched LLAMBO baseline and eval-uated non-LLM baselines. Prose-reflection and EI-only controls show mixed component effects. Six direct-training hyperparameter-optimization tasks show task-dependent trans-fer, strongest on diabetes SVM; exact-grid and reaction controls examine other settings. FGSS exposes hypotheses, their execution, and cross-round revisions for inspection while supporting search gains in selected settings. 

1 Introduction 

Bayesian optimization (BO) addresses black-box search when evaluations are expensive, including hyperparameter tuning and scientific experimen-tation (Jones et al., 1998; Shahriari et al., 2016; Frazier, 2018). Large language models (LLMs) have been incorporated into BO to interpret task descriptions, sample candidates, and predict out-comes from historical examples (Liu et al., 2024). These capabilities offer a flexible interface to ex-perimental parameters whose meanings and admis-sible values differ across tasks. LLAMBO maintains configuration–outcome ob-servations and conditions its LLM calls on that his-tory (Liu et al., 2024). It does not separately main-tain identified search hypotheses with evidence ref-erences and later test outcomes. This distinction matters under a limited evaluation budget: a sug-gestion to reduce one parameter may produce a candidate that also changes other coordinates, so its outcome need not directly test the suggested direction. How can an LLM optimizer turn such suggestions into revisable guidance linked to the decisions they influence? Inspired by reflection and evolving-memory ap-proaches (Shinn et al., 2023; Suzgun et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a), we propose Feedback-Guided Search State (FGSS) for structured, auditable hy-pothesis management in BO. Its continually up-dated guidance consists of search hypotheses with evidence references, an anchor, a parameter inter-vention, and an expected outcome direction. An LLM reflector proposes and revises these hypothe-ses; a deterministic curator validates operations and maintains identities and lifecycle records. Ac-cepted hypotheses guide candidate construction and priorities, while eligible matched tests update comparative support for their primary hypothesis. The base surrogate continues to use the observation history. We instantiate FGSS on LLAMBO and call the resulting optimizer FGSS-BO. LLAMBO sep-arates candidate sampling, surrogate prediction, and acquisition selection, allowing us to intro-duce hypothesis management into the search loop. FGSS-BO and its matched baseline use the same feasibility-preserving candidate interface, while re-taining the LLAMBO surrogate and default EI se-lection. FGSS-BO additionally permits a bounded exception for hypothesis-test selection. We assess search performance alongside this hypothesis-management interface. On retrospec-tive iPSC-RPE protocol search and C2C12 culture-1arXiv:2610.21246v1 [cs.CL] 15 Oct 2026 media search (Kanda et al., 2022; Cosenza et al., 2022), FGSS-BO attains higher sample mean final best and search AUC than its matched baseline. Prose-reflection and testing ablations show mixed component effects. Six preselected hyperparameter-optimization (HPO) tasks use di-rect model training, with the clearest gains on dia-betes SVM; exact-grid, reaction, and chromatogra-phy controls examine other settings. Our contribu-tions are: • A structured search-hypothesis representation linking evidence, parameter interventions, and revision and test records through persistent identities. • A search-control mechanism translating LLM-proposed hypotheses into feasible candidate priorities and matched tests through deter-ministic curation and comparative support up-dates. • Paired offline protocol/media comparisons with LLM and non-LLM baselines, compo-nent ablations, and direct-training HPO trans-fer checks, supplemented by candidate and oracle diagnostics. 

2 Related Work 

Bayesian optimization for scientific discovery. 

BO combines surrogate updating with acquisition-based query selection (Jones et al., 1998; Shahriari et al., 2016; Frazier, 2018). Applications include hyperparameter optimization (Snoek et al., 2012), robotic iPSC-RPE protocol search, and culture-media development (Kanda et al., 2022; Narayanan et al., 2025). LLM-guided methods add a language interface to parameter semantics and experimental knowledge within this numerical search loop. 

LLM-guided optimization. LLM-guided opti-mizers complement statistical optimization with contextual reasoning and pretrained knowledge. LLAMBO frames BO components in natural lan-guage and uses LLMs for warm-starting, candi-date sampling, and surrogate prediction (Liu et al., 2024); related systems address genetic perturba-tion design, scientific optimization, and molecular lead optimization (Roohani et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026b). Gupta et al. (2025) use randomized-feedback controls to diagnose out-come sensitivity in BioDiscoveryAgent. Whereas these controls measure dependence on feedback content, FGSS addresses how an optimizer repre-sents and revises search hypotheses and turns them into search actions. 

Feedback, memory, and reflection in LLM agents. Self-Refine iteratively revises outputs us-ing self-feedback, while Reflexion retains reflec-tive experience across trials (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023). Dynamic Cheatsheet accumu-lates reusable guidance, and Agentic Context Engi-neering (ACE) organizes evolving context through reflection and curation (Suzgun et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a). FGSS adopts these memory and cu-ration principles for numerical BO. Its hypothesis records specify an observed anchor, a parameter intervention, and an expected outcome direction, making the guidance executable. Candidate lineage connects eligible numerical comparisons to the cor-responding hypothesis, while the surrogate retains the observation history. Our prose-summary con-trol tests a simpler continuously updated memory interface; FGSS additionally maintains individual hypotheses and their test records. 

3 Background and Problem Setup 

Bayesian optimization and LLM-guided opti-mization. We consider maximization of an ex-pensive black-box objective f : X → R under a limited evaluation budget. Starting from K initial observations D0, after t subsequent search evalua-tions the history is 

Dt = D0 ∪ { (xi, y i)}ti=1 , yi = f (xi). (1) BO then selects an unevaluated configuration by maximizing an acquisition criterion over the re-maining search space. LLAMBO augments this loop with LLM-based candidate sampling and sur-rogate prediction while conditioning prompts on task descriptions and previous evaluations (Liu et al., 2024). 

Explicit search-hypothesis management. We augment Dt with a state St of search hypotheses, evidence references, revisions, and eligible test out-comes. Unlike the observations themselves, these records specify proposed actions whose guidance can change across rounds. 

Optimization settings. The method accepts nu-merical, ordinal, and categorical variables. The primary retrospective tasks use a historical-table or-acle bfNN , not new laboratory measurements. Exact-grid, emulator/simulator, and direct-training HPO 2tasks test other interfaces. We present the method for maximization; minimization reverses outcome comparisons and uses the corresponding improve-ment criterion.

5 Experimental Setup 

5.1 Datasets and Tasks 

The primary protocol/media tasks expose configuration–outcome feedback under limited 4budgets with different variable types. 

iPSC-RPE. The task concerns differentiation of induced pluripotent stem cells into retinal pigment epithelial cells, maximizing pigmenta-tion score (Kanda et al., 2022). We use the 

253G1_results_r5r7r10 table. The seven set-tings cover preconditioning concentration and du-ration, detachment duration and pipetting, and dif-ferentiation supplement schedules. Ordered finite concentration/detachment levels, integer durations and speed, and a binary pipetting-length setting define a discrete configuration space. 

C2C12. We use a 209-row processed table from the upstream DBO archive associated with multi-information-source culture-media optimiza-tion (Cosenza et al., 2022). Fourteen media co-ordinates are continuous on [0 , 1] in the supplied representation. All methods maximize the archived response score on its supplied scale, without addi-tional standardization. The source query label se-lects among four measurement sources for response lookup rather than specifying a physical culture in-tervention. Media and source-evaluation costs are outside the objective. Appendix A.3 details the table provenance and response interpretation. 

Response and initialization protocol. Both tasks return the score of a nearest historical configu-ration in normalized parameter coordinates; C2C12 matching is restricted by source. This oracle per-mits new parameter combinations but does not val-idate their physical response. Initialization uses the ten lowest-scoring historical observations, shared across methods, followed by twenty search evalua-tions. This outcome-conditioned low-start protocol is a retrospective stress test, not label-free prospec-tive initialization. The exact-grid iPSC control re-stricts candidates to observed configurations and removes nearest-neighbor assignment. Cross-domain checks include six preselected Bayesmark HPO tasks from the LLAMBO reposi-tory (Liu et al., 2024): breast, digits, and diabetes, each paired with RandomForest and SVM. These train models directly without a nearest-neighbor oracle. Classification maximizes five-fold CV ac-curacy; diabetes minimizes positive CV MSE. Ini-tialization uses ten label-free public configurations, shared within each pair, followed by twenty search evaluations. Held-out test scores never enter op-timization feedback (Appendix A.15). We also retain all five Summit reaction tasks (Felton et al., 2021; Hone et al., 2017; Reizman et al., 2016) and Olympus HPLC (Häse et al., 2021) in the appendix. 

5.2 Baselines 

We evaluate a feasibility-preserving adapta-tion of LLAMBO ( LLAMBO-Feasible , hereafter 

LLAMBO ). It retains the LLAMBO surrogate and EI, with the shared typed sampling/refinement in-terface but no FGSS state or test-policy overrides. The free-form variant, LLAMBO-Freeform , is evaluated separately for candidate-interface diag-nostics; it is an implementation-level variant rather than a strict reproduction of original LLAMBO. We also compare Random Search over the le-gal domains, HEBO (Cowen-Rivers et al., 2022), and GP-EI implemented in BoTorch (Balandat et al., 2020). GP-EI standardizes outcomes and normalizes numerical coordinates, using categor-ical kernels for pipetting length or measurement source. It optimizes EI over legal discrete settings for iPSC and continuous coordinates with enumer-ated sources for C2C12. All five methods share the actual initial observations, admissible domains, evaluation budget, and response oracle. Baseline fitting and acquisition settings are detailed in Ap-pendix A.6. The iPSC comparison adds Reflective Sum-mary : each round, the same extractor’s evidence and previous prose summary are used to produce a replacement summary for the shared candidate re-finer. It has no hypothesis curation or tests. FGSS w/o Tests retains structured reflection and ordinary state guidance but disables matched-test generation, test-policy overrides, and direct support updates. 

No-Override retains test candidates and support updates but always selects by EI. Reflection set-tings and evaluation budgets are shared, not real-ized token totals. HPO compares the two complete LLM pipelines.

5.4 Implementation Details 

The primary tasks use K = 10 , T = 20 , and batch size one. The shared LLAMBO configuration uses GPT-4o mini, ten candidates, two templates, and ten generations. We use this backend for its practi-cal balance of instruction following and API cost. FGSS adds a reflection call each round at temper-ature zero with an output cap of 1500 tokens and one configured retry, increasing total LLM com-pute. Table 8 reports its recorded per-run token use and elapsed time. Regime cutoffs use supplied task-response units (Table 5). Optional domain-prior cards and injections are disabled; task descriptions and pretrained LLM knowledge remain available. The appendix specifies settings, prompts, state ex-amples, and provenance.

7 Discussion 

FGSS organizes hypothesis management around inspectable records: a proposed direction identifies parameters, an anchor, and an intervention whose later comparison can revise the same record. The LLM interprets evidence and proposes changes; fixed rules govern admissibility and numerical sup-port updates. Prose reflection and EI-only selection also achieve higher sample means than LLAMBO, so the component results separate this record-and-action interface from a uniform optimization ad-vantage for full FGSS. A candidate can outperform its matched control without improving the incumbent. Local compara-tive support and global progress therefore answer different questions, and FGSS tracks them sepa-rately. The higher AUC of No-Override and FGSS w/o Tests suggests a trade-off between early objec-tive progress and obtaining targeted comparisons under this protocol. HPO extends the evaluation to directly trained objectives, with the strongest gains on diabetes SVM. Exact-grid iPSC’s lower mean FGSS AUC shows that state-guided priorities need not improve progress in a restricted candidate domain. 8 Conclusion 

FGSS provides structured hypothesis management for LLM-guided BO, turning proposed directions into executable parameter interventions and linking eligible comparisons to later revisions. FGSS-BO retains LLAMBO’s surrogate and default EI se-lection, with a bounded testing policy. Its records expose the path from language-based hypotheses to search actions and updates. The complete system achieves higher sample means on the two retrospec-tive protocol/media tasks and yields gains on some direct-training HPO tasks, while component and oracle controls show mixed effects across settings. 

9 Limitations 

The primary tasks use historical-table nearest-neighbor responses and outcome-conditioned low-score initialization. Their results describe that retro-spective protocol, not label-free prospective labora-tory search. C2C12 retains a measurement-source variable, so the query score should not be inter-preted as an assay-independent biological optimum. Prospective validation is needed to assess measure-ment noise, batch effects, experimental failures, and temporal drift. Evaluation uses one LLM backend and base op-timizer; FGSS adds reflection cost. The HPO suite compares the two complete LLM pipelines on di-rectly trained objectives. Future evaluation could extend paired runs to ten seeds and examine fur-ther backends, initialization policies, budgets, and optimizers; noise-aware support updates and test prioritization could refine the balance of local hy-potheses and exploration.

Local evidence and control matching. For nu-merical variables, differences are normalized by the specified range; unordered categories use a zero/one mismatch. For a target parameter, an ob-servational pair must have target difference at least 

0.10 and mean nuisance difference at most 0.30 .Pairs are ordered by increasing nuisance difference, decreasing target difference, and decreasing abso-lute outcome difference, retaining at most four per parameter and twelve overall. Median directional differences and agreement summarize support; at most five context-dependent interaction records are retained. These are observational contrasts, not identified causal effects. A direct test has one parent hypothesis, one or two target variables, and an observed anchor. Only the primary hypothesis receives its outcome-based support update, even if other hypotheses con-tributed to candidate guidance. In open spaces, non-target coordinates are held fixed and targeted coordinates are changed according to the interven-tion. Numeric steps are 10% of the range per depth (log-range for log variables); integer steps are rounded with a minimum of one, and ordered finite supports advance by their index. Depths one through six are attempted. Unordered levels change without a numerical direction. All changes are clipped/canonicalized, and unchanged or pre-viously evaluated configurations are rejected. In-teractions change the specified coordinates jointly; this is not an exhaustive factorial design. For table-backed tasks, only label-free covariate matches to observed rows can receive a direct support update, with root-mean-square and per-coordinate nuisance limits of 0.25 and distinct oracle identities. Exact-grid candidates must be observed configurations. Approximate matches can differ in non-target vari-ables; nearest-neighbor assignment can also change the underlying experimental row. These compar-isons are not estimates of an isolated biological causal effect. 

Ordinary candidate policies. Scientific-task global candidates use independent uniform sam-pling of numerical bounds or legal finite lev-els; HPO uses the stated linear/log/logit sam-pling scales (Appendix A.15). Local exploitation chooses a top-evidence configuration uniformly, then samples each coordinate locally with proba-bility 0.8 (globally otherwise). Continuous local sampling is uniform within 12% of the full range around the center, clipped to bounds; integers use the corresponding rounded radius, ordered supports use the center and adjacent levels, and unordered categories retain the center with probability 0.8 or 10 sample a legal level. State-guided candidates prior-itize testable anchor interventions using adaptive-priority sampling, with a small nuisance perturba-tion for ordinary guided proposals; these are not au-tomatically eligible for support updates. Recovery samples up to 128 global proposals to find normal-ized distance at least 0.25 from repeated low-gain centers, then falls back to global sampling. Chal-lenger proposals add evidence-linked alternative parameter/direction/context hypotheses; they do not replace every recovery draw. Ratios are normalized and integer quotas use largest-remainder allocation. Unusable guided quo-tas return to global search or recovery rather than generating ungrounded tests.

A.3 Search Spaces and Initialization 

Summit initializes continuous coordinates with seeded Latin hypercube sampling and balances cat-alyst categories; initial points are identical within each paired seed. The primary iPSC-RPE and C2C12 tasks use outcome-conditioned low-score initialization and nearest-neighbor response lookup for retrospective evaluation. Exact-grid iPSC re-stricts candidate configurations to observed rows. HPLC initializes uniformly from observed config-urations without labels and uses the reported of-fline lookup. C2C12’s source indicator denotes measurement provenance, so the optimizer queries configuration–source pairs. 

C2C12 table provenance and response scale. 

The processed table copies all 209 rows of the up-stream DBO archive: the first fourteen columns of 

BO_data.txt provide media coordinates already on [0 , 1] , and its fifteenth column supplies source .The two columns of BO_outputs.txt are retained verbatim as score and score_var . An all-row nu-merical audit confirms this mapping exactly. The source study measures C2C12 growth through cell counting and correlated assay responses; we report the saved response-score scale rather than recon-structing absolute cell counts or an assay-specific inverse transformation. Maximization seeks a larger archived response at a fixed evaluation bud-get. Media costs, source-specific evaluation costs, and source-bias calibration are outside this objec-tive. All rows define a fixed response lookup, with no C2C12 train/test split or learned emulator fitted in this study. Beyond the disclosed low-score ini-tialization, surrogate and reflector inputs contain queried outcomes only. Matching unobserved con-figurations and interventions consults covariates, not their response labels. The oracle returns a saved scalar deterministically; score_var is retained as metadata rather than used to sample additional ob-servation noise.

A.6 Non-LLM Baseline Implementations and Trajectories 

Random Search samples numerical bounds or finite legal levels uniformly. HEBO uses version 0.3.5, with the imported initialization count determining its startup size. In the primary tasks its GP fitter uses Adam instead of the default pSGLD after a numerical failure in C2C12 smoke; other model set-tings are retained. Ordered finite levels are encoded by index and unordered labels as categories. This fitting change is not applied to the Summit runs, which retain the package defaults. Cross-domain differences therefore cannot be attributed solely to task domain. GP-EI uses BoTorch 0.8.5 with standardized outcomes and range-normalized numerical coor-dinates. It uses a categorical covariance component for iPSC pipetting length, C2C12 source, and Reiz-man catalyst identity. Acquisition optimization uses ten restarts and 256 raw samples; continu-ous optimization has a maximum of 200 iterations. Fully discrete iPSC uses legal discrete local search, C2C12 enumerates source labels, and Summit uses continuous or mixed optimization as appropriate. No wrapper-level random fallback replaces failed fitting. Repeated evaluations consume budget, and all methods reuse the actual paired initialization.

A.11 Component-Ablation Protocol and Paired Differences 

All four iPSC variants share ten low-score initial observations, twenty search evaluations, the legal parameter domains, and the nearest-neighbor re-sponse oracle. The shared LLM settings are GPT-4o mini, ten candidates, two templates, and ten generations. The two reflective controls and full FGSS use a reflection output cap of 1500 tokens, temperature zero, and one configured retry. This matches call settings, not actual tokens or total compute with LLAMBO. 

Reflective Summary. Each round, the same structured evidence extractor supplies current evi-dence alongside the previous prose summary. The LLM replaces that summary with updated guid-ance on promising directions, contradictions, un-certainty, coverage, and exploration. The summary conditions the shared candidate refiner, without hy-pothesis cards, deterministic hypothesis curation, matched-test generation, direct support updates, or test-policy overrides. A.15 Direct-Training Bayesmark HPO Transfer 

The suite was fixed before evaluation: breast can-cer, handwritten digits, and diabetes datasets, each paired with RandomForest and SVM. All six tasks and both methods are retained. There are 72 completed runs and 2160 configuration evalua-tions, with six paired seeds, ten initialization points, twenty search evaluations, and batch size one. Ta-ble 14 reports CV-based optimization metrics. The suite compares the two complete LLM pipelines. 

Objective and preprocessing. Each configura-tion trains a scikit-learn model directly with five-fold cross-validation. Classification maximizes accuracy, using stratified folds; regression mini-mizes positive MSE. We retain the downloaded LLAMBO train/test splits and feature preprocess-ing. Diabetes targets are standardized once using the training split’s mean and standard deviation before CV, following that runner; this is not a fold-wise target-scaling protocol. The held-out test set uses the same training-target scaling and is used only for reporting, never for candidate generation, surrogate prediction, or state updates. RandomForest uses ten trees, random state zero, and no fixed maximum leaf count. Classification SVM uses an RBF SVC with probability estimation enabled and random state zero; regression uses an RBF SVR. Folds and training randomness are fixed. The optimization seeds index initialization configu-rations and candidate randomness, not independent training splits. 

Initialization and representation. Both meth-ods use the first ten public initialization configu-rations for their model/seed from the LLAMBO repository, canonicalized to eight decimal places without inspecting outcomes. The HPO spaces are copied from its Bayesmark configuration, with sam-pling scales in Table 15. Global and local candidate sampling respect linear, log, or logit coordinates; evidence distances remain raw range-normalized. Log-variable test steps use log-range increments. Both methods serialize candidate floats to eight decimals and outcomes to six, preventing small im-purity values from collapsing under the legacy for-matter. The shared interface follows the feasibility-preserving baseline, not an exact replication of free-form LLAMBO.
