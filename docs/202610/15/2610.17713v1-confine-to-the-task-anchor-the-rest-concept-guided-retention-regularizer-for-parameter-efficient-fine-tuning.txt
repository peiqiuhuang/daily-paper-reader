Abstract 

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has been widely used, yet the adapted model suffers catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge. While most existing work hardens the adapter by constraining where the weight update may lie, we note that constraining this weight channel does not constrain the activation channel. In this paper, we propose Concept-Guided Retention (CGR), a plug-and-play regularizer that constrains both for any PEFT base. CGR reads a frozen sparse autoencoder (SAE) and treats two disjoint concept sets differently. It softly confines the update to the task-concept subspace (via weight channel), and anchors a selected set of general-purpose concepts to the frozen model (via activation channel). We also provide anchor value bounds based on weight updates to understand how they interact, and show that both terms are necessary. On a CLIP vision panel, CGR significantly improves retention at comparable accuracy for every base, and the same pattern holds for world-knowledge retention under Llama-3.1-8B instruction tuning. Because the protected directions are named SAE concepts, CGR remains inspectable and controllable. 

# 1 Introduction 

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapts frozen founda-tion models at a fraction of full fine-tuning’s cost (Chen et al. 2022). Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)(Hu et al. 2022), the de facto standard, parameterizes weight updates as ∆W = BA .LoRA optimizes downstream loss without penalizing drift from W0. The adapter is therefore free to write into direc-tions that carry general-purpose capability, and fine-tuned adapters measurably forget pre-trained knowledge (Bider-man et al. 2024; Shuttleworth et al. 2025). The question is how an adapter can learn a new task while preserving pre-trained knowledge. 

The dominant answer is weight-geometric , which is one-sided. Recent work shows that initializing or confin-ing the adapter within a chosen weight subspace mitigates catastrophic forgetting to a degree (Xiong and Xie 2026; Tang et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2024; Luo et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025a). These methods differ in how they choose the subspace, but most of them constrain only where the weight update lies . A subspace constraint only bounds the compo-nent of the weight delta outside the chosen subspace and leaves the drift of activation space unconstrained (Peng et al. 2026). Thus, weight-subspace constraints alone are insuffi-cient for activation-space retention. Instead, we need to con-strain both: a weight-subspace (where the update lies) and an activation space (how retained features respond). To the best of our knowledge, no existing PEFT method constrains both, a key gap that motivates our work. To address weight- and activation-space retention together, we ask what to constrain and how to constrain it. We an-swer this with Sparse autoencoders (SAEs). SAEs have emerged as a tool for editing and steering concepts in a frozen model (Marks et al. 2025). A key innovation is how we leverage a frozen SAE from a fixed layer to derive two dis-joint concept sets: a task set and a concept-retention set. Us-ing these two sets, our proposed Concept-Guided Retention (CGR), develops a plug-and-play regularizer with two terms: A confine term softly holds the adapter weight-delta in the SAE task-concept subspace (the weight channel). An anchor term holds the fine-tuned model’s selected retention concepts (general-purpose features) to their pre-fine-tuning values (the activation channel). The construction is modality-agnostic, interpretable, and we validate it on vision and language tasks. 

Contributions. 

• Role-split retention. From a frozen SAE we select two small disjoint concept sets: a task set, to which the weight update is confined, and a retention set, which is anchored to the frozen model. Anchoring a small set of the most frequently active off-task concepts beats blanketing every feature. 

• A plug-and-play method. We introduce two regularizers that can be added to any PEFT adapter. Since adaptation is guided by named SAE concept basis, features remain interpretable after fine-tuning. 

• Bounds on retention-concept drift. We propose the an-chor term as the expected drift of the retention con-cepts and derive bounds based on weight-space quanti-ties (Prop. 2). This derivation places weight projections and concept control in a common framework, clarifying how learned weight updates translate into movement of selected concepts and vice-versa. 

• Evaluation. We evaluate CGR on ten vision tasks with CLIP ViT-B/32 and on instruction tuning with Llama-3.1-8B, each with six existing adapters. # 2 Related work 

We group prior work by the space in which its constraint acts (weight space, activation space) and how SAEs are used. Each addresses catastrophic forgetting only in part, and Ta-ble 1 summarizes which mechanisms each method covers. Concurrent preprints are distinguished in App. A. 

Weight-space methods confine the update ∆W to a chosen low-rank subspace of each frozen weight. OPLoRA (Xiong and Xie 2026) projects out of the top-k sin-gular subspace of W0. LoRA-Null (Tang et al. 2026) initial-izes in the null space of calibration activations. CorDA (Yang et al. 2024) uses an input-covariance–weighted SVD. SC-LoRA (Luo et al. 2025) contrasts fine-tuning against a pre-served covariance, a formulation closely related to CorDA, and in this paper we use CorDA as a baseline for both. SAILS (Wang et al. 2025a) initializes the LoRA B factor from SAE decoder directions. Consistent with this picture, how much LoRA forgets is predicted by the principal angles between the old- and new-task gradient subspaces (Steele 2026). As Table 1 shows, every weight-channel constraint in this cluster is a hard projection. Some of these projections are continuously imposed whereas others are only done during initialization. This can be read as a jump between hard-or-nothing continual-control strategies. CGR’s confine term is a soft continual version of these projections filling the gap of previous hard-or-nothing approaches. CGR’s anchor term adds the activation channel that these methods leave uncon-strained (§3). 

Activation-space methods in PEFT has received far less attention (Wang, Gu, and Huang 2025). Rather than con-straining weights, these methods regularize internal rep-resentations via output distillation (Li and Hoiem 2018), whole-feature alignment (Kumar et al. 2022; Mukhoti et al. 2024), or all-active SAE feature matching (Zhang et al. 2026). Beyond alignment, gradient projection techniques (Yan et al. 2025; Imanov 2026) isolate updates for specialized tasks. However, these existing anchors operate indiscriminately on raw hidden states or full feature maps, with no mechanism to select or name which concepts to preserve. In contrast, CGR anchors a selected, interpretable subset of named concepts. 

Sparse autoencoders decompose the residual stream into a dictionary of named concept directions (Bricken et al. 2023). Most work reads the dictionary to understand, steer, or edit a frozen model (Marks et al. 2025; Chalnev, Siu, and Conmy 2024), or adapts the model around an SAE integrated into the forward path (Chen, Engels, and Tegmark 2025). A few recent works bring the dictionary into the fine-tuning objective. SASFT (Deng et al. 2026) suppresses selected fea-tures during supervised fine-tuning, and SSPU (Wang et al. 2025b) unlearns target knowledge. SAE-FD (Zhang et al. 2026) distills all active features under LoRA in continual learning, and we evaluate it as a control (§4). CWAC (Peng et al. 2026) couples a hard weight projection with an SAE la-tent anchor to preserve safety concepts under full fine-tuning. CGR instead preserves pretrained knowledge under a PEFT adapter, splits one frozen dictionary into disjoint task and re-tention concept roles, and bounds the retention concept drift with its anchor term. 

# 3 Method 

Concept-Guided Retention (CGR) introduces two carefully designed regularizers for adapter base, both read from a sin-gle frozen sparse autoencoder (Figure 1). A confine term that keeps the low-rank weight update inside the task -concept subspace. An anchor term that holds the disjoint retention -concept activations to the frozen-model activations. The an-chor equals the retained concepts’ expected squared drift. We show that this drift is bounded between two weight-space quantities (Prop. 2). The lower side shows that a weight-subspace constraint alone carries no retention guarantee, and the upper side bounds the drift from the weights alone. CGR minimizes the task’s fine-tuning loss Ltask (the classification loss for vision, the language-modelling loss for text) together with the two auxiliary penalties, 

L = Ltask + λconf Lconf + λanc Lanc (1) with coefficients λconf , λ anc ≥ 0.CGR makes deliberate design choices. We constrain the weight space and the activation space through two disjoint concept sets (§3.3). We confine the weight update with a con-tinual soft projection instead of hard projection (§3.4). We anchor the retention concepts’ activations because weight-space control alone does not constrain their drift(§3.5). Both constraints act in a named concept basis, so the adapted fea-tures remain interpretable after fine-tuning.

3.2 Reading concepts 

In a transformer, the residual stream is a shared vector that every component writes into and later components read from (Elhage et al. 2021). At a read-out point in each layer, the frozen model produces a residual stream vector that we denote h0(x) ∈ Rd (d = dout ). Here, x ∈ Rdin is an internal representation from earlier layers (different from the raw input) entering the last adapted weight matrix W0.Fine-tuning adds the adapter’s contribution to this vector, 

hft (x) = h0(x) + ∆ W x . Everything computed before is preserved in h0(x), and only ∆W x is new. This holds ex-actly when W0 writes directly into the stream and to first order otherwise. CGR works by limiting the effect of this added term at the read-out points. However, to define the concepts, we use an SAE that was pre-trained at a specific 

read-out point on the residual stream of a fixed layer ℓ. CGR reads how strongly each concept fires. Each concept j ∈ S

3.3 Concept selection: task vs. retention sets 

Concept selection is key to CGR. First, CGR selects a task set Stsk that contains the concepts the fine-tuning task drives most strongly. It confines the weight update to the subspace these concepts span. Second, CGR selects a retention set 

Sret that contains the off-task general-purpose concepts on the weight update path. CGR anchors the activations of these concepts to those of the frozen model because their activation drift drives forgetting most. Both sets are read from the task’s own inputs Dtask on the frozen model, with no held-out data. 

Task set. An unconstrained update may write along off-task directions and cost the pretrained model its retention. The confined update must still learn the task so the allowed subspace must span the directions that causally drive the task loss. Eq (10) provides lower and upper bounds on the retention-concepts drift. The lower bound indicates that if a subset of the task data activates a retention concept, then move-ment restricted to the task-weight subspace may still amplify the drift of that concept. Although concepts correspond to separate coordinates in the latent space of the SAE, their encoder and decoder directions are not generally orthogonal when pulled back to the residual stream or weight spaces. This overlap is understandable because the retention con-cepts are themselves active on part of the task data. In this setting, the non-orthogonality may be advantageous: directly controlling retention-concept drift can steer movement to-ward task-relevant directions that learn the new task without unnecessarily disrupting the remaining concepts. CWAC (Peng et al. 2026) introduced the channel coupling via a first-order argument for full fine-tuning in safety align-ment tasks. Prop. 2 strengthens it for the PEFT with exact bounds, and shows that confined updates can amplify drift. We anchor a disjoint off-task set (§3.3) rather than one pro-tected set. # 4 Experiments 

For evaluation, we ask two questions. (i) Does adding CGR improve retention over the weight-only subspace-LoRA adapters? (ii) Does the recipe transfer beyond vision? We evaluate on target-task accuracy and zero-shot retention on the held-out probes after fine-tuning. 

4.1 Setup 

Backbones and SAEs. For vision, we adapt a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 and read concepts from the frozen, publicly re-leased Prisma SAE (Joseph et al. 2025) on the residual stream at layer ℓ=8 . For language, we adapt Llama-3.1-8B and read concepts from the frozen Llama-Scope SAE on the residual stream at layer 19 of 32 (He et al. 2024). Both are inter-mediate read-out points, where semantic features are most accessible (Templeton et al. 2024; Lad et al. 2024), before the final layers specialize toward the output (Gandelsman, Efros, and Steinhardt 2024). The layer is fixed per modality, never tuned. App. L reports reconstruction faithfulness at the vision read-out layer for every task. Both SAEs stay frozen and CGR only reads from them, with |S tsk |=|S ret |=128 for CLIP and |S tsk |=256 , |S ret |=128 for Llama-3.1-8B. Neither size is tuned, and App. K shows the effect of concept-set size (Tables 15 and 16). 

Baselines. We evaluate CGR on six adapter families. Each base runs alone and as “base +CGR”, so every comparison is a controlled ablation of the CGR terms with the adapter held fixed. LoRA (Hu et al. 2022) and the parallel-adapter Adapt-Former (Chen et al. 2022) are retention-agnostic bases. 

OPLoRA (Xiong and Xie 2026), LoRA-Null (Tang et al. 2026), CorDA (Yang et al. 2024), and SAILS (Wang et al. 2025a) are weight-geometry retention methods that together cover the family’s subspace constructions (top-k SVD, ac-tivation null space, input covariance, and SAE directions). Anchors on the activation channel are compared in the abla-tion of §4.3. App. B.1 describes each base. 

Protocol. Vision methods fine-tune on ten classification tasks, eight from the VTAB-1k benchmark (Zhai et al. 2019) and the two fine-grained tasks FGVC-Aircraft (Maji et al. 2013) and CUB-200 (Wah et al. 2011). The language ex-periment follows the instruction-tuning forgetting protocol of Tang et al. The selected concept set also outperforms anchoring ev-ery active concept of (Zhang et al. 2026; Peng et al. 2026) and distilling the whole representation of (Mukhoti et al. 2024) on both downstream accuracy and retention. Cru-cially, retention performance is not dictated merely by the total magnitude of anchored activation: our selected set an-chors only ∼53% of the total concept activation yet achieves the highest retention. Anchoring the entire representation re-tains less, while anchoring an equivalent activation mass on lower-scoring concepts performs no better than unselective anchoring. Retention-concept drift correlates with held-out retention across every run (Figure 3, App. F). This ordering holds consistently on the language track (Table 6), confirm-ing that the SAE provides tangible value through targeted selection and interpretability (§4.3). 

Interpretability. Because CGR’s subspaces come from a frozen SAE rather than opaque singular vectors or an activa-tion null space, the protected retention directions are named 

and manipulable . Figure 4 shows the mechanism on FGVC and the weight-side complement is in App. H. Removing the selected concepts costs ∼10 pp of zero-shot retention while a size-matched random set costs ∼0pp (App. G). Steering them moves retention smoothly with the intervention strength. Editing them through a concept-bottleneck head (Koh et al. 2020) corrects predictions on demand. We discuss each in detail in App. H.

Two concurrent preprints overlap parts of CGR’s design. 

SAE-FT (Morelli et al. 2026) penalizes the addition and removal of SAE features for robust fine-tuning, on a sin-gle feature set in the activation channel alone. PRISM (Lin and Sun 2026) bounds a cross-entropy risk gap by whole-representation drift, without concept selection or a weight-space certificate. # B Experimental Details 

B.1 Baselines 

LoRA (Hu et al. 2022) learns a low-rank update ∆W =BA 

on frozen pretrained weights. It places no constraint on where the update acts, so it is a retention-agnostic base. 

AdaptFormer (Chen et al. 2022) inserts a parallel bottle-neck adapter (down-projection, nonlinearity, up-projection) beside the MLP block. It is the one non-LoRA adapter in the panel and is likewise retention-agnostic. 

OPLoRA (Xiong and Xie 2026) applies a double-sided projection that keeps the update out of the top-k singular subspace of the pretrained weight, which leaves the dominant spectral directions of the base model untouched. 

LoRA-Null (Tang et al. 2026) initializes the adapter in the null space of calibration activations, so the update starts with no effect on the calibration inputs. 

CorDA (Yang et al. 2024) builds the adapter from an input-covariance weighted SVD of the pretrained weight. The covariance comes from general data in its knowledge-preserving mode and from task data in its instruction-preserving mode. 

SAILS (Wang et al. 2025a) initializes the LoRA B factor from SAE decoder directions, which starts the update in an interpretable concept basis. We do not include Wise-FT (Wortsman et al. 2022). It leaves fine-tuning unconstrained and interpolates the fine-tuned weights back toward the pre-trained ones afterward, so it composes with any of the methods we compare and leaves the ranking between them unchanged (Mukhoti et al. 2024). 

B.2 Datasets 

Table 7 lists the vision datasets with their class counts. The panel spans fine-grained recognition, textures, natural cat-egories, remote sensing, medical imaging, and structured pose and digit recognition. Eight tasks (DTD, Flowers-102, Oxford-Pets, EuroSAT, RESISC45, Diabetic Retinopathy, dSprites-Orientation, SVHN) come from the VTAB bench-mark (Zhai et al. 2019) and train on 1000 examples each under its VTAB-1k protocol. The two fine-grained tasks, FGVC-Aircraft (Maji et al. 2013) and CUB-200 (Wah et al. 2011), train on their full training sets. Model selection is based on the best validation accuracy on a held-out 20% 

split of the training budget. Retention is measured after fine-tuning as zero-shot accuracy on four held-out natural probes, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 (Krizhevsky 2009), Caltech-101 (Fei-Fei, Fergus, and Perona 2004), and SUN397 (Xiao et al. 2010). No probe is ever a fine-tuning task.

# E Small experimental exploration of relation between weight and activation concept space 

We want to compare the change in weight direction (in the residual stream) for each concept to their respecting concept drift in the SAE. To do that we will compare two quantities for each concept j, first the total weight change ∥W Tdec [: ,j ]∆W ∥

and second the concept drift Ex|fj (hf t ) − fj (h0)|.In Figure 6, we divide the concepts into task and retention and plot the weight change and concept drift for several strategies. We do that for the LoRA baseline and then add five other options: Confine only(in task set), Anchor only(in ret set), Anchor all(task and ret), CGR(Anchor ret and confine task), and Anchor all(task and ret sets) and Confine(task set). In terms of task accuracy and retention, the full CGR method is the best option. However, the interesting part is how different strategies control the weights and concept drifts. The Anchor term seems to reduce the concept drift not only in the portion it is applied to but even when applied only to the retention concepts; it helps reduce the drift in the task concepts as well. The confine term seems to always accelerate the change in weights on the task set and reduce the weight change in the retention set; nevertheless, it is unable to control the concept drift in retention. This indicates that both terms are needed. Moreover, anchoring every concept seems to be working against the new task accuracy when compared with only anchoring the retention set, both with or without confine. In an informal way, one could think that the retention concepts are not only helping in retention but also act as sanity checks; the machine changes the internal representations in weight space for the new task, but every time it does it has to pass these checks. This, at the same time, keeps the main concept drift low. For example, if we want to improve our understanding of an elephant, we can change our representation of an elephant, but it still has to be an animal and have four legs.

# G Causal validation of the selection 

The previous section shows the selected concepts correlate with retention. Here we test whether they carry it. In each trained model, we zero the retention concepts Sret at the read-out layer and re-measure zero-shot retention. The con-trol repeats the deletion on a size-matched random set, drawn only from concepts that fire on the evaluation data, since re-moving inactive dictionary entries would leave retention un-changed by construction. Removing the selected set destroys a large share of retained zero-shot accuracy, while removing the random set leaves retention unchanged (Fig. 9). A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test over all FGVC run-probe pairs separates the two conditions at p = 1 .7 × 10 −13 , and the same gap holds on CUB-200 and DTD. Retained capability therefore flows through the concepts the anchor holds, so the drift it measures belongs to the mechanism rather than to a correlated quantity.

is the symmetric untuned choice and sits in the stable middle of the range on both datasets. 

# L SAE faithfulness across VTAB 

Table 12 reports the in-pipeline layer-8 reconstruction EV of the Prisma SAE on 1000 samples per task used in this paper (frozen backbone, no training). EV spans 0.87 (Caltech-101) down to 0.23 (dSprites). Natural-image tasks are high-EV (the natural-image SAE is in-distribution, so its concepts are faithful) while structured/synthetic tasks are low-EV (out-of-distribution), where the concept channel is expected to weaken. All the mechanism studies above sit in the high-EV natural regime; the low-EV tasks map the boundary of the SAE-concept approach. (Notably the +CGR retention gain 

tracks forgetting pressure rather than EV directly, and EV is anti-correlated with that pressure across the natural tasks, so we do not read EV as a direct cause of the gain.)
