## ABSTRACT 

This paper investigates stochastic multi-level optimization where the objective is a nested composition of several smooth non-convex functions. We assume that only stochastic estimates of the gradient and function values for each level are accessible. Consequently, obtaining an accurate estimate of the overall gradient is challenging due to the nested structure. To address this, we employ a momentum-based estimator with mini-batches to track the function values of each level, which are subsequently used to construct momentum gradient estimators. We establish an optimal sample complexity of O(ϵ−4) for finding an ϵ-stationary point, avoiding the stronger average smoothness assumption commonly relied upon in prior literature. Furthermore, by employing a normalization technique, we attain the same rate without requiring problem-dependent constants to set hyperparameters. To achieve the optimal rate without mini-batches, we further develop a batch-free method that incorporates a first-order approximation and a clipping technique for function value estimation. Finally, we validate the effectiveness of our proposed methods through experiments on risk-averse portfolio optimization and hierarchical tilted empirical risk minimization.

In this paper, we study stochastic multi-level compositional optimization (Yang et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2021; Zhang & Xiao, 2021; Balasubramanian et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2025) of the form

We assume that only unbiased stochastic estimates are available for the function values and gradients at each level i,denoted as fi(·; ξi) and ∇fi(·; ξi), such that Eξi [fi(·; ξi)] = fi(·) and Eξi [∇fi(·; ξi)] = ∇fi(·).Multi-level optimization enjoys broad applications in machine learning, including risk-averse op-timization (Bruno et al., 2016; Shapiro et al., 2021), reinforcement learning (Dann et al., 2014), model-agnostic meta-learning (Ji et al., 2020), graph neural network training (Cong et al., 2020), stochastic variational inequalities (Ruszczy´ nski, 2021), adversarial attacks and defenses in deep neural networks (Yuan et al., 2019), risk management (Cole et al., 2017; Dentcheva et al., 2017), and hierarchical tilted empirical risk minimization (Li et al., 2021; 2023). Unlike traditional single-level stochastic optimization, where unbiased stochastic gradient evaluations are readily available, obtaining an unbiased estimate of the overall gradient and function value is generally infeasible in multi-level problems. This difficulty stems from the nested composition structure, specifically: 

EξT ,··· ,ξ 2,ξ 1 [fT (· · · f2(f1(x; ξ1); ξ2) · · · ; ξT )] ̸ = EξT [fT (· · · Eξ2 [f2(Eξ1 [f1(x; ξ1)]; ξ2)] · · · ; ξT )] .

Furthermore, estimation errors can accumulate as the composition depth increases, making the problem more challenging. To improve sample complexity, existing literature often imposes additional 

average smoothness and average Lipschitz conditions (Chen et al., 2021; Zhang & Xiao, 2021; Jiang et al., 2025), which require that stochastic estimates of each layer be smooth and Lipschitz continuous. 1arXiv:2610.01572v1 [math.OC] 1 Oct 2026 However, in single-level stochastic optimization, it is well known that momentum-based methods can achieve the optimal sample complexity of O(ϵ−4) under standard smoothness assumptions (rather than the stronger average smoothness condition). This motivates the following question: Is it possible to obtain the optimal rate in stochastic multi-level optimization without these additional assumptions? 

In this work, we answer this question affirmatively by proving the optimal O(ϵ−4) sample complexity using a simple momentum-based approach with mini-batches. Our analysis avoids the restrictive average smoothness and Lipschitz continuous conditions. Furthermore, we provide a normalized variant that eliminates the need for prior knowledge of problem-dependent constants when setting hyperparameters. Finally, to avoid large batches, we develop a batch-free method that leverages first-order approximation together with clipping, preserving the optimal sample complexity with a constant batch size. To summarize, our main contributions are as follows: • Our proposed MML method uses momentum-based estimators to track function values and gradients, obtaining the optimal O(ϵ−4) sample complexity with mini-batches. Our analysis does not rely on the average smoothness and Lipschitz continuity used in previous literature. • Employing a normalization technique, our MML-v2 removes the reliance on problem-dependent constants to set hyperparameters while preserving optimal sample complexity. • To avoid large batches, the proposed MMLFA method uses a first-order approximation for function-value tracking, combined with a clipping operation to control the estimator range. This design allows us to maintain the optimal rate with a constant batch size. • We validate the effectiveness of the proposed methods through numerical experiments on risk-averse portfolio optimization and hierarchical tilted empirical risk minimization. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

In this section, we review related work on stochastic multi-level optimization and its decentral-ized/federated variants. 2.1 MULTI -LEVEL OPTIMIZATION 

Multi-level compositional optimization was first investigated by Yang et al. (2019), who developed stochastic methods based on an extrapolation-interpolation scheme. They established a sample com-plexity of O(ϵ−(7+ T )/2) for non-convex objectives and O(ϵ−(3+ T )/4) for strongly convex objectives. To further improve the convergence rate, Chen et al. (2021) proposed the stochastically corrected stochastic compositional gradient method, deriving a sample complexity of O(ϵ−4). However, their analysis requires the stronger average smoothness assumption, which is typically used to obtain improved O(ϵ−3) rates via variance reduction. Concurrently, Balasubramanian et al. (2022) obtained 

O(ϵ−6) sample complexity for a momentum-based method with mini-batches and improved the rate to O(ϵ−4) using linearized stochastic estimates. However, their analysis neglects dependency issues, rendering the results invalid (a detailed discussion is provided in the appendix). To enhance the rate under the average smoothness assumption, Zhang & Xiao (2021) introduced the normalized proximal approximate gradient method, which evaluates gradients via nested variance reduction with large batch sizes. They achieved a total sample complexity of O(ϵ−3) in the expectation setting, and 

O(n + √nϵ −2) for the finite-sum structure, where n denotes the number of component functions across all levels. Similar results were obtained by Jiang et al. (2022; 2025) using a constant batch size, further deriving optimal rates for convex and strongly convex functions. Beyond smooth non-convex settings, Ruszczy´ nski (2021) considered non-smooth non-convex multi-level optimization, assuming that functions at each level are locally Lipschitz continuous and differ-entiable in a generalized sense. For smooth and convex problems, Zhang & Lan (2025) proposed a stochastic sequential dual method, achieving a sample complexity of O(ϵ−2). Constrained multi-level optimization has also been studied recently. Xiao et al. (2022) proposed a projection-free conditional gradient-type algorithm, attaining O(ϵ−2) and O(ϵ−3) complexities for stochastic first-order and linear-minimization oracles. Following this, Jiang et al. (2024) presented a comprehensive study on projection-free methods, covering non-convex, convex, and strongly convex objectives. Recent studies have also focused on analyzing stability and generalization performance (Pan et al., 2024) and extending multi-level optimization to minimax settings (Zhang & Gao, 2025). 22.2 DECENTRALIZED /F EDERATED MULTI -LEVEL OPTIMIZATION 

Multi-level optimization has also been widely studied in decentralized and federated environments. Gao (2024b) first studied decentralized stochastic multi-level optimization, proposing two algorithms that achieve level-independent convergence rates for non-convex problems under milder conditions. For smooth and strongly convex objectives, Yang & Li (2024) proposed the federated stochastic compositional gradient extrapolation method. Based on a double-loop strategy, this method attains an optimal O(log(1 /ϵ )) communication complexity while maintaining an almost optimal eO(1 /ϵ ) sample complexity. To address imbalanced data classification in federated learning, Zhang et al. (2024) developed a federated multi-level compositional minimax algorithm for AUC maximization, equipped with rigorous theoretical guarantees. To achieve linear speedup under heterogeneous settings, Gao (2024a) developed a federated stochastic multi-level algorithm based on a Jacobian-vector product estimator and proved level-independent convergence for non-convex objectives.

## 5 EXPERIMENTS 

In this section, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed methods on mean-deviation risk-averse portfolio optimization and hierarchical tilted empirical risk minimization. We compare our methods with existing stochastic multi-level algorithms, including A-TSCGD (Yang et al., 2019), NLASG (Balasubramanian et al., 2022), Nested-SPIDER (Zhang & Xiao, 2021), and SCSC (Chen et al., 2021). Hyperparameters for each method are selected via grid search: the momentum parameter is tuned over the set {0.01 , 0.05 , 0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 0.95 , 0.99 } and the learning rate is searched from the set {1, 10 −1, 10 −2, 10 −3, 10 −4}. We report the best results obtained from the grid search for each method. If the optimal hyperparameter lies on the boundary of the grid, we extend the search range to ensure it lies within the interior. We set the batch size to 128 for each method. For the clipping threshold C, we observe that a sufficiently large value works well in practice, avoiding the need for tuning. MML and MMLFA perform similarly overall: MMLFA performs slightly better on Industry-10/12, while MML is slightly better on Industry-17. Note that although MML requires large batch sizes in the theoretical analysis, we do not observe significant performance degradation in the experiments when using smaller batches. In Appendix K, we validate the performance with different batch sizes, i.e., {48 , 64 , 96 , 128 }. As shown in Figure 3, we do not observe significant performance degradation with smaller batches, demonstrating the practical robustness of MML with small batches. Overall, we would recommend using MML first, since it is simpler and therefore easier and more stable to implement. When the batch size is extremely small, MMLFA may be preferable. We further study the effect of the clipping threshold C in MMLFA. Theoretically, choosing C above the true upper bound of the relevant function values does not change the order of the convergence rate. Here, we vary C within the range {0.5, 1, 5, 10 , 10 20 }, with results depicted in Figure 4 in Appendix K. The results indicate that performance is stable provided C is not too small (e.g., C ≥ 1). In contrast, overly aggressive clipping (e.g., C = 0 .5) degrades performance, suggesting that a large default value of C is reasonable in practice. 5.2 HIERARCHICAL TILTED EMPIRICAL RISK MINIMIZATION

## 6 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we addressed stochastic multi-level optimization by employing momentum-based estimators to track function values and gradients. With mini-batches, the resulting method achieves 9the optimal sample complexity O(ϵ−4) under standard smoothness assumptions, without requiring the stronger average smoothness conditions used in prior work. We further introduced a normalized variant that preserves the same rate while avoiding dependence on problem-specific constants to set hyperparameters. To eliminate large batches, we developed a batch-free approach based on first-order approximation and clipping, which retains the optimal sample complexity with a constant batch size. Experiments on risk-averse portfolio optimization and hierarchical tilted empirical risk minimization further demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods.
