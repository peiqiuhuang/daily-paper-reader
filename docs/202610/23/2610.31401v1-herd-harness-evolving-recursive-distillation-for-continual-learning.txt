# HERD: Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation for Continual Learning 

Xun Sun 1 Thanh Thi Nguyen 2 Thuy-Trang Vu 1

Abstract 

Continual learning requires a model to keep acquiring new skills and knowledge without compromising its existing capabilities. Para-metric methods suffer from catastrophic for-getting and loss of plasticity. Non-parametric methods, such as prompts, skills, and harnesses, leverage in-context learning but are limited by context length and lossy plain-text compres-sion. To effectively combine their complemen-tary strengths, we propose HERD (Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation), a hybrid con-tinual learning framework that learns efficiently in the harness and internalizes experience into cumulative capabilities via distillation. On the harness side, HERD continually refines its skills to capture reusable patterns, while retain-ing high-residual examples that the skills fail to capture in a bounded memory. On the pa-rameter side, HERD performs drift-constrained distillation with dual anchors, substantially re-ducing forgetting over long recursive runs. On the 25-stage task stream of TRACE+17, under fixed memory and compute budgets, HERD matches the strongest parametric baseline in new-task acquisition while exhibiting the least forgetting and the strongest retention of general capabilities. 

1 Introduction 

Large language models (LLMs) are highly capable, yet cannot independently carry out much of the work people need done (Mazeika et al., 2025). One pressing problem in closing this gap is continual learning: humans keep learning from experience while retaining what they know, whereas models no longer change once deployed. Continual learn-ing (CL) seeks to enable this ability by learning from a stream of tasks without forgetting existing capabilities or retraining from scratch on earlier data (Wu et al., 2024).

Parametric methods, however, inevitably en-counter catastrophic forgetting (McCloskey and Cohen, 1989; Kirkpatrick et al., 2017; Huang et al., 2024) and loss of plasticity (Dohare et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2026). They also give little control over what is internalized: raw experience carries noise, duplicates, and conflicts alongside the few samples that matter. In-context learning (ICL) offers a non-parametric path to continual learning (Brown et al., 2020; Gutiérrez et al., 2025). New knowledge is stored in external memory and retrieved into context, so a frozen LLM learns across tasks (Shi et al., 2025). This appears to sidestep the flaws above: a frozen model forgets nothing, loses no plasticity, and keeps knowledge inspectable and editable. It is also the norm in practice: users write prompts, skills, and harnesses rather than post-train, and recent works build continual learning systems on harnesses (Zhang et al., 2026c; Kang et al., 2026). Could a harness alone, recursively compressing its context, solve continual learning once models are strong and context windows long enough? We argue that it cannot. External stores can hold unbounded data, but nothing accumulates in the model itself: whatever it acts on must pass through the context, so experience is compressed into bounded plain text, which loses information at a very high rate. The more experience accumulates, the more is lost. The resolution lies in the weights themselves. A few hundred gigabytes of parameters recall his-tory, law, medicine, and science (Allen-Zhu and Li, 2025; Morris et al., 2026; Roberts et al., 2020), or even reproduce entire books nearly verbatim (Cooper et al., 2026). Language models are general-purpose compressors (Delétang et al., 2024), and compression is intelligence (Hutter, 2005). Contin-ual learning therefore still relies on weight updates, but should not apply them to every example. Each layer should store what it is best suited for: capacity in the weights, selection in the harness. The design mirrors the human brain: a harness resembles episodic memory formed during the day, flexible but limited in capacity, which sleep consolidates into long-term memory (McClelland et al., 1995). HERD likewise internalizes its har-ness into the weights after every stage, under fixed memory and compute budgets and at no added in-ference cost. It does not eliminate forgetting, but preserves existing capabilities as far as possible while the model keeps learning. HERD is designed around recursion: it must run stably stage after stage, which raises two chal-lenges. First, the context is bounded, so the har-ness must decide what to change and what to keep. Second, recursive training drifts, and unchecked drift ends in collapse. HERD answers each with one module (Figure 1). For the first, the harness evolves with the task stream: the model iterates on its skills to abstract reusable rules, and retains in a bounded memory the high-residual samples the skills cannot cover. For the second, HERD performs drift-constrained distillation with dual anchors. The student is the previous checkpoint trained to match itself under the harness, which shifts the model less than SFT and thus forgets less (Shenfeld et al., 2026, 2025). The previous check-point bounds per-stage drift and the base model bounds cumulative drift. The harness is kept, in-spectable and editable, for the next stage; only the weights are deployed. Across a 25-stage stream HERD keeps more of what it learns than any other method: its final mean over all seen tasks is 0.706, above every parametric baseline and 27 points above the non-parametric ones, while its acquisition, 0.713, is statistically in-distinguishable from the plasticity floor set by Seq-LoRA (Table 1). Forgetting is 0.010, about half or less that of every weight-updating baseline but SDFT, whose generation collapses (Appendix D); the general test falls 6.3 points where these other baselines lose 20.0 to 25.5. In Figure 1, the rule learned at stage 5 still holds in the weights alone at stage 12 across three seeds. Our contributions are: (1) HERD, a hybrid con-tinual learning system that evolves a harness of skills and high-residual memory and recursively internalizes it into the weights (§2); (2) drift-constrained distillation with dual anchors, where the previous checkpoint bounds per-stage drift and the base model bounds cumulative drift (§2.4); (3) an evaluation on TRACE+17, a 25-stage stream of eight TRACE tasks (Wang et al., 2023b) and 17 Super-NaturalInstructions tasks (Wang et al., 2022), against parametric, non-parametric, and hy-brid baselines (§3). Our code and dataset are available at https:// github.com/a1exsun/HERD , and the project page is at https://a1exsun.github.io/HERD .

Harness representation. We use harness in the sense of DeepSeek Harness (DSH) (DeepSeek-AI, 2026) and of harness-level continual learning (Kang et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a): the state and procedures around a model at inference, such as system prompts, skills, memories, and tools, as opposed to its weights. The harness of HERD is 

H = ( K, R). K is a registry of skills, each a rout-ing prefix and instructions; an input x is routed by prefix, and the matched instructions K(x) be-come the system message. R is a residual memory of whole training records, prompt and verified an-swer, kept verbatim. The budgets are BK and BR,with BM = BK + BR. Executing the harness is f (x; θ, K ): the model θ answering x under the system message K(x).

In this notation, HERD after stage t is Lt =(f¯θt , (Kt, Rt, G )) : merged dense weights ¯θt, the harness Ht = ( Kt, Rt), and a fixed set G of gen-eral probes reserved before the stream. 3 Experiments 

Our experiments evaluate HERD under a unified protocol to answer three questions. Q1. Does HERD outperform purely parametric and purely non-parametric methods? Q2. As the number of CL stages grows, does HERD maintain long-horizon stability? Q3. Are harness evolution, high-residual memory, and the two drift anchors all in-dispensable? 

3.1 Experimental Settings Benchmark. TRACE+17 appends 17 Super-NaturalInstructions tasks (Wang et al., 2022) to the eight TRACE tasks (Wang et al., 2023b), because eight stages are too few to tell stability from slow collapse. Each of the 25 tasks has 500 training, 100 validation, and 100 test examples; no prompt appears in more than one task or split. A G-test of five categories, 200 held-out items each, tracks general capability: knowledge, mathematics, com-monsense, instruction following, and multilingual understanding (MMLU, GSM8K, HellaSwag, IFE-val, XCOPA; Hendrycks et al., 2020; Cobbe et al., 2021; Zellers et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2023; Ponti et al., 2020). All methods start from Qwen3.5-4B with the same decoding, length limits, and scorers; all weight updates share one budget, 32 updates of 32 records per stage. After every stage we score the current task; after stages 4, 8, 16, and 25 we also score all seen tasks and the G-test (Appendix B). 

Baselines. Non-parametric : ACE (Zhang et al., 2026c) grows a playbook; HCL (Kang et al., 2026) keeps a harness edit only if it improves the cur-rent task and breaks no retained example; both attach the harness to every prompt at inference. 

Parametric : Seq-LoRA trains one adapter through the stream and serves as the floor; uniform re-play keeps a memory the size of HERD’s residual memory, filled by uniform sampling; OPR (Chen et al., 2026) replays the model’s own best earlier an-swers; TreeLoRA (Qian et al., 2025) and MoRAM (Lu et al., 2026) isolate updates, in a gradient-similarity tree and in rank-1 experts; SDFT (Shen-feld et al., 2026) self-distills from a demonstration-conditioned teacher, without a harness. Hybrid :OPHSD (Zhao et al., 2026) distills a fixed harness into the weights and deploys without it; the harness never changes and drift is not budgeted. Controls :the base model as is, and the same model under a fixed harness that never learns. 

Metrics. Acquisition, the mean of Rj,j , is each task’s score right after it is learned; AA is what remains after stage 25; F is what is lost in between (Eq. 1). We report both the mean change over the five G-test categories, ∆C, and the worst, ∆Cmin ,because a collapse in one category can hide be-hind a mean of five (§2.4). All numbers are means over three seeds, with standard deviations and 95% bootstrap intervals over items.

Cost. A HERD stage runs a fixed number of up-dates and probes, so its training time tracks answer length, not the stage index: 316 s at stage 1, 284 s at stage 25, 10,723 s in total against Seq-LoRA’s 10,463 s. OPHSD, which regenerates its teacher context by rollouts at every stage, needs 659,000 s; ACE pays its 15,410 tokens at every inference. 

Long-horizon stability. On the first eight tasks (Figure 3a), HERD’s mean stays within half a point of its stage-8 level throughout, from 0.664 at stage 8 to 0.665 at stage 25. Every parametric baseline moves more: Seq-LoRA falls to 0.372 at stage 12 before recovering to 0.637, TreeLoRA to 0.593, and OPR and MoRAM decline to 0.621 and 0.625. On the G-test, the parametric baselines take most of their loss by stage 4 or 8 and then plateau, while HERD loses 1.5 points by stage 4 and 6.3 by stage 25. The two recursive self-distillation baselines instead drift further at every checkpoint. OPHSD’s G-test falls through 0.741, 0.661, 0.613, and 0.582 at stages 4, 8, 16, and 25, and its first eight tasks from 0.524 to 0.506. 4 Related Work 

Parametric continual learning. Surveys group LLM continual learning by training stage (Wu et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025); we group it by where expe-rience is kept. Structural methods isolate task up-dates in low-rank subspaces, adapter trees, per-task experts, or retained prompts and modules (Wang et al., 2023a; Yang et al., 2026; Qian et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2026; Razdaibiedina et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). Replay methods synthesize or select the data mixed into training (Huang et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2026; Feng et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026). Self-supervised methods write their own fine-tuning data (Zweiger et al., 2025), distill from a demonstration-conditioned self (Shenfeld et al., 2026), or consolidate fast memories into stable weights (Behrouz et al., 2026). Other work finds part of measured forgetting spurious (Zheng et al., 2025), consolidates in weight space (Cho et al., 2026), or reports limits of on-policy self-distillation (Wang et al., 2026a). HERD also updates low-rank factors by self-distillation, but a harness decides what to internalize and drift budgets bound each step. 

Non-parametric continual learning. Non-parametric methods adapt an external memory, context, or harness around a fixed model, where the stability–plasticity tension reappears through retrieval (Gutiérrez et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026). Recent systems consolidate memory banks (Ye et al., 2026a), evolve contexts into playbooks (Zhang et al., 2026c), gate harness edits on improvement and retention checks (Kang et al., 2026), or refine harnesses over task streams (Wei et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026). All pass experience through a bounded context, so nothing accumulates in the model itself (§1). HERD keeps such a harness as its fast layer but internalizes it into the weights after every stage. 

Hybrid continual learning. A few methods com-bine the two. OEL consolidates deployment expe-rience online (Ye et al., 2026b), Continual Harness updates the model from teacher-relabeled harness rollouts (Karten et al., 2026), and EvoSC consoli-dates trajectories into soft prompts (Yu et al., 2026). HERD keeps only skills and the high-residual sam-ples they miss, and deploys the weights alone. 

Distillation and harness adaptation. Context distillation internalizes instructions and examples into the weights (Snell et al., 2022). On-policy vari-ants condition the teacher on experiential knowl-edge (Ye et al., 2026c) or on a harness (Zhao et al., 2026), and RL’s Razor ties forgetting to the distri-butional shift that on-policy training keeps small (Shenfeld et al., 2025). HERD’s privileged teacher is of this kind, but it keeps the harness across stages where OPHSD discards it, bounds drift explicitly, and scores verified answer prefixes instead of stu-dent rollouts. Self-Harness accepts edits only af-ter regression testing on fixed benchmarks (Zhang et al., 2026a); HERD’s commit gate is a simpler version of the same check (§2.3). Two evaluations separate updating a harness from benefiting from it (Lin et al., 2026) and ask whether optimizer gains compound over a continual stream (Wang et al., 2026b). 

5 Conclusion 

We presented HERD, a hybrid continual learning system that learns in the harness and internalizes it into the weights. The harness evolves with the stream, iterating on its skills to abstract reusable patterns and keeping the high-residual samples they do not cover in a bounded memory. Distillation then internalizes the harness under drift constraints with two anchors, the previous checkpoint for per-stage drift and the base model for cumulative drift; only the weights are deployed. On the 25-stage TRACE+17 stream, HERD matches the strongest parametric baseline in acquisition, forgets the least among weight-updating methods that avoid col-lapse, and loses the least general capability. Re-moving any part costs acquisition, retention, or general capability, and attaching the final harness at inference barely changes the scores. 

Limitations 

Our empirical validation is currently scoped to one base model and one 25-stage stream rather than a large-scale sweep. HERD also relies on a verified 8answer y∗ for every training record and assumes a stream with known task boundaries, one task per stage, and no revisits. A detailed discussion regarding these boundaries and the corresponding plans is provided in Appendix E.

B Experiment Details 

TRACE+17 is built once, with seed 42, from pinned revisions of the TRACE and Super-NaturalInstructions repositories: the eight TRACE tasks in their canonical order, then SuperNI tasks 024, 074, 195, 511, 1219, 574, 192, 177, 064, 346, 108, 111, 1415, 1447, 828, 1728, and 217. Prompts are deduplicated and the splits drawn in a seeded hash order. Each task keeps its official metric: exact match, leading-choice accuracy, ROUGE-L (Lin, 2004), edit similarity, or SARI (Xu et al., 2016). Validation targets and test records are hid-den from the model, and no test score selects a can-didate or hyperparameter. The G-test draws from pinned snapshots of MMLU, GSM8K, HellaSwag, IFEval, and XCOPA, disjoint from the stream: 200 test items per category, a 40-item probe nested in them, and a separate 40-item development panel. Stage 0 is the base model. After stage t we score the current task; at stages 4, 8, 16, and 25 every seen task and the full G-test; at stages 12 and 20 tasks 1 to 8; at every other stage the 200-item probe. Each cell uses all 100 test items, decoded greedily with thinking off, at most 5,120 new tokens, and 16,384 input tokens; an input over the ceiling counts as an empty prediction. LoRA baselines decode merged checkpoints with vLLM 0.31.0 (Kwon et al., 2023); HERD and MoRAM decode with Transformers; frozen-model methods use MLX (Hannun et al., 2023), PyTorch’s MPS backend, 2 or vLLM servers.

15 GSM8K is scored on the final number, IFEval by its strict verifier, and the choice categories by one extractor that reads the last explicit answer. Every weight-updating method trains for 32 up-dates of 32 records per stage on sequences of at most 16,384 tokens; longer records are excluded, not truncated. Unless the official code fixes other-wise, the adapter is a rank-32 LoRA with α = 64 

and no dropout on every linear layer of the text backbone, trained with AdamW at 10 −4 and gradi-ent clipping at 1.0. Uniform replay keeps a 32,768-token memory, 8,192 of them for 23 reserved gen-eral records, filled by uniform sampling without re-entry. OPR-RU samples one response per ear-lier training prompt at temperature 0.1 and keeps the best-scoring five per stage (replay ratio 0.01), with an equal quota per task. TreeLoRA uses the authors’ regularizer weight of 0.5. MoRAM fol-lows the official runner: eight rank-1 atoms per module per stage, α = 16 , dropout 0.1, top-8 self-activation at temperature 0.03 with an infer-ence gate of 0.2, and Adam (0 .9, 0.95) at 5 × 10 −4.SDFT trains a fresh adapter per stage on 32 of its own rollouts per update, at temperature 1 and at most 1,024 new tokens; its teacher is an EMA copy (0.01) of the stage-start weights shown the reference under the official thinking-process tem-plate. OPHSD uses the official draft-verify harness on the six stages with at most ten labels and plan-solve elsewhere; the teacher is the stage-start model given the draft labels, or the plan and the reference answer, and the student minimizes the reverse KL on one on-policy rollout per record. The static harness wraps the frozen model in a fixed system prompt, an empty skill registry, and a Python tool in a 10-second sandbox, for at most 32 turns per item. ACE runs the official offline trainer: batch size 1, one epoch, three reflection rounds, ADD-only curation, the upstream 4,096-token call limit, and a rollback of any sample whose call ex-ceeds the input ceiling. HCL-Stable proposes one alternative per component per stage under a 12,000-token budget and commits it only on a validation gain of at least 0.01 that breaks no item of each earlier task’s 20-item validation panel. Learning methods run with seeds 42, 43, and 44 and report the mean and sample standard deviation; intervals are 95% item bootstraps with 4,000 draws, resam-pled within task and G-test category and paired between HERD and each baseline.

HERD’s failing cases. The first limitation is probe coverage. Protection follows the residual memory (Figure 9d,e): a learned task has low resid-ual, leaves the memory, and loses its checkpoint probes with it. Superni:192, copying the sentence that supports a given answer, lost its last probe at stage 17 in all three seeds; seed 42 then fell from 0.83 to 0.52 by stage 25, 70 of its 100 outputs copy-ing the answer already in the input, while seeds 43 and 44 held above 0.90. At stage 25, nine or ten of the 24 earlier tasks have no probe; a floor of probes per learned task is the obvious repair. The second limitation is skill quality. For superni:074, writing a question about a passage, seed 42’s skill demands a question and 89 of 100 outputs are questions; the skills of seeds 43 and 44 list a question under Avoid that recurs under Examples, and only 21 and 64 outputs are questions, for ROUGE-L of 0.326 and 0.349 against the base’s 0.421. The guard passed all three, as it passed all 75 commits. It checks the window’s mean residual without replaying the an-swer (Eq. 7); improvement under verified-answer prefixes does not guarantee that free generation follows the skill’s intended format. OPHSD: drift in wording. OPHSD’s outputs stay clean to the end, at most 1.3% cut off, and its gains fall on the tasks the base gets right in the wrong format, ScienceQA from 0.03 to 0.85 and NumGLUE from 0 to about 0.5. The official solve message gives the teacher a plan and a “Reference answer (for verification only)”, and the student, whose prompt holds neither, begins to cite them (Figure 9f): the share of test outputs that mention a plan or reference answer rises from 0.01 at stage 1 to 0.17 at stage 25, and to 0.84 on the mathematics items, whose outputs shorten from about 1,180 to 405 tokens. Mathematics ends at 0.24 and instruc-tion following at 0.38, against the base’s 0.89 and 0.82, with planning-style openers in about 37% of answers against 4% for the base. The harness is run by the current student and the teacher is the stage-start model, so nothing pulls back: the gen-eral probe never rises at any of the 25 stages. 

SDFT: collapse by leakage and length. SDFT’s teacher prompt shows an example and asks for “a response of your own, including the thinking pro-cess”. Here the example is a one- to three-token label, so the teacher invents a thinking process, and rollouts cut at 1,024 tokens are trained on regard-less, so stopping is never supervised. The drift compounds: test outputs cut at the 5,120-token ceiling go from 0.01 at stage 1 to 0.21, 0.74, and 0.94 at stages 4, 8, and 12, and the share that re-produces the teacher’s instruction verbatim from 0.00 to 0.23, 0.77, and 0.95. On C-STANCE item 0, stage 12 gives the letter, then “This is an ex-ample for a response to the question:” and a new thinking process, restarted until the ceiling (Fig-ure 9f). The loss gives no warning, settling at 0.005 to 0.008, because the teacher is an EMA of the stu-dent. HERD’s target is the verified answer replayed as the model’s own turn, so there is no instruction text to copy, and its KL is scored on that answer’s prefix rather than on the student’s samples, where teacher and student can agree on a degenerate path. 21 E Future Works 

Larger streams. Our experiments cover one base model and one 25-stage stream; given HERD’s for-getting of 0.010 and fixed per-stage cost (§3.2), we plan to test longer streams with more task families. 

Learning from user harnesses. Our long-term goal is a continual learner that trains recursively on a user’s own harness, the prompts, skills, and mem-ories they already write (§1), and so internalizes their organization’s procedures, domain knowledge, and codebases. 

Beyond verified answers and known boundaries. 

User harnesses supply neither verified answers nor task boundaries. To reduce the dependence on y∗,we are considering: (i) computing the residual and the guard on an accepted target other than y∗, such as a user correction, a rubric, or a majority answer from several harness rollouts; (ii) opening a stage when the mean residual of incoming records rises and closing it when it falls; and (iii) routing skills by retrieval over the record rather than by a tem-plate prefix, so that one stage may hold several tasks. 

Recursive self-improvement. Because the har-ness HERD evolves at one stage is distilled into the weights that evolve it at the next, the loop could extend to recursive self-improvement (Zhang et al., 2026b): (i) letting the current checkpoint rather than the frozen θ0 write the skill (§2.3), so that a model that has internalized one stage writes a bet-ter skill for the next; (ii) placing the drift budgets, window size, and record selection in the harness, where the model proposes changes adopted only af-ter a controlled commit; and (iii) applying the same residual test and drift audit to every self-proposed change, with rollback when a gate fails. 22
