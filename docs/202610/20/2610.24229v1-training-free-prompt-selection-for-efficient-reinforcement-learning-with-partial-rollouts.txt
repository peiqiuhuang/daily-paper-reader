Abstract 

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for training reasoning models, but its com-putational cost makes efficient data selection increasingly important. Since training exam-ples contribute unequally to learning, selecting a small, informative subset can offer a more efficient alternative to using the entire data pool. The training-free selection method (Wu et al., 2026) estimates prompt value with hid-den states from complete greedy rollouts. How-ever, in our datasets, 26 –32% of these rollouts fail to terminate within the generation budget. We introduce LIFT, a training-free data selec-tor that identifies impactful prompts using only partial reasoning rollouts. At its core, LIFT uses Partial Rollout Context Lift (PRCL), a score inspired by pointwise V-information that accumulates token-level log-likelihood ratios between a question-conditioned context and a null context with the question removed. This enables prompt scoring from a short rollout pre-fix rather than a complete trajectory. Across Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B, prefixes retaining at least 90% and 95% of the full-rollout PRCL score, respectively, require only 26% and 4.7% of the tokens gen-erated by full rollouts. Compared with SHIFT, LIFT reduces selection cost by 3.7–9.1 × while achieving competitive downstream RLVR per-formance, with the highest six-benchmark av-erage in three of six settings. 1

By avoiding training-time prompt utility estimation and reward supervision, training-free methods provide a more general way to select prompts before RL training. However, SHIFT’s full trajectory requirement can be costly when greedy decoding becomes repet-itive. On our two datasets, Math-500 (Lightman et al., 2023) and LIMR (Li et al., 2025), 26–32% of greedy trajectories fail to terminate within the generation budget, and most exhibit fixed n-gram repetition. We observed that such trajectories can spend a substantial number of tokens after the infor-mative part of the reasoning has already been gener-ated, suggesting that a complete trajectory may not be necessary to obtain an effective selection signal. We therefore ask: Under the training-free setting, can we efficiently select a high-quality subset of prompts with only a partial informative rollout? 

We answer this question by showing that effec-tive prompt selection can be performed from par-tial rollouts. First, we introduce LIFT, a training-free data selector that needs only a single, partial 

reasoning trajectory. Second, motivated by point-wise V-information(PVI) (Ethayarajh et al., 2022), which estimates example difficulty, we propose 

Partial Rollout Context Lift (PRCL). PRCL mea-sures how much the question changes the entropy of its partial reasoning trajectory compared with removing the question. We show that partial roll-outs suffice for effective training-free prompt selection : short prefixes retain over 90% of the selection signal of complete trajectories while re-quiring substantially fewer generated tokens. Third, the resulting subsets achieve competitive down-stream performance at substantially lower se-lection cost . Across models and selection budgets, PRCL-selected subsets achieve performance com-parable to full-pool training and competitive with full-trajectory selection, while reducing selection cost by a factor of 3.7–9.1×. Additionally, inspired by the effectiveness of partial rollouts, we further adapt SHIFT with an early-stop rule that halts de-coding at the onset of n-gram repetition. This cuts its selection cost by 1.5 to 2.0× at no loss in down-stream accuracy in four settings. 

2 Related Work 

RL for LLM reasoning. RL was initially used in LLM post-training for preference align-ment (Ouyang et al., 2022), optimizing the policy against a learned reward model with proximal pol-icy optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017). With automatically checkable answers, a verifier can replace the reward model; this approach is known as reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) (Lambert et al., 2025). Group rela-tive policy optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) can then drop PPO’s value network, instead esti-mating advantages by normalizing rewards within a group of rollouts per prompt. GRPO has been used to train recent reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025)). Follow-up work refin-ing the GRPO objective (Yu et al., 2025) decouples the lower and upper clipping bounds and discards prompts whose rollouts all receive the same reward. 

Data Selection for LLM Training-dependent methods use signals that emerge during training to filter prompts or rollouts online (Zheng et al., 2025; Bae et al., 2026b; Sun et al., 2025b), real-locate rollout budgets across prompts (Kim et al., 2026), or identify informative prompts from re-ward curves, historical reward variance, or gradient similarity (Li et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026b; Yang et al., 2026). Training-free methods estimate prompt utility using signals extracted from the base model. Sener and Savarese (Sener and Savarese, 2018) select rep-resentative examples by maximizing embedding-space coverage. Marion et al. (Marion et al., 2023) explore model uncertainty signals such as perplex-ity and entropy for assessing when a language model needs assistance. Li et al. (Li et al., 2026a) use the diversity of model-generated answers to guide instruction-data selection. SHIFT (Wu et al., 2026) estimates prompt utility from hidden-state shifts across a single greedy reasoning rollout and applies quality-weighted coreset selection to bal-ance prompt utility and diversity. Our method falls into this category, using only token log-probabilities from a fixed-length rollout prefix. 

3 Methodology 

Task Formulation. Given an unlabeled prompt pool U and a selection budget B, our goal is to select an impactful subset S ⊂ U of size B for downstream RLVR training. We propose a training-free data selection method LIFT that uses only partial reasoning rollouts to identify impactful prompts. First, we randomly sample a subset of the pool to estimate an appro-priate rollout prefix length. Then, we score the remaining prompts using this prefix length and se-lect the top-B prompts based on their PRCL scores. 

Setup. Let U = {xi}Ni=1 be an unlabeled pool of prompts and B ≪ N a selection budget. Let πθ be the initial policy and T (·) the chat template used for GRPO training. For each prompt x, we generate a deterministic rollout y(x) = ( y1, . . . , y Lx ) using greedy decoding under T (x). We define a null context T (∅) by removing the user prompt while retaining the same template and system prompt.

Effect of repetition. The flat tail consists largely of repeated tokens. About a fifth of greedy rollouts end in a verbatim loop (21.3% for Qwen and 22.5% for R1-Distill), and those loops account for 41.8% and 32.4% of all generated tokens while conveying very little of the score. Qwen’s mean |st| falls from 0.0998 before loop onset to 0.0012 after, since inside a loop both contexts predict each repeated token from the rollout itself. Conditioning on the problem has almost no effect. 

Model dependence. The two panels of Figure 4 (i.e., the two datasets) differ far less than the two curves within each panel (i.e., the two models). Qwen2.5-Math reaches its target share at 25.9% and 20.3% of the answer, compared to R1-Distill’s 5.1% and 6.3%, and 55.5% and 50.4% of its to-kens carry st > 0, compared to 25.8% and 25.6%. In both cases, the gap between the models is sev-eral times the gap between the two datasets of one model. The tightness of the signal concentration therefore depends on the model’s generation behav-ior rather than on the problems it is given, motivat-ing the prefix length estimation per model that we analyze in Section 5.3.

6 Conclusion 

We investigated whether training-free prompt selec-tion for RLVR requires decoding complete greedy rollouts and found that short prefixes can provide sufficient information for effective selection. We introduce LIFT, a training-free RLVR data selector that estimates prompt value from partial reasoning rollouts using a token-level information score. The core of LIFT is the Partial Rollout Context Lift (PRCL), which measures how the question context changes the log-likelihood of the opening tokens of the model’s own greedy rollout relative to a null context with the question removed. PRCL’s cumulative score allows multiple prefix lengths to be evaluated from a single decode. More-over, the prefix length can be estimated using a ran-dom 10% of the candidate pool rather than fixed in advance. By using partial rollouts, PRCL reduces dataset-level scoring cost by 3.7–9.1× relative to SHIFT. Across six benchmark and budget settings, it achieves the highest average downstream per-formance in three settings while remaining com-petitive with full trajectory selection and full pool 8training. 

7 Limitations 

Choosing the prefix length. Our prefix-length rule identifies the point at which a random γ-fraction of the dataset has accumulated a target share α of its score. Although this replaces an ab-solute token-count heuristic with a score-based cri-terion, the target share α remains model-dependent and must be calibrated for each model. We set 

α = 0 .90 for Qwen2.5-Math and α = 0 .95 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill based on their score accumu-lation curves. The estimate is also sensitive to the sampled subset: at γ = 0 .1, ˆK ranges from 224 to 496 tokens for Qwen2.5-Math and from 128 to 832 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill. Although the median matches the whole-dataset estimate, increasing γ is needed to reduce variability, leaving a more robust, model-agnostic prefix-length estimation for future work. 

Scope of the evaluation. We evaluate two 1.5B models on two mathematics datasets at selection budgets of 2% and 4%. The smallest setting se-lects seven prompts, where the differences between methods fall within the spread across training seeds. Whether the early concentration of PRCL holds at larger model scales, on other reasoning domains, or at larger budgets remains untested.
