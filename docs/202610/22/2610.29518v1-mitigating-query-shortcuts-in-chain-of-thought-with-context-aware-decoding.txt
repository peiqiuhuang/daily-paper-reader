## Mitigating Query Shortcuts in Chain-of-Thought with Context-Aware Decoding 

Shin’ya Yamaguchi 

NTT 

shinya.yamaguchi@ntt.com 

Abstract 

While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting im-proves the reasoning performance of large lan-guage models (LLMs), recent studies have shown that final answers do not always de-pend on the generated rationales. In particu-lar, LLMs may rely on query-specific shortcuts rather than the information provided by their reasoning steps. In this paper, we investigate whether context-aware decoding (CAD), an ex-isting contrastive decoding method, can miti-gate such shortcut behavior when applied to CoT reasoning. We study this application as Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), which contrasts the next-token distributions condi-tioned on the query with and without its ra-tionale, amplifying tokens whose likelihood increases due to the rationale. We provide a the-oretical interpretation of this decoding rule via a KL-regularized reward-maximization objec-tive. Experiments across five reasoning bench-marks demonstrate that RCD improves answer accuracy over standard CoT. Furthermore, in-tervention analyses using ground-truth and ran-dom rationales reveal that RCD increases the behavioral dependence of final answers on the rationale content. Our findings suggest that con-trastive decoding offers a simple, training-free approach to mitigating query shortcuts. 

1 Introduction 

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022; Kojima et al., 2022) has emerged as an ef-fective inference strategy for improving the rea-soning performance of Large Language Models (LLMs). By generating multiple intermediate rea-soning steps, or rationales, before producing a fi-nal answer, CoT enables LLMs to solve complex problems. Beyond improving task performance, these rationales are often interpreted as explana-tions of the model’s predictions. However, recent studies have revealed that generated rationales do not necessarily reflect the actual decision-making process (Turpin et al., 2023; Arcuschin et al., 2025; Lewis-Lim et al., 2025). In particular, LLMs may rely on shortcut information directly from the in-put query, even when their rationales suggest a different conclusion. Such query-shortcut behavior weakens the behavioral dependency between ratio-nales and final answers, raising concerns about the reliability of CoT. Prior research has investigated this issue by ra-tionale interventions that measure whether changes to reasoning steps affect final predictions (Lanham et al., 2023; Turpin et al., 2023) or improving ratio-nale reliability with additional supervision or ratio-nale selection (Paul et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2026). While these studies provide important insights, a complementary question is whether query-shortcut behavior can be mitigated directly during answer decoding, without modifying model parameters or employing external evaluators. In standard CoT decoding, the query and generated rationale are jointly provided as context for next-token predic-tion. However, this formulation does not explicitly distinguish the contributions of the query and the rationale, allowing the model to generate answers primarily from query-conditioned predictions. In this work, we investigate the application of Context-Aware Decoding (CAD) (Shi et al., 2024) to mitigate query-shortcut behavior in CoT. CAD is an established contrastive decoding approach orig-inally developed to prioritize contextual evidence over a model’s parametric knowledge. We examine whether the same principle can encourage LLMs to rely more heavily on self-generated rationales dur-ing decoding. Specifically, we study Rationale Con-trastive Decoding (RCD), an application of CAD in which the context part is a CoT rationale rather than an external document. RCD contrasts the next-token distributions conditioned on both the query and rationale with those conditioned only on the query. By subtracting query-only logits, this decod-ing rule increases the relative preference for tokens arXiv:2610.29518v1 [cs.CL] 21 Oct 2026 supported by the rationale, potentially suppressing query-shortcut behavior without additional training. We further interpret this established decoding for-mulation as a KL-regularized reward-maximization objective, in which the reward is the log-density ratio between the rationale-conditioned and query-only distributions. We empirically investigate RCD through com-prehensive experiments across multiple reason-ing benchmarks. First, intervention analyses with ground-truth and randomly substituted rationales demonstrate that RCD makes final predictions more sensitive to rationale content, providing be-havioral evidence of increased rationale–answer dependency. Second, RCD consistently improves answer accuracy over standard CoT across five rea-soning benchmarks and multiple model families. Third, an analysis of rationale correctness reveals that the performance gains primarily stem from cor-recting answers that fail to follow valid rationales, rather than repairing incorrect reasoning. These findings suggest that applying contrastive decoding to CoT can mitigate query-shortcut behavior and improve the utilization of generated rationales.

In fact, previous works have reported the unfaithful behavior of CoT, such as post-hoc rationalizing by biasing toward the order of multiple-choice options for the answer (Turpin et al., 2023), silent error correction, where the model implicitly fixes the computational or logical errors in the rationales due to shortcuts (Arcuschin et al., 2025). These findings indicate that standard CoT can exhibit weak or inconsistent behavioral dependence on its rationale.

4.2 Proof-of-Concept by Intervention Analysis 

We conduct an intervention analysis on GSM8K by replacing the rationale r in Eq. (6) with either ground-truth (GT) or random rationales. If a decod-ing method sensitive to rationales, GT rationales should improve performance and irrelevant random rationales should degrade it. We use the GT ratio-nales provided in GSM8K and construct random rationales by shuffling them across examples. Table 1 shows the intervention results. Stan-dard CoT improves over direct answering, but still keeps non-trivial accuracy even with random ra-tionales, suggesting that it can often ignore the given rationale and rely on shortcut biases. In con-trast, RCD achieves higher accuracy with GT ratio-nales and substantially lower accuracy with random rationales, indicating stronger behavioral depen-dence on rationale content. Notably, Logit Addi-tion degrades accuracy with both self-generated and GT rationales while retaining higher accuracy with random rationales. These contrasting results suggest that subtracting query-only logits, rather than reinforcing them, is effective in increasing rationale–answer dependency. 5 Related Work 

Recent studies have examined CoT faithfulness through rationale interventions (Lanham et al., 2023) and methods for measuring or improving rationale-answer alignment (Paul et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2026). These approaches primarily focus on faithfulness evaluation or require additional mechanisms (e.g., preference tuning and multiple-rationale evaluations), rather than directly provid-ing comparable answer-decoding baselines. In contrast, we investigate the application of exist-ing Context-Aware Decoding (Shi et al., 2024) to CoT rationales, without training or external eval-uators. We therefore focus on decoding baselines and separately examine compatibility with Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) in Appendix H. 

6 Conclusion 

In this work, we investigated the application of context-aware contrastive decoding to mitigate query-shortcut behavior in Chain-of-Thought rea-soning. By contrasting rationale-conditioned and query-only next-token distributions, Rationale Con-trastive Decoding (RCD) encourages LLMs to gen-erate answers that depend more strongly on their rationales. Our experiments demonstrate increased behavioral rationale–answer dependency and con-sistent accuracy improvements over standard CoT across multiple reasoning benchmarks. These find-ings suggest that existing contrastive decoding tech-niques can improve the utilization of CoT ratio-nales without additional training or external evalu-ators. Nevertheless, stronger rationale dependency does not guarantee faithful reasoning, particularly when the rationales themselves are incorrect. Our study thus highlights both the potential and the limitations of decoding-level interventions for miti-gating query shortcuts in CoT. Acknowledgment

Limitations 

First, although our rationale interventions show that RCD increases the behavioral dependency of final answers on the provided rationales, they do not establish that the rationales faithfully reflect the model’s internal reasoning process. In partic-ular, changes in accuracy under ground-truth or randomly shuffled rationales measure sensitivity to rationale content but do not necessarily indi-cate more correct or faithful reasoning. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as evidence of stronger rationale–answer dependency rather than a general improvement in reasoning reliability. Second, stronger rationale dependency can be detrimental when rationales are incorrect or incom-plete. Since RCD does not verify rationale cor-rectness, it may amplify erroneous reasoning while suppressing useful information from the query-only distribution. Future work could explore adaptive contrastive weighting based on rationale quality. We further discuss potential societal and deploy-ment risks in Appendix B.

B Potential Risks 

First, RCD has potential dual-use risks. By making answers more consistent with explicit rationales, the method could be misused to produce more per-suasive but misleading explanations, such as ra-tionalized misinformation, deceptive arguments, or fabricated justifications. While our work does not target such applications, future deployment of rationale-sensitive decoding methods should con-sider monitoring, provenance tracking, and safe-guards against persuasive misuse.

Second, the fairness and robustness implications of RCD remain underexplored. Our experiments fo-cus on standard English reasoning benchmarks and do not evaluate multilingual, culturally grounded, or demographically sensitive scenarios. Fu-ture work should evaluate RCD on more diverse languages, domains, and user populations before deployment in broad-facing applications.

H Compatibility with Self-Consistency 

We further examine whether RCD can be combined with Self-Consistency (SC; Wang et al. (2023)), which aggregates predictions from multiple sam-pled rationales. Unlike SC, which improves answer selection through multiple reasoning paths, RCD modifies the answer decoding distribution condi-tioned on each rationale. We evaluate their com-bination on GSM8K using Qwen3-8B with three sampled rationales ( k = 3 ). Table 7 presents the results. Standard SC achieves 89.58% accuracy, whereas combining SC with RCD improves accuracy to 90.79%. Simi-larly, in the single-rationale setting, RCD improves standard CoT from 89.16% to 89.91%. These re-sults suggest that RCD is complementary to multi-sample aggregation. Furthermore, RCD incurs only a 1.5% increase in end-to-end latency over standard CoT, indicating that its additional computational overhead is small in this setting.
