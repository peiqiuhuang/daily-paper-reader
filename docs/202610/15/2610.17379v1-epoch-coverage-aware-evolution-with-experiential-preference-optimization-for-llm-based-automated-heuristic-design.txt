## EPOCH: Coverage-Aware Evolution with Experiential Preference Optimization for LLM-based Automated Heuristic Design *

Oguzhan Gungordu, Ayse Tursucular, Siheng Xiong, Faramarz Fekri 

Georgia Institute of Technology 

{ogungordu3, atursucular3, sxiong45}@gatech.edu faramarz.fekri@ece.gatech.edu 

Abstract 

LLM-based automated heuristic design (AHD) discovers heuristics for combinatorial optimiza-tion problems (COPs), where an LLM gener-ates candidates in an evolutionary search. Ex-isting frameworks freeze the generator or fine-tune it on a scalar fitness signal hiding which in-stances each heuristic covers. Reflection-based frameworks improve heuristics with natural-language feedback, but it stays in the prompt and never reaches the generator’s weights. We propose EPOCH , which makes instance cover-age the objective of the evolutionary search and fine-tunes the generator to internalize reflec-tion. Parent selection, operator allocation, and stagnation recovery all optimize how well the heuristics jointly cover the training instances, so the search returns a complementary heuris-tic ensemble. The generator learns from its own search by experiential preference opti-mization. A teacher LLM returns a natural-language insight on the generated heuristics, and the generator produces new heuristics with this insight. These improvements join the pref-erence pairs for direct preference optimiza-tion (DPO). Since DPO only teaches the gen-erator to rank these improvements, a reflec-tive self-distillation (RSD) term makes it more likely to generate them on its own, and the gains accumulate over the long horizon of the search. Across COPs and open-source LLM backbones, EPOCH ’s heuristic ensembles out-perform inference-only and fine-tuned base-lines and generalize across problem scales. 

1 Introduction 

Many operational decisions, from vehicle routing to scheduling, are COPs (Tam et al., 2024). Be-cause these problems are typically NP-hard, practi-tioners rely on heuristics that trade optimality for speed (Kirkpatrick et al., 1983; Glover, 1990). AHD makes the heuristic itself the ob-ject of search (Burke et al., 2013), classically with genetic programming, which cannot express heuris-tics beyond its human-defined function set (Lang-don and Poli, 2013; Duflo et al., 2019). LLM-based AHD replaces that function set with a large language model (LLM) that generates heuristics in the evolutionary loop (Liu et al., 2023), demonstrated by FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2023) and EoH (Liu et al., 2024). ReEvo (Ye et al., 2024) adds reflection (Shinn et al., 2023), HSEvo (Dat et al., 2025) maintains population di-versity, MCTS-AHD (Zheng et al., 2025) organizes heuristics in a Monte Carlo tree, and EoH-S (Liu et al., 2026) evolves a complementary set of heuris-tics. All keep the generator frozen. CALM (Huang et al., 2026) and EvoTune (Šurina et al., 2025) in-stead fine-tune the generator on the heuristics it generates, using group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) with a manually de-signed, graded scalar reward and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) with fitness-ranked pairs, respectively. In both, a heuristic is reduced to a single number, so the update drives the generator toward one best-on-average heuristic, with no reflection on the gen-erated heuristics. Appendix A gives an extended review of related work. Despite this progress, two limitations remain. First, no heuristic is best on every instance of a distribution (Liu et al., 2026), and a scalar fit-ness hides which instances each heuristic wins, so the coverage of the training instances cannot guide the evolutionary search beyond survivor se-lection. Second, the natural-language feedback of the inference-only methods stays inside the prompt and never reaches the generator’s weights, and the fine-tuned methods employ no reflection. 3 Methodology 

EPOCH augments an LLM-based evolutionary loop with two coupled ideas. The search main-2tains a heuristic pool P, initialized from a seed heuristic; its Np=10 heuristics with the lowest av-erage gap form the reproduction population PNp .Each iteration draws an operator, builds a prompt from parents selected from this population, sam-ples candidates from the policy πθ, and admits the valid ones into P. A per-instance gap-vector representation makes the search coverage-aware, and an experiential preference-optimization update, fed by teacher-guided reflection and an RSD term that internalizes its gains, fine-tunes the generator on its own evolutionary search. Sections 3.1–3.7 present the representation, parent selection, the evo-lutionary operators, the operator bandit, coverage stagnation and recovery, the reflective rollout, and experiential preference optimization, respectively. 3.3 Evolutionary Operators 

EPOCH evolves heuristics with five evolutionary operators, a two-parent crossover operator and four 3single-parent mutation operators, all sharing a com-mon prompt skeleton that presents each parent’s design idea, code, and average gap; the full tem-plates and their sources are given in Appendix I. • Crossover recombines two parents, with the variant set by the branch of Eq. (9): 

– Exploitative crossover , used when the second parent was chosen by average-gap rank, asks for a heuristic that out-performs both parents on the same in-stances. 

– Complementary crossover , used when the second parent was chosen by comple-mentarity, asks for a heuristic that unites the parents’ strengths across all instances either of them already handles well. • Simplification takes one parent and distills it into a more concise, elegant version, counter-ing the build-up of redundant components. • Injection takes one parent and adds a novel component to it while preserving its core mechanism. • Replacement takes one parent, locates a com-ponent that scores competing choices nearly equally, and rewrites it to differentiate them. • Refine takes one parent, identifies its single most important weakness, and rewrites it to fix that weakness while preserving the parts that already work well.

3.5 Coverage Stagnation and Expert-Preserving Recovery 

Self-reinforcing prompting can drive the pool onto minor variants of the current best heuristic, so EPOCH performs coverage-aware population man-agement, detecting coverage stagnation and coun-tering it with an expert-preserving recovery that shrinks the pool to Np/2 heuristics. The stagna-tion criterion is keyed to the coverage gap rather than the single best heuristic, which can stay un-changed while the pool keeps gaining expert heuris-tics. Each iteration recomputes ρ(P) and its best value ρ⋆, registering an improvement when 

ρ(P) < ρ ⋆ − ϵρ, (12) for an absolute margin ϵρ=10 −4. The recovery fires after Tρ=10 iterations without improvement, or Tcap =40 iterations after the last recovery, the latter forcing a periodic reset in long improvement stretches. It rebuilds the pool from the best-by-average-gap heuristic, the seed, and the highest-expert-count heuristics, resets the bandit’s quality estimates, and restarts the stagnation window by setting ρ⋆ to the recovered pool’s coverage gap. A persistent expert archive A, untouched by recovery, retains the best heuristics found on each training in-stance, so the pool can shrink aggressively without losing discovered expert heuristics. Algorithm 5 implements the recovery as R ECOVER .

The critique adapts to how many valid responses Round 1 produced, where a response y is valid when it follows the output format, parses into an executable heuristic hy, and runs on the training instances without failure. • Contrastive critique. With two or more valid heuristics, the teacher contrasts the best and worst, reporting the design choices that re-duced the gap and an instance-grounded sum-mary of the training instances each wins. • Repair critique. With one valid heuristic, the teacher pairs it with a failed one and combines what worked with what to repair. • Diagnostic critique. With no valid heuristic, the teacher inspects two distinct failure modes and proposes a runnable direction. 

3.7 Experiential Preference Optimization 

An iteration produces a pooled batch Y1 ∪ Y2 of valid and invalid responses. Each valid response re-ceives the reward r(y) = −¯γ(hy), the negative av-erage gap of its heuristic. Invalid responses require no reward, only a failure-type label that selects the repair and diagnostic critiques of Section 3.6. Two families of preference pairs are appended to a grow-ing buffer B by B UILD PAIRS in Algorithm 6, all conditioned on the unhinted prompt q:• Within-batch pairs. Following Šurina et al. (2025), the valid responses are split by re-ward into halves and randomly matched across them, each used at most once. Each invalid response is paired as dispreferred against a random valid response, teaching the policy to avoid unparseable or crashing code. • Cross-round pairs. Each valid Round-2 hinted response y+ ∼ πθ(· | ˜q) is preferred over a strictly worse valid Round-1 unhinted response y− ∈ Y1 with r(y−) < r(y+).These pairs encode the lift from reflection and form the reflective subset Bref .The insight z biases Round 2 toward better heuris-tics but is only a sampling aid; every pair condi-tions on the unhinted q, so the policy is trained on the prompt distribution it generates from, and the optimality gap certifies every pair’s ordering. Every Tdpo =20 iterations we run a preference-optimization burst, the D PO BURST step of Algo-rithm 1, over the accumulated buffer. A threshold filter drops pairs whose preferred reward falls be-low the 30 th percentile of the valid-output rewards observed since the previous burst (Šurina et al., 2025), and the buffer accumulates across all itera-tions, which preserves diversity. The reference πref 

is the current model with its low-rank adaptation (LoRA) disabled (Hu et al., 2022).

4 Experiments 

We evaluate EPOCH on three COPs, the TSP, OBP, and Knapsack Problem (KP), solved within a step-by-step construction framework. We assess the single best heuristic, the expert-heuristic ensemble, and generalization from the in-domain (ID) training size to out-of-distribution (OOD) sizes. Problem definitions, datasets, and evaluation protocol are in Appendix B, and the KP results in Appendix E.1. 

Benchmarks and metrics. We report mean op-timality gaps in percent, where lower is better. For TSP this is the gap of the tour length to the LKH-3 (Helsgaun, 2017) optimum averaged over 300 

test instances per size, and for OBP the gap of the bin count to the L1 lower bound averaged over the Weibull test instances. Each method saves a set of heuristics, scored at two granularities. Top-1 evalu-ates the saved heuristic with the best training fitness, while top-K evaluates the saved set as a portfolio, taking on each test instance the lowest cost among the K heuristics. We use K ∈ { 1, 3, 10 } for TSP and K ∈ { 1, 4} for OBP. EPOCH’s saved set is the expert-heuristic ensemble from its archive A,ranked by expert count; Appendix B states how each baseline ranks its saved set.

5 Conclusion 

We propose EPOCH, an LLM-based AHD frame-work that fine-tunes the generator during the search and represents every heuristic by its per-instance optimality-gap vector rather than a scalar fitness. This representation makes the entire search loop coverage-aware, driving expert-aware parent selec-tion, an adaptive operator bandit, and coverage-8based stagnation recovery. Teacher-guided reflec-tion becomes a training signal, and RSD internal-izes these improvements into the generator. Across COPs and LLM backbones, EPOCH’s complemen-tary expert-heuristic ensembles outperform AHD methods and generalize across problem scales. 

Limitations 

EPOCH fine-tunes only the generator. The teacher LLM that returns the natural-language insight stays fixed throughout the search, so reflection guides the training of the generator without being trained it-self. Fine-tuning the reflection jointly with the gen-erator, toward an end-to-end fine-tuned system, is left for future work. EPOCH is evaluated within the step-by-step construction framework; Appendix B explains this choice, and solver-driven frameworks such as ant colony optimization and guided local search remain to be explored.

A Extended Related Work 

Heuristic design and hyper-heuristics. Exact algorithms for NP-hard COPs rarely scale to prac-tical instance sizes, so routing, scheduling, and design problems are commonly solved with heuris-tics (Desale et al., 2015; Tam et al., 2024) and with metaheuristics such as simulated annealing and tabu search (Kirkpatrick et al., 1983; Glover, 1990). Such solvers encode expert knowledge, and adapt-ing one to a new problem variant largely restarts the design effort (Pillay and Qu, 2018; Choong et al., 2018). Hyper-heuristics and AHD instead automate the design itself, selecting or construct-ing heuristics for a problem class (Burke et al., 2013). Genetic programming is the classical re-alization, evolving heuristic programs built from a human-defined function set (Langdon and Poli, 2013; Duflo et al., 2019), and free-form LLM code generation widens the search space beyond what that function set expresses. 

LLMs in evolutionary search. A line of work runs a standard evolutionary algorithm but lets an LLM generate the offspring, prompting it with the selected parents to produce new code or text, while fitness evaluation and survivor selection stay out-side the model (Lehman et al., 2024; Meyerson et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2023). LLM-based AHD builds on this setup, and EPOCH keeps the same outer loop but additionally trains the operator rather than treating it as fixed. 

LLM-based AHD frameworks. Fun-Search (Romera-Paredes et al., 2023) pioneered evolutionary program search with an LLM proposer, EoH (Liu et al., 2024) co-evolves thoughts and code, ReEvo (Ye et al., 2024) adds reflection (Shinn et al., 2023; Pryzant et al., 2023), HSEvo (Dat et al., 2025) couples diversity metrics with harmony search, and MCTS-AHD (Zheng et al., 2025) organizes heuristics in a Monte Carlo tree. Analyses of the AHD fitness landscape and code-evolution dynamics further motivate diversity (Liu et al., 2025; van Stein et al., 2025), and planning-based approaches organize the search over a tree (Wang et al., 2025) or an entailment graph that carries derivation history (Gungordu et al., 2026). Most relevant to our coverage view, EoH-S (Liu et al., 2026) evolves a set of complementary heuristics, using per-instance performance vectors to select survivors that specialize on different parts of the instance distribution. All of these methods keep the LLM frozen, whereas EPOCH trains the generator and uses the per-instance best gaps as an online control signal throughout the run. 

Training the LLM generator for AHD. 

CALM (Huang et al., 2026) co-evolves the heuristic pool and the LLM, fine-tuning with GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) on a progressive scalar reward and using a stagnation-driven collapse that resets the pool to the seed and current best. EvoTune (Šurina et al., 2025) interleaves FunSearch-style search with periodic DPO over a program database treated as a replay buffer, using a forward-KL variant that preserves output diversity and a dynamic reward-percentile filter. EPOCH likewise fine-tunes the generator, but governs selection, operator allocation, and recovery by per-instance gap vectors rather than scalar fitness, converts teacher-guided reflection into preference pairs and distills the hint-guided improvement into the unhinted policy with an RSD term, and deploys a complementary ensemble of expert heuristics. 

Reflection and natural-language feedback. 

Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) improve LLM outputs through in-context natural-language feedback, ExpeL (Zhao et al., 2024) reuses accumulated experience across tasks, and prompt-optimization methods treat textual critiques as gradients (Pryzant et al., 2023); all act at inference time without updating weights. Arecent thread instead folds text feedback into training. Text feedback can be distilled into first-attempt behavior so the model needs no feedback at test time (Song et al., 2026), and an experience–reflection–consolidation loop can internalize corrective revisions into the policy (Shi et al., 2026), though both target domains other 11 than heuristic design. EPOCH brings this idea to AHD, where the executable, verifiable reward orders reflection-conditioned generations without a learned judge, and its RSD term internalizes the hint-guided improvement into the policy’s unhinted behavior so it carries into later generations of the search. 

Preference optimization and learning from feed-back. Aligning LLMs from preferences was popularized by reinforcement learning from hu-man feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022); DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) removes the learned re-ward model by optimizing preferences directly, and Reflective Preference Optimization (Zhao and Li, 2025) constructs the pairs by conditioning regener-ation on a critic’s hint and contrasting hinted with unhinted responses, keeping the update on-policy. EPOCH adopts DPO for the generator together with this reflection-as-preference construction, but the preference ordering is certified by the optimal-ity gap on executed code rather than by a learned or model-based judge, and the contrasted responses are a hinted second batch versus a strictly worse unhinted first batch from the same evolutionary it-eration. That work also distills the hint-conditioned behavior back into the unconditioned policy to re-move a test-time dependence on the hint. EPOCH’s RSD term shares this mechanism but is motivated within training, makes each iteration’s unhinted rollouts better and accumulates across generations, and is gated to the pairs whose verifiable reward certifies a genuine reflective gain. 

B Problem Definitions and Datasets 

We instantiate EPOCH on three COPs, solved within a step-by-step construction framework; the function signatures match the conventions of prior LLM-based AHD work (Zheng et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2024). 

Solution framework. We evaluate within the step-by-step construction framework because there the heuristic alone builds the solution, so the opti-mality gap measures the heuristic an AHD frame-work discovered. In solver-driven frameworks such as ant colony optimization and guided local search (Ye et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2025), the heuristic only biases a solver that continues the search on its own, through pheromone updates or local search, so the solver narrows the differences between heuristics and the gap reflects the solver as much as the discovery method. Each candidate evaluation also runs the solver on every training instance, which makes it far costlier than a con-struction run and the 1,000 -generation search of every method correspondingly slower. Extending EPOCH to these frameworks is left for future work. 

Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP). A TSP in-stance is a complete graph on n nodes with co-ordinates sampled uniformly from [0 , 1] 2 and Eu-clidean edge costs; a solution is a Hamiltonian tour scored by its total length. Within the step-by-step construction framework, the LLM designs the key function below, which chooses the next city from the current one given the unvisited set and the dis-tance matrix, and the framework calls it repeatedly until the tour is complete. 

> TSP key function
> select_next_node(current_node, destination_node, unvisited_nodes, distance_matrix) -> next_node

Per-instance gaps in Eq. (3) are computed against LKH-3 (Helsgaun, 2017) optimal tour lengths. Training uses m=64 instances at n=50 ;held-out evaluation uses a range of sizes ( n ∈{20 , 50 , 100 , 200 }) to test OOD generalization. 

Online Bin Packing (OBP). In OBP, items arrive sequentially with sizes drawn from a Weibull distri-bution and must be placed immediately into a bin that can hold them, minimizing the number of bins used. The LLM designs the priority function below, which scores each feasible bin for the arriving item, and the item is placed in the highest-priority bin. 

> OBP key function
> step(item_size, remaining_capacity) -> priority

Why this accumulates. A related distillation term has been motivated by inference-time hint independence, dropping the teacher at deploy-ment (Zhao and Li, 2025). That reading does not apply here, since EPOCH deploys saved heuristic 

artifacts rather than the policy. Our motivation is in-ternal to training. Every iteration draws its Round-1 rollouts from the unhinted policy πθ(· | q), so raising log πθ(y+ | q) makes those rollouts more likely to recover the reflective improvement un-prompted, which in turn seeds better parents, pref-erence pairs, and experts in subsequent iterations. Because search and policy update interleave, an improvement internalized in one burst propagates through the population it generates, so the effect accumulates over the run rather than acting only at its end. 

Implementation. We restrict RSD to Bref be-cause each of its pairs contains, by construction, a valid Round-2 winner over a strictly worse valid Round-1 rollout, so the term distills genuine re-flective gains rather than arbitrary accepted code. We length-normalize log πθ(y+ | q) by the token length of y+ so the objective does not favor short heuristics, and we keep λ small. With few reflective samples per burst, an unnormalized or large-λ term behaves like maximum-likelihood concentration onto a handful of accepted heuristics and can over-sharpen the policy, eroding the output diversity that the coverage objective depends on. Setting λ = 0 

recovers the plain-DPO update of Eq. (2), which makes the contribution of RSD a single-coefficient ablation.

20 E.4 Generalization to Real-World TSPLIB Instances 

The experiments of Section 4 train and test on ran-dom uniform instances. To test whether the dis-covered heuristics transfer to real, structured in-stances of varied size and scale, we evaluate on TSPLIB (Reinelt, 1991), using its 15 instances with fewer than 500 nodes. Coordinates are scaled to [0 , 1] 2 to match the training distribu-tion, each tour is built from three start nodes and averaged, and the gap is taken against the known optimum. For every method we deploy the top-10 ensemble of its single best run, se-lected by the lowest mean top-10 gap over the four uniform test sizes. We compare against the manually designed greedy, nearest-insertion, and nearest-neighbor heuristics (Rosenkrantz et al., 1977) and the genetic-programming hyper-heuristic GPHH (Duflo et al., 2019). As reported in Table 9, EPOCH attains the low-est average optimality gap and the best result on the majority of the TSPLIB instances. 

F Significance Testing and Uncertainty Quantification 

We test whether the advantage of EPOCH in Ta-bles 5 and 6 is statistically reliable along the two axes that matter for an LLM-driven search: the ran-domness of an entire run (LLM sampling and the stochastic search trajectory), and the spread of dif-ficulty across test instances. The first is addressed by a significance test across runs, the second by a paired comparison across instances. Every method is run at least five times. 

F.1 Statistical Significance Across Runs 

We treat each independent run of a method as one observation, summarized by the mean optimality gap of its deployed ensemble on a test set: the top-10 ensemble for TSP and the top-4 ensemble for OBP. For each test set we test whether EPOCH reaches a lower mean gap than a given baseline with a one-sided two-sample t-test in the Welch form, which does not assume the two methods share a variance (Welch, 1947). The per-size p-values for TSP on both backbones are in Table 10; those for OBP, where the test is run on the gap averaged over the six Weibull sets as each set holds only a few instances, are in Table 11. A p-value below 0.05 indicates that the lower mean gap of EPOCH is unlikely to be an artifact of run-to-run noise. EPOCH attains a significantly lower mean gap than the baselines in nearly all settings. 

F.2 Cross-Instance Uncertainty Quantification 

The run-level test takes a run as its unit; we now ask whether the advantage holds across the indi-vidual test instances, that is, whether it is distri-butional rather than carried by a few easy cases. We restrict this analysis to TSP and KP, whose test sets contain 300 and 1,000 instances each; the OBP sets are too small for a per-instance compari-son. For each method we average its per-instance optimality gap over runs to obtain one gap per in-stance for the deployed top-10 ensemble, and com-pare EPOCH against each baseline on the same instances. The paired per-instance gap differences are tested with the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (Wilcoxon, 1945), reported in Tables 12 and 13 together with the number of instances on which EPOCH wins, ties, or loses against the baseline (a tie is a gap difference within 10 −9). EPOCH wins the large majority of instances at every size and on both backbones on TSP, and on KP it wins several times more instances than it loses at every size, where most ties are instances both ensembles solve optimally; the test is significant throughout, so the gains in Tables 5 and 7 reflect a population-wide advantage rather than a few outliers.

G.2 Teacher LLM Ablation 

The teacher is a critic rather than a generator: it reads two heuristics with their gaps and explains their difference in a few sentences. We fill this role with a reasoning model that reads code, as is now common for critic roles, and take it from a different model family than the generator, so that the critique is an independent view of the candidates rather than the generator’s own.
