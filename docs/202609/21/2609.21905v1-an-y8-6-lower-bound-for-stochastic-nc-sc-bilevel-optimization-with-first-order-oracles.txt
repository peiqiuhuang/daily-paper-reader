Abstract 

We study the oracle complexity of finding ϵ-stationary points of smooth bilevel optimization problems with a nonconvex upper-level objective and a strongly convex lower-level problem. We consider a stochastic first-order oracle that returns unbiased stochastic gradients of both the upper- and lower-level objectives, with variance bounded by σ2. # 1 Introduction 

Bilevel optimization is a fundamental framework for hierarchical decision making in which one optimization problem is nested inside another (Colson et al., 2007). In its canonical unconstrained form, the problem is written as min

We study the smooth nonconvex-strongly-convex setting, where g(x, ·) is strongly convex for every x, while the upper-level objective f , and hence the hyper-objective Φ, may be nonconvex. Under standard smoothness assumptions, Φ is differentiable, but its gradient involves the inverse lower-level Hessian. Classical approaches therefore approximate this inverse (Ghadimi and Wang, 2018; Ji et al., 2021), typically requiring Hessian-vector and thus access to second-order information. Since such products can be substantially more expensive than gradients, first-order bilevel methods using only upper- and lower-level gradients have been developed (Shen and Chen, 2023; Kwon et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025b; Kwon et al., 2024b). Lower bounds and zero-chain constructions. The modern lower-bound theory for first-order optimization originates from chain-structured quadratic instances used in convex optimization (Ne-mirovskij and Yudin, 1983; Nesterov, 2013). The essential feature of these instances is that first-order information can reveal new coordinates only sequentially. Carmon et al. (2020, 2021) formal-ized this mechanism through zero-chains and developed robust zero-chain constructions for smooth nonconvex optimization. Their results include the classical Ω( ϵ−2) lower bound for smooth noncon-vex optimization under the first-order oracle, as well as the Ω( ϵ−12/7 ) and Ω( ϵ−8/5 ) lower bounds under second-order and arbitrarily high-order smoothness, respectively. In stochastic optimization, Arjevani et al. (2023) introduced probability-p zero-chains together with random rotations and soft projections, establishing the tight Ω( ϵ−4) lower bound under bounded variance and the Ω( ϵ−3)

lower bound under mean-squared smoothness. Random rotation is also the standard device for lifting coordinate-based zero-chain arguments beyond a fixed coordinate system (Carmon et al., 2020; Arjevani et al., 2023). Lower bounds for general bilevel optimization are more recent. Liang et al. (2023) established lower bounds for convex-strongly-convex and strongly-convex-strongly-convex bilevel problems un-der second-order oracle access. In finite-sum bilevel empirical-risk minimization, Dagréou et al. (2024) derived a matching finite-sum lower bound, while Chen et al. (2024) studied hardness phe-nomena that arise when the lower-level problem is not strongly convex. For stochastic NC-SC bilevel optimization, Kwon et al. (2024a) established an Ω( ϵ−6) lower bound under bounded variance and an Ω( ϵ−4) lower bound under an additional stochastic-smoothness assumption. Their oracle is y∗-aware, supplying an O(ϵ)-accurate approximation of y∗(x), while requiring the stochastic gradients to be unbiased only locally around y∗(x).Consequently, these lower bounds do not directly characterize the standard globally unbiased stochastic first-order oracle considered in this paper.
