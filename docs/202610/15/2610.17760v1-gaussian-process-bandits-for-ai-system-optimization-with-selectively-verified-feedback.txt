# Gaussian Process Bandits for AI System Optimization with Selectively Verified Feedback 

Shuang Wu UCLA 

shuangwu222@ucla.edu 

Abstract 

Gaussian process bandits exploit similarities among configurations to optimize prompts, retrieval systems, and agent workflows. In these applications, however, inexpensive model-based evaluations can disagree with trusted task outcomes. We study selective verification when an action is chosen before a diagnostic is observed and trusted feedback is informative on an unknown set of diagnostic states. A finite reliability class and a finite RKHS signal dictionary describe these two sources of uncertainty. We construct a fixed audit design that combines reliability coverage with reward separation and establish a finite-time regret bound that accounts for a hard audit budget and the rounds needed to obtain informative observations. A safe adaptive extension mixes this robust design with predictable Chernoff allocations while preserving a uniform identification floor and exact expected acquisition-cost accounting. For signed-coordinate kernels, structured and diffuse reliability classes require different audit scales despite sharing the same reward geometry. Synthetic experiments distinguish these certified designs from an adaptive posterior heuristic and GP controls. Safe adaptive allocation reduces aggregate regret relative to the fixed design under the exact dictionary, but its finite-budget error certificate is numerically loose. Conditional-feedback errors can remove the heuristic’s advantage and target perturbations can reverse it. Separate AI-task replays assess feedback correction and fitted-dictionary policies without establishing end-to-end validation of a learned selective-verification method. 

# 1 Introduction 

Language-model judges make repeated evaluation inexpensive, but they can reverse the ranking of prompts, retrieval pipelines, or agent configurations. A Gaussian process (GP) fitted to such scores can estimate the wrong objective precisely. Trusted evaluation—human adjudication, execution in a protected environment, or a high-quality test suite—repairs the target but can dominate total cost. This motivates a bandit that sees cheap post-action information and selectively purchases the trusted outcome. Existing methods cover selective audits, prediction-powered inference, auxiliary-feedback kernel bandits, and post-surrogate acquisition [23, 29, 30, 42]. We ask how many trusted outcomes are required when the learner must discover where feedback is informative. For unrestricted outcome laws, target-kernel rank and a fixed finite collection of conditional statistics do not determine that information. We introduce a finite conditional-reliability class. A known dictionary {Ch} contains possible informative diagnostic sets, but h is unknown. Its fractional hitting number τ ⋆ measures the audit mass needed to touch every candidate reliable set. We couple this uncertainty to a finite RKHS signal dictionary through a max–min experimental design. # 2 Related work 

Selective verification and surrogate feedback. Prediction-powered best-arm identification and adaptive inference combine inexpensive predictions with selectively acquired trusted labels [29, 42]. Limited-verification bandits and surrogate-reward bandits study trusted feedback under attacks or machine-learning-generated rewards [19, 37]. ASB permits acquisition after observing a surrogate and accounts for learned refinements and hard budgets [30]. Confidence–budget matching 2and costly-observation bandits provide reward-only controls [15, 44]. Our question concerns the additional cost of identifying an unknown reliable-state set while optimizing cumulative true reward. 

Kernel bandits and AI evaluation. Auxiliary-feedback IGP-UCB , prediction-augmented GP-UCB ,and robust multi-fidelity optimization exploit additional feedback under structural assumptions [22, 23, 39]. Kernel experimental design and misspecified GP bandits characterize reward geometry and target approximation [13, 14]. Related exploration methods include residual-bootstrap LinReBoot ,graph-neural GNN-TS , and Laplacian kernelized bandits [17, 27, 36]. ARES and HbBoPs connect trusted evaluation and kernel optimization to RAG and prompt selection [25, 28]. We study how conditional reliability interacts with kernel decision geometry, rather than a new kernel posterior or a generic correction estimator. 

Controlled sensing and decision-focused learning. Controlled sensing, decision-region active learning, and transductive design allocate measurements to distinguish consequential alternatives [1, 2, 4, 7, 18]. Best-arm identification with post-action context provides context-tracking and Track-and-Stop rules [41]. These ideas motivate our likelihood decoder and adaptive allocation. Our analysis separates reliability coverage, action separation, and the deployment cost imposed by choosing an action before observing the diagnostic. 

Partial monitoring and identifiability. Kernelized partial monitoring, conditional mean em-beddings, and cost-aware streaming labels provide broader sequential abstractions [11, 12, 20, 21]. Exact rejection sampling implements the desired accepted-query law [26]. Classical best-arm and mixture testing explain the polynomial rates in our structured–diffuse comparison [3, 8, 32], while contaminated and misspecified identification establish related identifiability failures [6, 16]. Our results specialize these tools to a finite reliability–signal dictionary, with an adaptive converse and matched-summary counterexamples. Appendix D compares the assumptions and guarantees in detail.

3.1 AI evaluation protocol and objective 

Each action is a fixed prompt, retrieval pipeline, or agent configuration used on a fresh request. A cheap diagnostic is observed after deployment, and a trusted assessment is available only if purchased. We optimize expected trusted quality under a stationary action set and kernel. Request-dependent routing and within-agent-step optimization are outside this model. Let A and S be finite, with integer horizon T ≥ 1 and audit cap M ≥ 1. 6.1 Evaluation protocol and comparison methods 

We evaluate whether conditional reliability improves decisions under a fixed trusted-label budget, whether adaptive verification improves the proved fixed design, and how these benefits depend on model specification. Cumulative target regret RT charges every deployed action, including rounds without an audit. Terminal simple regret is the gap of the recommended action. Lower values are better. We separately report trusted-label counts and the round of the final audit, since equal label budgets need not imply equal acquisition time. 

Synthetic tasks. The primary study uses signed-coordinate kernels of ranks two and eight and a periodic RBF kernel on 16 actions. It crosses one or four reliability groups, candidate-state probabilities 1 and .25, and audit caps 128, 512, and 2 ,048, with horizon 8 ,192 and 256 paired replications per setting. The independent GP comparison uses 24 settings, T = 4 ,096, M ∈{64 , 256 , 1,024 }, and 128 paired replications. A separate adaptive-design study uses eight actions, four squared-exponential kernel signals, three reliability families, ( T, M ) ∈ { (512 , 32) , (2048 , 64) },and 512 paired replications. Each primary evaluation uses prespecified dictionaries, policies, and seeds. 

Policies and controls. Reliability-Kernel-Design is Algorithm 1. The safe adaptive policy in Proposition 4 mixes a fixed robust joint law with Chernoff allocation using ε = .25. Conditional TS instead samples a reliability–signal pair from its posterior to select actions and audits all candidate-state outcomes until the cap. Its gate is fixed, so it is an adaptive-action heuristic, not the proved adaptive-verification policy. Controls include marginal-dictionary pooled TS , direct-arm UCB, likelihood decoders, GP policies, and a known-reliability oracle. Actions precede states, and only purchased outcomes update the models. GP controls use a Gaussian working likelihood with variance 1 /4. Appendix C gives the grids, default parameters, and separately development-selected parameters. 

Uncertainty and aggregation. Policies share environment and noise streams. Per-setting paired 95% intervals are descriptive and unadjusted. Aggregate ratios are geometric means of per-setting mean-regret ratios. Standard errors and bootstrap intervals condition on the fixed task grid, not a population of new tasks.

6.3 Model sensitivity and AI-task evaluation 

Reliability errors and target perturbations. The robustness study separates changes to the conditional feedback law from changes to the reward target. Target-preserving feedback error gradually erodes the conditional policy’s advantage. The conditional-to-pooled regret ratio is 1 .027 at feedback error .20 and 1 .068 after exact feedback flattening. Target perturbations of .05 and .10 reverse the comparison, with ratios 1 .398 and 1 .799. Correct target geometry alone therefore does not ensure informative conditional feedback, as Proposition 5 predicts. Appendix C.4 gives the laws and uncertainty. 

AI evaluation with fitted dictionaries. JudgeBench and the 32-judge ChaosNLI study test whether calibration can supply the model required by the online policy. The former uses 144 calibration labels and 1,584 archived judge outputs, while the latter uses 591 labels and 18,912 outputs. Online reveal budgets are additional to these calibration costs. On JudgeBench, fitted dictionaries predict worse than a pooled model and give regret ratios 11 .63 and 16 .46 against uniform verified TS. In the 32-judge study, clipping nearly every fitted signal entry makes the action design collapse to a tie, and selective verification changes acquisition time without improving regret over its matched uniform-state control. A postdiagnostic 32-judge study separates reliability-set coverage from action-model expansion. Retaining two fitted signals while completing the reliability class improves all four settings relative to expanding both classes. The two-state reliability model has ratio .581 against uniform verified TS, whereas the one-state model has ratio 1 .143. This contrast identifies action-model constraints as a concrete source of the earlier loss, but it is fitted-table development evidence and does not validate a learned selective gate. Appendix C gives the complete comparisons, offline replays, calibration uncertainty, and data-access provenance. The evidence supports the exact-model mechanism, while practical gains remain sensitive to model estimation and acquisition time. 12 References

# D Detailed comparisons with prior work 

Verified and prediction-powered bandits. PP-LUCB studies best-arm identification with LLM judges, selective audits, and residual-aware allocation [29]. PPAI estimates prediction weights under adaptive sampling [42]. Limited-verification bandits quantify the value of trusted observations under attacks [19]. These works establish that auditing and correction are not new primitives. Our objective is cumulative true-reward regret under a hard audit budget, and our new parameter is an unknown conditional-reliability class. MLA-UCB uses machine-learning-generated surrogate rewards for cumulative-regret reduction [37], while confidence–budget matching treats optional costly observations in sequential learning [15]. They provide controls for judge-assisted and audit-scarce regimes, respectively. Neither models a latent post-action reliability set coupled to kernel action geometry. 37 Kernel and multi-fidelity optimization. Auxiliary-feedback IGP-UCB uses parameterized correlated feedback [23]. PA-GP-UCB models prediction and reward jointly and observes both during online selection [39]. Robust multi-fidelity BO guards against unreliable low-fidelity sources [22]. Kernel experimental design and misspecified GP bandits already control target approximation error [13, 14]. ASB permits post-surrogate acquisition: its fixed-comparison theorem gives PRE/POST variance-optimal allocations, its main guarantees cover finite policy classes and a hard-budget linear construction, and its learned-refinement proposition charges residual-model error [30]. Chandak et al. optimize context-conditioned collection policies and use exact multi-proposal rejection sampling to reuse adaptively collected data [26]. These results establish post-surrogate timing, residual-based allocation, kernel modeling, and rejection sampling as existing components. 

Controlled sensing and experimental design. Controlled multihypothesis testing optimizes Chernoff information over sensing controls, and adaptive causal controls can improve on open-loop designs with three or more hypotheses [1, 18]. Bayesian decision-region determination already selects tests only to resolve the action-relevant equivalence class, including correlated noisy tests [2, 4]. Transductive linear best-arm identification separates measurement and decision sets through experimental design [7]; recent hybrid-feedback GLB identification optimizes a joint query space and includes heterogeneous acquisition costs [46]. These methods supply decision-focused likelihood tests and query designs with exponentially decreasing error. Proposition 4 composes their predictable adaptive allocation principle with a fixed robust information floor and the exact action-before-state cost identity. The composition is scoped to this protocol and is not a new generic adaptive-testing principle. Our reliability–action factorization, adaptive converse, and deterministic coverage formula specialize this approach to unknown reliable-state sets; they do not characterize general adaptive optimality. 

Mixture testing and the diffuse regime. Adaptive mixture testing already shows that repeated allocation can overcome rare-component dilution when the same latent item can be queried repeatedly [8]. Our diffuse construction instead makes state repetitions negligible over the decision horizon. After the negligible-collision coupling, marginalizing the reliability set gives a standard d-armed Bernoulli identification problem with gap ρ∆; classical best-arm lower bounds and fixed-budget conversions already yield the d/ (ρ2∆2) scale up to logarithms [3, 32]. The resulting comparison shows that rank and hitting number alone are insufficient to determine audit complexity. 

Partial monitoring. Kernelized linear partial monitoring already supplies information-directed sequential policies, observability-based regret classifications, and RKHS observation operators [11]. Its contextual extension also plans against a known context distribution, so a sequential information ratio, context-weighted observability coefficient, or kernelization is not new here. A finite verified-feedback dictionary can be unfolded into a constrained partial-monitoring game over contingent audit maps; our protocol-specific remainder is the hard pathwise audit cap together with the exact action-before-state compilation cost. This is why the paper states a structured finite-family factorization rather than a new generic sequential complexity. 

Nonparametric conditional feedback. Nonparametric models provide another way to represent conditional feedback laws. CME-UCB already estimates conditional mean embeddings while sequen-tially optimizing an unknown conditional expectation [12]; distributionally-aware kernel bandits model probability kernels through kernel mean embeddings and prove UCB guarantees and lower bounds [20]; and active cost-aware streaming labeling chooses expensive labels after inputs arrive and gives matching discrete and continuous-RKHS rates [21]. These models do not impose the action-before-state protocol with a hard audit cap. Extending their estimators to that protocol would require accounting for the acquisition costs in Proposition 3. 38 Post-action contexts. Post-serving-context bandits also observe informative features only after action selection; poLinUCB learns a pre-to-post context map and attains tight linear-contextual regret [24]. The trusted outcome here is optional and subject to a hard pathwise budget. The row-max formula is the exact domination constant obtained when classical rejection sampling is restricted to proposals of the form q(a)μ(s); it quantifies acquisition cost under this proposal restriction. 

Cost-sensitive acquisition. Costly-observation bandits provide complementary cost-sensitive guarantees. Schur et al. give two-phase GP algorithms, cost-adjusted information-gain complexities, and matching finite-arm rates for observation-priced objectives [44]. Gong and Zhang establish hard free-observation-budget phase transitions for contextual bandits with paid observations [33]. Harding et al. choose a costly source before an unobserved group is revealed and obtain matching budgeted minimax rates by effective-sample-size design and post-stratification [34]; Reyzin et al. give tight cost-adaptive rates for heterogeneous expert queries [43]. Progressive-feedback bandits quantify early-surrogate value by conditional mutual information, but eventually observe the primary outcome for every play [35]. Cost-aware acquisition, hard feedback budgets, and rejection-sampled reuse are therefore prior methods. Harding et al. do not select labels after seeing the group or study sequential decision regret, while Chandak et al. do not study hard audit budgets, sampling regret, or latent reliability. The remaining scoped distinction is the finite-dictionary reliability–kernel theorem and its adaptive converse; Schur et al. explicitly leave general-kernel optimality open, so Proposition 3 is not presented as a matching kernel converse. 

LLM routing with restricted feedback. Recent LLM-routing studies consider related feedback restrictions. Wei et al. give linear-contextual upper and lower bounds under a limited reward-feedback budget [45]. Sridhar et al. combine context-dependent graph feedback with ML surrogate rewards and use expert aggregation to recover a reward-only guarantee when those surrogates are misspecified [31]. Unlike both settings, trusted outcomes here are optional after observing a post-action state, and the unknown object is the state-conditional reliability law. 

AI evaluation. ARES uses prediction-powered inference for RAG evaluation [25]. HbBoPs applies deep-kernel Bayesian optimization to prompt selection [28]. NAOD designs preference queries while accounting for judge nuisance [38]. Our model is narrower: fixed configurations, i.i.d. requests, bounded outcomes, and a fixed kernel. It excludes contextual routing, nonstationarity, and within-agent-step credit assignment.
