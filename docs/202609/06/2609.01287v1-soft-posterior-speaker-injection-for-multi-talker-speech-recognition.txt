ABSTRACT 

Multi-talker automatic speech recognition (MT-ASR) re-mains challenging under overlapping speech. Hard diarization-based segmentation introduces irreversible errors, whereas serialized output training (SOT) avoids explicit segmentation but does not condition a pretrained encoder on speaker ac-tivity. We propose Soft Posterior Speaker Injection (SPSI): a lightweight head predicts frame-level speaker posteriors ˆP

and injects them into Whisper through multi-layer feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) and decoder speaker-memory prompts. On controlled two-speaker LibriSpeech overlap, SPSI reduces utterance-mean constrained permutation word error rate (cpWER) from 50.7% (SOT) to 49.6% (one-sided paired bootstrap p≈0.006 ), with a larger reduction in the high-overlap bin (60.4% →58.8%). Same-backbone speaker-auxiliary objectives and voice activity detection (VAD) pipelines do not outperform SOT; zero-shot (ZS) LibriCSS is comparable. Freeze-posterior adaptation with overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) continuation reduces held-out LibriCSS cpWER (sessions 8–9) to 32.4% (versus 37.5% for SOT). Ablations indicate complementary encoder FiLM and decoder prompts, and that the effective signal is a soft simplex-valued speaker share. 

Index Terms — Multi-talker Speech Recognition, Over-lapped Speech, Soft Speaker Posterior, Serialized Output Training 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Multi-talker automatic speech recognition (MT-ASR) tran-scribes conversations with multiple speakers, often overlap-ping, into per-speaker text streams [1–5]. Cascaded systems that segment mixtures with voice activity detection (VAD) or diarization boundaries, or that first separate sources [6–8], are sensitive to boundary errors on the order of tens to hun-dreds of milliseconds that truncate onsets and leak compet-ing speech into the wrong stream. Serialized output train-ing (SOT) [4, 9] avoids explicit segmentation, but the acous-tic encoder is typically not conditioned on speaker activity. Diarization-conditioned Whisper [10] injects hard speaker ac-tivity. In contrast, we inject a mixture-normalized speaker

Soft Posterior Speaker Injection (SPSI) predicts ˆP from Whisper encoder states [11] and injects it (i) after encoder blocks 8, 16 and 24 by feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) [12] and (ii) into the decoder as K=4 speaker-memory tokens (Fig. 1). Training combines an SOT recog-nition loss with a soft diarization loss; on synthetic data, checkpoints are selected by high-overlap validation con-strained permutation word error rate (cpWER). Relative to speaker-distinguishable connectionist temporal classification (SD-CTC) [13] and speaker-aware CTC [14], SPSI is a con-tinuous share injector , rather than a one-speaker-per-frame classifier. The contributions of this paper are summarized as fol-lows: • We propose a novel SPSI method to address over-lapped MT-ASR without irreversible hard segmenta-tion. SPSI injects a mixture-normalized speaker share into Whisper through multi-layer FiLM and decoder speaker-memory prompts. • Based on SPSI, we present a two-pass encode on a shared Whisper-medium backbone for MT-ASR. It is a simple and effective share injector that requires no external diarization or source separation at inference. • The comprehensive experiments demonstrate that SPSI reduces cpWER relative to same-backbone SOT on controlled LibriSpeech overlap, with a larger gain un-der high overlap, and that freeze-posterior overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) adaptation yields a 5.1-point held-out reduction on oracle-segmented LibriCSS [2].

4. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

Soft Posterior Speaker Injection (SPSI) injects continuous speaker posteriors via multi-layer feature-wise linear modu-lation (FiLM) and decoder prompts for multi-talker automatic speech recognition (MT-ASR). It reduces constrained permu-tation word error rate (cpWER) relative to serialized output training (SOT) and hard-segmentation pipelines on stratified LibriSpeech overlap; encoder FiLM and decoder prompts are complementary, and hard masks do not outperform SOT. On oracle-segmented LibriCSS, zero-shot cpWER is compa-rable to SOT. Freeze-posterior training with overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) adaptation yields a 5.1-point held-out reduction versus SOT ( p< 0.001 ); hard-mask, adapter, and ω=2 variants do not match this transfer.
