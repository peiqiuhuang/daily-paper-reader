## ABSTRACT 

Recent advances in penalty-based methods for stochastic bilevel optimization (SBO) have eliminated the need for second-order derivative oracles. However, for stochastic nonconvex–strongly convex bilevel problems, existing first-order meth-ods typically rely on nested loops and/or large batch sizes for attaining O(ϵ−6)

or O(ϵ−4) sample complexity under standard bounded-variance assumption or mean-square smoothness assumption. Achieving these rates with a single-loop penalty method and a constant batch size remains challenging due to a large penalty value needed for an accurate approximation. To address this challenge, we develop a stochastic SIngle-loop COnstant-Batch first-order penalty method (SICO) that combines two complementary ingredients. First, it performs one stochastic-gradient update per-iteration for both the original lower-level and pe-nalized problems, with a projection that controls the separation between their it-erates. Second, it applies an exponential moving average to stabilize the upper-level gradient estimator. We show that this combination achieves O(ϵ−6) sample complexity using only O(1) stochastic-gradient samples per iteration under un-biased, bounded-variance stochastic gradients. Under the additional mean-square smoothness assumption on the lower-level stochastic gradients, the same algo-rithm improves the complexity to O(ϵ−4) also with O(1) batch size. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to match the best-known convergence rate for fully first-order SBO methods using a single loop and a constant batch size. This result addresses an open problem posed in the literature.

This gap motivates the following open question (Wright, 2025; Yang, 2026): 

Can we design a fully first-order method for NC-SC SBO that uses a single loop and constant-size batches while matching the best-known sample complexity under standard stochastic assumptions? 

We answer this question affirmatively with a simple algorithmic design supported by refined conver-gence analyses. Our method maintains yt and zt to track the minimizers of the penalized problem over y and original lower-level problem over z in (3), respectively, taking one stochastic-gradient step for each at every iteration given wt. Inspired by the projected-coupling idea of Kwon et al. (2024), we project the provisional z-iterate onto a ball of radius O(λ−1) centered at the updated y-iterate. This projection constrains the separation between the two iterates rather than their individual locations. We combine it with a standard exponential moving average of the stochastic gradient esti-mator for the upper-level variable w. The projection controls the relative lower-level tracking error, while the moving average controls the upper-level gradient estimation error with a constant batch size. We refer to the proposed algorithm as SICO. By establishing refined contraction and sensitivity bounds for coupled lower-level tracking, we prove that SICO achieves O(ϵ−6) sample complexity under unbiased, bounded-variance stochastic gra-dients and O(ϵ−4) under the additional mean-square smoothness assumption on the lower-level stochastic gradients, using only O(1) stochastic-gradient samples per iteration in both settings. In addition, our results also achieve the best known dependence on the condition number (cf. Table 1). We evaluate the proposed method on bilevel problems arising in data hyper-cleaning and regulariza-tion selection, demonstrating its effectiveness compared with existing fully first-order baselines.

Kwon et al. (2023) introduced F2SA, a fully first-order penalty method that avoids Jacobian and Hessian evaluations. It uses simple stochastic gradient updates for all variables. Its established sam-ple complexity is O(ϵ−7) in the fully stochastic setting. Their F3SA variant incorporates STORM-style gradient estimators for both the upper- and lower-level variables, improving the complexity to O(ϵ−5) under additional mean-square smoothness for both the upper and lower level stochastic gradients while using a single loop and constant-size batches. Kwon et al. (2024) established two improved complexity bounds: O(ϵ−6) under bounded-variance stochastic gradients and O(ϵ−4) under additional mean-square smoothness. Their method uses a double-loop structure with batch sizes of order ϵ−4 and ϵ−2 in the two settings, respectively. The analysis exploits the proximity of the original and penalized lower-level solutions through projection and, under mean-square smoothness. These ideas motivate our algorithm, while our refined tracking analysis enables single-loop updates with constant-size batches. Chen et al. can be bounded independently of λ. Their F2BSA method combines inner solvers with large batch sizes to attain eO(κ12 ϵ−6) sample complexity in the fully stochastic setting. Their F3BSA variant uses multilevel Monte Carlo and single-sample stochastic updates to attain eO(κ11 ϵ−6) complexity. However, constructing its gradient estimator still requires nested stochastic solvers and a batch size in the order of O(κ).Using an equivalent min–max reformulation of the penalty problem (3), Yang (2026) developed a single-loop stochastic momentum gradient descent–ascent method with constant-size batches. These methods approximate Hessian–vector products using finite differences of first-order gradients. Thus, its single-loop structure still entails an ϵ-dependent per-iteration sampling cost. ## 6 CONCLUSIONS 

We have proposed a fully first-order method for non-convex-strongly-convex stochastic bilevel op-timization. Building on the penalty framework and projected lower-level coupling, we combine projected tracking of lower level variables with a momentum update of the upper level variable to ob-tain a single-loop algorithm using only O(1) batch size. Our refined analysis establishes O(κ11 ϵ−6)

complexity under standard bounded variance and O(κ9ϵ−4) complexity under the additional mean-square smoothness assumption, matching the best-known rates of existing fully first-order methods without requiring large batches or nested loops. 9Preprint
