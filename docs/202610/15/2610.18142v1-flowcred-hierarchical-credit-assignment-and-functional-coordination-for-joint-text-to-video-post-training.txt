## Abstract 

Prompt optimization and generator post-training are two complementary ways to improve text-to-video (T2V) generation, and recent systems increasingly train both modules jointly. However, optimizing a prompt policy and a video generator from the same terminal reward creates two ambiguities: prompt quality is confounded with stochastic generation, and the two modules may realize overlapping local improvements. We introduce F LOW CRED , a two-level framework for joint T2V post-training. First, hierarchical credit assignment uses matched prompt–seed rollout grids to separate prompt-level credit from prompt-conditioned generator residuals under shared randomness. Second, functional coordination probes alterna-tive current-policy prompts at states from actual generation trajectories, providing generator-aware evidence for prompt optimization while selectively attenuating generator updates already supported by conditioning changes. Across multiple T2V benchmarks, F LOW CRED consistently improves matched joint-training base-lines. On LTX-2.5, F LOW CRED improves a strictly matched joint-training control from 61.47 to 64.46 on T2V-CompBench and from 55.58 to 58.08 on StoryEval, without increasing decoded-video or reward-model-call budgets. Factorial and realized-update analyses show complementary contributions from prompt shaping and generator routing, while human evaluation and transfer across backbones and updater families provide further supporting evidence. 

## 1 Introduction 

Text-to-video (T2V) systems increasingly expose two trainable adaptation channels: a language-side prompt policy and the video generator itself. Prompt-side methods improve conditioning while leaving the visual model fixed (Hao et al., 2023; Cao et al., 2023; Ji et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025); generator post-training instead changes the diffusion/flow model using reward or preference feedback (Black et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2023; Wallace et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025a; Xue et al., 2025; 2026). Recent systems optimize both modules jointly (Wang et al., 2026a; Cheng et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Jia et al., 2026). Prompt changes can reorganize entities, relations, and event descriptions before generation, whereas generator updates address residual motion, appearance, and execution errors that conditioning alone cannot remove. When only one module is updated, a terminal reward can be treated as supervision for that module; once both are trainable, the same scalar signal must implicitly serve both attribution and coordination roles. Joint post-training is therefore attractive; once both channels are trainable, however, one terminal score leaves two distinct questions unresolved. The first is statistical credit . A terminal reward conflates prompt quality with downstream generator randomness: identical prompts can score differently across seeds, while favorable trajectories can mask weaker prompt choices. The reward alone therefore does not identify which variation belongs to prompt choice and which remains after the prompt is fixed. For request u, prompt action p, stochastic generator trajectory T , and terminal reward R, let Q(u, p ) = E[R | u, p ] and V (u) = EpQ(u, p ).

The second question is functional coordination : even after statistical credit is separated, changing the prompt at a generator state may induce a response aligned with the same reward-improving direction used by the downstream optimizer. The two modules can therefore duplicate part of the same local improvement. Statistical credit and functional overlap are thus distinct problems. These two ambiguities require different remedies: variance reduction can make credit estimates cleaner without preventing functional overlap, while coordinating local updates does not by itself determine where terminal variation originated. Treating them separately lets joint post-training distinguish who should receive credit from which module should realize an overlapping improvement .To address both, we introduce F LOW CRED , named for its joint treatment of credit assignment and coordination across the prompt-to-video generation flow. Figure 1 summarizes the two ambiguities and their corresponding mechanisms. Hierarchical credit assignment uses matched prompt–seed rollouts to separate prompt-level credit from prompt-conditioned generator residuals. Functional coordination probes alternative prompts at actual rollout states and uses same-state response geometry for generator-aware prompt shaping and reliability-gated generator routing. The two mechanisms have different mathematical status: Eq. 1 is exact for any per-trajectory scalar score, whereas functional coordination is local and first-order. Its prompt term is auxiliary shaping and its generator rule is a minimum-overlap regularizer, not a conserved transfer of gradient mass. To keep the statistical claim separate from optimization heuristics, all affine calibration used to combine reward credit and functional prompt-credit terms is estimated once on disjoint training-only data and then frozen (Appendix D.3). Our contributions are threefold. 

(1) We introduce hierarchical credit assignment with matched prompt–seed rollouts, separating prompt-level credit from prompt-conditioned generator residuals under shared generator randomness. This gives the prompt policy and generator distinct estimators tied to their positions in the stochastic hierarchy. 2(2) We develop functional coordination from same-state prompt responses, using local response geometry for generator-aware prompt shaping and reliability-gated routing of the generator reward update. It targets overlap that remains even after statistical credit has been separated. 

(3) We evaluate F LOW CRED with controlled joint-training baselines, mechanism audits, repeated runs, human evaluation, and transfer across backbones and updater families. Across four T2V benchmarks, FLOW CRED consistently improves the matched controls without increasing decoded-video or reward-model-call budgets. 

## 2 Related Work 

Prompt optimization and generator post-training. Prompt-side methods optimize conditioning with a fixed generator (Hao et al., 2023; Cao et al., 2023; Ji et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025), whereas PromptRL, VANS, UniGRPO, and CAPE-T2V couple language- and generator-side adaptation in different ways (Wang et al., 2026a; Cheng et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Jia et al., 2026). Generator alignment spans policy gradients, preference objectives, and differentiable reward optimization (Black et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2023; Wallace et al., 2024; Clark et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2023; Kirstain et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025a; Choi et al., 2026; Prabhudesai et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2026b); recent methods further refine reward shaping, trajectory allocation, and temporal credit (Xue et al., 2025; 2026; Luo et al., 2026; Zhu et al., 2026; Ye et al., 2026; Pu & Kong, 2026; Shen et al., 2026; Guan et al., 2026; Savani et al., 2026; Oba et al., 2026). These directions are complementary to our cross-module question. Modern open T2V systems span diverse backbone designs (Chen et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025; Kong et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025); our experiments hold the downstream harness fixed whenever numerical comparisons require matched conditions. 

Credit assignment, variance reduction, and functional coordination. COMA counterfactual credit holds other agents fixed while marginalizing one action (Foerster et al., 2018), while leave-one-out baselines and common random numbers are standard tools for variance reduction (Ahmadian et al., 2024; Ng & Jordan, 2000). PCGrad/CAGrad manipulate conflicting parameter gradients across objectives (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021). F LOW CRED instead separates statistical credit across the stochastic prompt-policy/generator hierarchy and measures functional overlap through same-state conditioning responses in the generator’s native output space.

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Protocol and controlled comparisons Setup. Our primary system uses LTX-2.5 (HaCohen et al., 2026; Lightricks, 2026), a Qwen3.5-9B prompt optimizer, Flow-GRPO, and K = M = 4 . Controlled variants share initialization, request stream, prompt sampling, matched seeds, reward, decoded-video budget, and optimization schedule; functional coordination adds same-state transition forwards but no extra video decode. The frozen training reward uses public, fixed scorers with weights 0.35 /0.30 /0.25 /0.10 : the Text-Alignment head of VideoReward (Liu et al., 2025b) for request faithfulness, frame-averaged VQAScore (CLIP-FlanT5-XXL) (Lin et al., 2024) for compositional correspondence, the temporal-consistency/text-alignment aggregate of VideoScore-v1.1 (He et al., 2024) for event realization, and the visual-/motion-quality aggregate of VideoReward as a bounded quality stabilizer. All scorers and calibration statistics are frozen before controlled training. Appendices D and D.3 specify checkpoints, frame sampling, calibration, and scorer-family robustness. 

Evaluation and comparisons. We report VBench (Huang et al., 2024), VBench-2.0 (Zheng et al., 2025), T2V-CompBench (Sun et al., 2025), and StoryEval (Wang et al., 2025) on the original request u with matched evaluation seeds. The hierarchical-credit-versus-Full comparison and the functional-coordination factorial use three independent training runs; other trainable baselines use two. The controlled chain spans prompt-only adaptation, a raw-request Flow-GRPO generator-only reference, PromptRL, UniGRPO, Matched Global-LOO, hierarchical credit, and Full. For methods originally reported with different backbones or training stacks, we implement the published objective in the common Qwen3.5/LTX-2.5 harness; the table therefore reports controlled common-harness results rather than copied published absolute scores. CAPE-T2V is positioned separately because its public LTX recipe uses sequential two-sided adaptation with additional video-derived caption supervision and a different released LTX backbone; Appendix D.5 gives the precise comparison scope. Uncertainty and data-integrity protocols are summarized in Appendices D.4, G, and J.

The increment remains positive in every tested backbone/updater setting, although its magnitude varies, indicating that functional coordination is not tied to a particular generator backbone or reward-update rule. Full keeps the decoded-video and reward-model-call budget identical to hierarchical credit; its additional generator work consists of non-decoded same-state transition probes, counted explicitly in Appendix I. Frozen-module swaps attribute measurable gains to both sides: substituting the Full generator into the hierarchical-credit pair adds +0.90 T2V-CompBench and substituting the Full prompt policy adds +0.72. In a 240-request blinded evaluation, Full is preferred over hierarchical credit on 134 versus 48 requests (58 ties; 73.6% of non-ties) and over PromptRL on 142 versus 46 (52 ties; 75.5% of non-ties). An independent VisionReward evaluator, never used in training or model selection, also prefers Full to hierarchical credit on 63.3% of 300 held-out matched pairs. Detailed annotation and replication protocols are in Appendices J and L. Taken together, these checks test complementary explanations of the primary gain. Transfer across a second backbone and alternative updater families probes dependence on the default generator stack; frozen-module swaps verify contributions from both trained modules; and blinded human judgments plus the held-out VisionReward evaluator test whether the improvement is confined to the training reward.

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented F LOW CRED , a framework for jointly post-training a prompt optimizer and a text-to-video generator without treating a shared terminal reward as undifferentiated supervision. Hierarchical credit assignment uses matched prompt–seed rollouts to separate prompt choice from prompt-fixed stochasticity, while functional coordination uses same-state response geometry for generator-aware prompt shaping and reliability-gated routing of the generator reward branch. Controlled comparisons show that the gains cannot be explained by stronger prompts, additional decoded rollouts, or generic update shrinkage alone, and evidence from repeated runs, mechanism analyses, human evaluation, and transfer studies is consistent with the intended division of labor between prompt-side conditioning and residual generator execution. More broadly, F LOW CRED suggests that joint post-training should distinguish where outcome variation arises from where trainable modules can realize overlapping functional changes : sharing a reward is not sufficient by itself; credit assignment and update coordination are separate design problems in cascaded generative systems.

## D Implementation and Evaluation Protocol 

D.1 Backbone, prompt optimizer, and training pool 

The primary generator is the public LTX-2.5 trainable DiT checkpoint from the offi-cial Lightricks/LTX-2.5 release (HaCohen et al., 2026; Lightricks, 2026), specif-ically diffusion_models/ltx-2.5-22b-dev-transformer-bf16.safetensors ,with its native flow parameterization and scheduler. The prompt optimizer is the instruction-tuned 

Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B checkpoint (Qwen Team, 2026), used as a text-generation policy; we do not use the separate Qwen3.5-9B-Base checkpoint. The main configuration uses full-parameter prompt-policy updates. HunyuanVideo-1.5 (Wu et al., 2025) provides backbone transfer, while 17 RVM (Choi et al., 2026) provides an alternative generator reward updater under the same LTX evaluation harness. The training pool contains 8,000 T2V requests. We build it from 4,800 programmatically instantiated compositional templates over entities, attributes, relations, counts, and actions; 2,000 independently generated motion/interaction requests; and 1,200 manually curated multi-event requests, then stratify the mixture across object/attribute binding, spatial relation, motion binding, action binding, interaction, numeracy, and longer event descriptions. Before training, requests are deduplicated against every benchmark split by normalized exact match, 5-gram MinHash (Jaccard threshold 0.80), and sentence-embedding cosine (threshold 0.92); no retained training request crosses any of these thresholds. Validation prompts are disjoint at the request level and are never used for benchmark reporting. Prompt candidates are constrained to preserve the user request, avoid unsupported entities, and remain within the generator context budget. Candidate validity is checked by a frozen Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B copy that is separate from the trainable prompt-policy weights. The checker receives the original request and one candidate and deterministically returns PASS/FAIL under a fixed rubric: a candidate fails if it changes required entities, counts, relations, actions, or event ordering, or introduces unsupported entities; descriptive camera, lighting, and appearance details are permitted only when they do not alter those constraints. Greedy decoding is used, and the same checker and rubric are applied to all prompt-side baselines and to additional support probes. Rejected candidates are resampled before video generation. For the theory above, πϕ denotes the effective distribution over accepted prompt actions induced by this fixed filtering rule. Instantiation with RVM. RVM (Choi et al., 2026) uses reward-weighted velocity matching together with an optional anchor toward a reference velocity field. In the reported RVM transfer, functional coordination is instantiated in this backend’s native velocity-output coordinates. At a sampled noised endpoint state (xt, t ) under executed prompt pk, write ht = vθ (xt, t, p k). We obtain the local reward-driven direction from the reward-weighted velocity branch, qRt = −∇ ht LRVM ,reward , absorbing the backend’s scalar timestep weight into qRt and using the Euclidean metric in ht coordinates. Alternative-prompt responses use the identical noised state and time under the detached behavior snapshot,

Instantiation with differentiable reward flow. We additionally instantiate functional coordination on a Diffusion-DRF differentiable reward-flow backend (Wang et al., 2026b). Following the public 20 DRF setup, the critic is frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B ; ten uniformly sampled video frames are used for VLM feedback, and gradients are truncated to the final three denoising steps. At a routed state we take the differentiable reward gradient with respect to the backend transition output as qRt and use its native Euclidean output metric for gRt . Alternative-prompt responses are evaluated at the identical state under the detached generator snapshot. The same trust clip, trace-normalized ridge operator, and held-out support gate are then applied before backpropagating the routed reward direction. Critic weights, frame sampling, the three-step backpropagation window, gradient checkpointing, and non-reward stabilization terms are held fixed between hierarchy-only and Full. This experiment tests whether cross-module routing remains useful when the generator already receives dense differentiable feedback rather than a scalar policy-gradient advantage. 

Backend applicability. Functional coordination requires two backend-local objects: (i) an output coordinate in which changing text conditioning at a fixed state yields a response vector, and (ii) a local improvement covector qRt supplied by the generator updater. Flow-GRPO, RVM, and differentiable reward flow provide both directly. A preference objective can be compatible if its implementation exposes a differentiable state-local pairwise loss, in which case the loss gradient can play the role of 

qRt ; a purely trajectory-level preference update with no local improvement covector is outside the current formulation. Likewise, closed API-only generators are outside our target setting because joint generator post-training itself already assumes access to internal transitions and parameters. 

D.4 Benchmark protocol 

VBench (Huang et al., 2024) and VBench-2.0 (Zheng et al., 2025) use their official aggre-gate/component definitions. T2V-CompBench (Sun et al., 2025) uses its official compositional dimensions and reported aggregate. StoryEval (Wang et al., 2025) follows the same official event-completion protocol for every generator updater and backbone. Every checkpoint uses identical resolution, duration, scheduler steps, negative-prompt convention, and three fixed generation seeds per benchmark request. The three scores for a request are averaged first; official benchmark aggregation is then applied to those request-level means. This preserves matched stochasticity while preventing seed count from being treated as sample size. We distinguish three sources of uncertainty: training-run variation, within-checkpoint generation randomness, and request sampling. The headline hierarchical-credit-versus-Full comparison uses three independent training runs; the functional-coordination factorial uses the same three-run protocol, and other trainable baselines use two independent runs. Confidence intervals for the headline increment are computed on paired run-level deltas using a Student-t interval; request-level paired bootstrap intervals are retained as a within-run sensitivity analysis. Generation seeds are averaged within each checkpoint and are never treated as independent training replicates. The same checkpoint and seed definitions are used across all controlled comparisons.

Preference pattern. The request-level majority analysis favors Full in both primary comparisons. Against HC, Full wins 134 requests versus 48 baseline wins, with 58 ties; conditioning on non-ties gives a 73.6% Full win rate. Against PromptRL, the corresponding counts are 142 / 52 / 46 and a 28 75.5% non-tie win rate. The Holm-corrected exact tests are significant in both comparisons, while Fleiss’ κ values of 0.51 and 0.53 indicate moderate agreement rather than near-deterministic annotator consensus. 

Where human gains concentrate. The largest descriptive gap occurs on composition/event com-pletion: Full is preferred on 62.9% of requests against HC and 65.8% against PromptRL. Faithfulness improves more modestly (53.3% and 55.0%), and motion/visual preference remains closer to parity (50.8% and 52.9%). This pattern is consistent with the automatic results, where the largest gains ap-pear on compositional and multi-event benchmarks, while also showing that the preference advantage is not driven solely by a generic visual-quality shift.
