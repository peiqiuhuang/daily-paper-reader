Abstract 

Fake news detection under low resource conditions is challenged by the scarcity of labeled data and the difficulty of capturing subtle deceptive patterns. In this work, we focus on low resource fake news detection, where only a small labeled set is available for training, while evaluation is performed under a fixed and unchanged test protocol. This setting is practically motivated by the high cost and ambiguity of expert fact-checking, as well as the rapid emergence of new topics and domains that are weakly covered by ex-isting benchmarks, which together make large-scale, fully supervised training difficult to sustain. Existing data augmentation methods, such as synonym substitution, generative adversarial networks, and large language model (LLM)-based generation, often produce fluent but overly generic content, lacking the task-specific relevance needed for accurate detection. To address this, we propose SharP , a boundary-aware text generation frame-work guided by soft and hard prompts, which leverages LLMs as tools adapted to produce task-specific synthetic samples. SharP employs a dual prompt mechanism: hard prompts provide structured guidance and factual constraints, while soft prompts introduce learned semantic patterns from the data. These two types of prompts are combined to guide the generation process, enabling the model to produce samples that closely align with the characteristics of news. To further enhance detection performance, we incorporate a boundary-aware strategy that steers generation toward areas where the classifier is less confident, helping to clarify subtle distinctions between real and fake content. In addi-tion, we adopt a dual objective optimization that balances semantic alignment with the source data and classifier feedback. This encourages the generation of samples that are both domain-consistent and helpful for refining decision boundaries, ultimately improving model robustness and generalization in low resource fake news detection.

1. Introduction 

The proliferation of fake news has emerged as a pressing global issue, driven by the unprecedented speed and reach of information dissemination enabled by social media platforms. Misinformation can severely distort public perception and trigger widespread consequences across domains such as politics, economics, and public health. Notable real-world examples include coordinated disinformation campaigns during elections and the circulation of false medical claims during the COVID-19 pandemic. Given its broad societal impact, fake news detection has become a focal research area across natural language processing, information retrieval, and computational journalism. In recent years, a variety of techniques have been developed to tackle fake news de-tection, including textual content analysis, social propagation modeling, and multimodal fusion methods. However, the effectiveness of such models fundamentally relies on access to large-scale, high-quality labeled datasets. While existing benchmark datasets have facilitated significant progress, they remain limited in coverage, especially for emerging misinformation topics and early-stage detection tasks. Manual annotation of fake news is expensive, domain-specific, and subject to ambiguity, making the scalability of supervised learning approaches a persistent challenge. Consequently, enhancing model performance under low resource conditions has become a critical concern. Data augmentation has emerged as a practical strategy to alleviate this bottleneck by expanding training sets with synthetic or transformed examples. Traditional text augmentation methods, such as synonym substitution, function word deletion, and back-translation, have shown effectiveness in improving model generalization (Kapusta et al., 2024). More recent efforts have introduced contrastive learning (Hua et al., 2023), mul-timodal synthesis, and GAN-based generation to further enrich the diversity of training data. With the advent of large-scale pre-trained language models, such as GPT and T5, researchers have begun to explore their potential for generating high-quality augmented samples in NLP tasks (Choi et al., 2025). These models offer strong semantic representa-tion and generative capabilities, presenting a new frontier for data augmentation in fake news detection. Nevertheless, existing LLM-based augmentation techniques often treat the genera-tion process as a general-purpose paraphrasing task, lacking task-specific alignment and domain-level control. While these methods produce grammatically correct and fluent samples, they frequently fail to capture the deceptive stylistic patterns and subtle lin-guistic cues that distinguish fake news from real news. Moreover, most approaches do not consider the evolving weaknesses of the detection model during training. This results in a gap between the augmented data and the actual regions in the input space where the model is uncertain or error-prone. Several recent studies have highlighted both the potential and the limitations of LLMs in this domain. Chen et al. (2025) empirically demonstrated that LLMs are effective at identifying textual misinformation but struggle to model propagation structures and social context. Raza et al. (2025) compared BERT-like encoders with autoregressive LLMs, finding that the former achieve better classification accuracy while the latter offer stronger robustness under noisy inputs. Liu et al. (2025) proposed a few-shot LLM-based framework that integrates external evidence and reasoning chains to enhance detection under ambiguity. These works collectively point to the need for more targeted LLM utilization strategies in fake news detection. This paper addresses a central question: How can LLMs be guided to generate aug-mented samples that are consistent with the domain, preserve the original labels, and 

2effectively inform the classifier, thereby improving fake news detection in low resource settings? We argue that effective augmentation must satisfy three key requirements: maintaining semantic fidelity to preserve the true labels, ensuring domain specificity to capture deceptive traits, and being sensitive to the model’s uncertain predictions in order to improve classification performance. To this end, we propose SharP , a boundary-aware text generation framework guided by soft and hard prompts. SharP transforms LLMs into task-aligned generators through prompt engineering, active boundary sampling, and dual objective optimization. The core idea is to control both how and where samples are generated: not only by instructing the model to preserve stylistic and semantic constraints, but also by strategically augmenting high-uncertainty regions in the classifier’s decision space. Specifically, we develop a dual prompt architecture that combines structured hard prompts , which encode generation rules to enforce factual stance and stylistic fidelity, with 

soft prompt that are continuously optimized to inject domain-aware inductive bias into the LLM’s attention layers. This enables fine-grained control over the generation process without altering the model’s parameters. In addition, we introduce a boundary-sensitive dynamic augmentation strategy, which integrates an active learning loop to identify sam-ples close to the classifier’s decision boundary. These samples are often associated with the uncertainty of the model and provide the most informative training signals. By fo-cusing augmentation on these regions, SharP not only increases data diversity but also enhances model discrimination where it is most needed. Finally, we propose a dual objec-tive optimization mechanism that jointly enforces feature-level semantic alignment and classification performance. The first objective ensures that augmented samples remain close to the originals in the feature space, maintaining semantic fidelity. The second uses the classifier’s error feedback on generated samples to iteratively refine the soft prompts, guiding the augmentation process to improve task relevance. In this work, we define low resource fake news detection as a label-scarce training regime instantiated by restricting the amount of labeled training data. Concretely, we construct low resource settings by subsampling the labeled training set at multiple ratios, yielding progressively larger labeled pools while keeping the overall experimental setting consistent. All methods are trained under the same sampling ratios to enable a controlled comparison across different levels of supervision scarcity. Taken together, SharP represents a principled integration of prompt-based control, uncertainty-driven sampling, and optimization-aware generation. By aligning augmen-tation with domain knowledge and classifier behavior, it substantially enhances model robustness and generalization in low resource fake news detection scenarios. The contributions of this work are summarized as follows: • A prompt-guided LLM-based augmentation framework was developed, in which structured hard prompts were integrated with learnable soft prompts to enable domain-consistent and label-preserving generation across diverse news topics. This framework was implemented and empirically validated on the PHEME dataset. • A boundary-aware sample selection strategy was proposed, leveraging classifier un-certainty to dynamically guide augmentation toward informative regions in the decision space. • A dual objective optimization scheme was designed to jointly promote semantic fidelity and discriminative enhancement, enabling end-to-end coordination between 3the augmentation module and the detection model. The contribution of this scheme was validated via hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, confirming its role in enhanc-ing model performance. The source code for SharP is available at https://github.com/Fujizzz/SharP .

2. Related Work 

2.1. Rumor Detection 

Rumor detection on social media has evolved rapidly as misinformation becomes more prevalent and time-sensitive. An early milestone in neural rumor detection was to model textual signals for timely identification; for example, Gao et al. (2020) combined a character-level bidirectional language model with stacked LSTMs to capture sequential cues for early detection. A subsequent milestone was the shift from plain-text modeling toward leveraging richer supervision signals, incorporating heterogeneous user-generated context and multimodal evidence. For example, Qian et al. (2021) proposed a cross-modal detection framework that integrates textual content, social comments, and image-level sentiment information to improve robustness under diverse scenarios. More recently, the literature has increasingly emphasized robustness under limited supervision, where labeled rumors are scarce and newly emerging events deviate from past distributions. To alleviate label scarcity, Sun et al. (2022) combined self-supervised learning with augmentation to improve representation stability in rumor detection. In parallel, feature fusion architectures have been advanced for platform-specific settings; Wang et al. (2022) studied Chinese social media and combined textual, visual, and user-level features via ERNIE and VGG-19 to strengthen multimodal integration. Overall, the field has progressed from sequence-based textual modeling to multimodal and context-aware learning, and further toward augmentation- and self-supervision-driven robustness. Despite substantial progress, rumor detection still faces a persistent bottle-neck in low resource and evolving-event scenarios, where annotation is costly and am-biguous and models must learn from limited, shifting evidence—motivating the need for task-aligned augmentation under scarce supervision. 

2.2. Benchmark Datasets for Fake News and Rumor Detection 

To situate our study on low resource fake news detection, we briefly review repre-sentative benchmarks that are widely used to evaluate models under controlled label budgets. A major line of work focuses on social-platform rumor verification, where the input often comes with conversational or event structures, such as the breaking-news threads in PHEME (Zubiaga et al., 2016) and the event-centric Twitter15/16 bench-marks (Ma et al., 2017a,b); complementary to English Twitter benchmarks, the Weibo rumor dataset provides a widely used Chinese microblog testbed, reflecting the linguistic and platform diversity of misinformation benchmarks (Ma et al., 2016). Another line targets claim/news-based fake news detection, including LIAR with fine-grained veracity labels for short fact-checked claims (Wang, 2017) and FakeNewsNet, which pairs news articles with associated social context from PolitiFact and GossipCop (Shu et al., 2020). In addition, CoAID offers a domain-specific benchmark for COVID-19 healthcare misin-formation (Cui and Lee, 2020), enabling evaluation under rapidly evolving topics. Im-portantly, these datasets differ in annotation granularity, instance structure (e.g., claim-, article-, or thread-level), and available auxiliary signals; therefore, low resource settings are typically instantiated by subsampling the labeled pool while keeping the evaluation 4protocol fixed. This makes them well-suited for assessing whether augmentation meth-ods can improve generalization when labeled supervision is scarce, which is the primary setting addressed in our work.

2.3. Data Augmentation for Fake News Detection 

Data augmentation is a widely adopted strategy for improving text classification under limited labeled data, and it has been extensively explored in fake news detec-tion. Early augmentation methods relied on syntactic or lexical perturbations. Back-Translation (Sennrich et al., 2016) generates semantically preserved paraphrases through round-trip translation, and EDA (Wei and Zou, 2019) further popularized lightweight op-erations such as synonym replacement, random insertion, swapping, and deletion. Sub-sequent work investigated interpolation-style augmentation at the representation level; MixText (Chen et al., 2020) extends the Mixup principle to textual inputs by mixing latent features to synthesize new training instances. In addition, Cheng et al. (2020) combined back-translation with adversarial training to promote diversity and robustness. A major milestone in text augmentation is the emergence of large pre-trained lan-guage models, which shifts the paradigm from heuristic perturbations toward controllable, prompt-guided generation. LM-CPPF (Abaskohi et al., 2023) explored prompt-based few-shot paraphrasing with large models such as GPT-3 and OPT-175B, and Kesgin and Amasyali (2024) proposed leveraging the fill-mask capability within Transformer-based models for augmentation. Despite these advances, existing augmentation methods often produce fluent yet task-agnostic samples and underutilize domain-specific deceptive cues, underscoring the need for controllable, label-preserving, and domain-aligned generation in low resource fake news detection.

Early work demonstrated that opti-mizing continuous prompt representations can effectively steer model behavior with a small number of trainable parameters. A milestone in this line is Prefix-Tuning (Li and Liang, 2021), which learns task-specific continuous prefix vectors to condition genera-tion while keeping the backbone frozen. As shown in Figure 1, the proposed model consists of two core modules: the classifier module and the data augmentation module. The classifier module integrates a feature extractor and a classifier, responsible for identi-fying the authenticity of fake news. The data augmentation module, centered on an LLM, combines hard prompts and soft prompts to guide the model in generating augmented text, aiming to alleviate the problem of scarce training samples and improve the model’s detection ability and generalization performance in low resource environments.

3.2. Active Learning Module 

We design a classifier model integrated with a feature extractor and combine it with an active learning strategy to select samples near the model’s decision boundary, i.e., those for which the model exhibits the highest uncertainty in its predictions. These samples are highly valuable for model training and will be used for data augmentation in subsequent steps, further enhancing model performance. To effectively extract emotional features, we construct emotion representations based on five categories of emotional lexicons: Emotion, Sentiment, Morality, Imageability, and Hyperbole. We use the NRC Emotion Lexicon (Mohammad and Turney, 2013) to ex-tract 8 emotional features, and the NRC Sentiment Lexicon and the Moral Foundations Dictionary (Graham et al., 2009) to extract emotional and moral features (2 and 10, re-spectively). Additionally, we leverage the MRC Psycholinguistic Database (Wilson, 1988) to extract 2 Imageability-related features and construct emotional features by analyzing approximately 350 hyperbole words. Finally, these multi-dimensional emotional features are integrated into a unified emo-tional vector, representing the diverse emotional information in the news. We design a dual feature extraction model that combines sentiment and textual fea-tures based on feature extraction. The model comprises two key components: the first component uses Convolutional Neural Networks to extract word-level latent representa-tions of the text ( vword ). Specifically, the input text sequence is encoded by BERT as 

S = {w1, w 2, . . . , w n}, where wi is the embedding of the i-th word. The CNN applies multi-scale convolutions to the word embeddings, using kernel sizes of 3, 4, and 5 to extract vertical features. The max pooling layer selects the maximum value from each 8feature map generated by the convolution operations, forming a pooled feature vector and producing the text’s latent representation ( vword ): 

vword = MaxPool (CN N (wi)) . (4) The second component uses a Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit to capture senti-ment flow in the text, producing a sentiment representation ( vaffect ). Here, e represents the sentiment vector: 

vaffect = Bi-GRU (e). (5) After obtaining the word-level representation ( vword ) and sentiment features ( vaffect ), we combine them using element-wise multiplication to generate a unified text-sentiment representation ( vfuse ). This step deeply fuses sentiment and text information, enhancing the model’s ability to perceive sentiment within the text: 

vfuse = vword ⊙ vaffect . (6) The classifier, which utilizes the features extracted by the feature extractor, distin-guishes between real and fake samples. It is a multilayer perceptron consisting of a fully connected layer and a softmax layer. During the pre-training phase, the feature extractor and classifier are jointly trained to learn initial feature representations and classification abilities. In the fine-tuning phase, an active learning strategy is applied to guide the sample selection process. Based on the classifier’s current predictions, we select samples near the decision bound-ary, i.e., those with the highest predictive uncertainty. 3.3. Data Augmentation Module 

Figure 3 illustrates the architecture of the data augmentation module, whose core lies in the training of soft prompts. By learning soft prompts, implicit knowledge from the fake news domain, such as high-frequency argumentation structures, is injected into the Transformer attention mechanism. This effectively guides the large model to perform data augmentation on news samples from specific domains, thereby enhancing the domain adaptability of the large model and achieving fine-grained generation control. Specifically, we first define a list of hard prompts containing various instructions to guide the large model in generating texts with different styles. These hard prompts are combined with the input text, and after passing through the embedding layer of the large model, we obtain the embedded representation of the input text. Next, we initialize a soft prompt vector and concatenate it with the input text’s embedding vector, using the concatenated result as input to the large model. The soft prompt vector, serving as prefix information of the input text, after training, will contain guiding information specific to the task context within the large model.

The training pipeline consists of two major stages: pre-training and fine-tuning. The model architecture comprises two key modules: a classifier and a data augmentation module. To maximize detection performance, we separately optimize the classifier and the augmentation module during each stage. 

Pre-training Stage: In the pre-training stage, we begin by training the classifier. The feature extractor encodes both textual and emotional information into feature rep-resentations, which serve as inputs to the classifier. The classifier is trained to minimize detection loss, enabling the model to distinguish fake news from real news based on the initial labeled dataset and laying a foundation for subsequent fine-tuning and augmenta-tion. Following this, the data augmentation module is trained. The goal of this stage is to initialize and optimize the soft prompt vectors, enabling the language model to generate high-quality augmented texts conditioned on the input. Through a dual objec-tive optimization mechanism, the soft prompts enforce semantic consistency between the generated and original texts, while also increasing the sample density near the decision boundary to support the classifier in later stages.

This prompt-tuning step adapts Ts to the current data distribution and makes the generation process more task- and domain-aligned. 15 4. Experiments 

In the experimental section, we aim to address the following key questions: • Does the SharP model demonstrate significant performance advantages in low re-source environments while maintaining satisfactory performance under varying re-source conditions? • Does the model exhibit good generalization ability across news data from different domains? • Can the texts generated by the data augmentation module effectively enhance the classifier’s detection performance? • Do different hard prompts and other hyperparameter choices have a significant impact on the model’s performance? We systematically evaluate SharP by first describing the experimental setup and then conducting comprehensive tests across various conditions to validate its effectiveness in fake news detection. 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Datasets: In Group A, we evaluate our model on five distinct event-specific datasets: 

Germanwings-crash , Sydneysiege , Ottawashooting , Charliehebdo , and Ferguson . These subsets are derived from the PHEME dataset (Zubiaga et al., 2016), which consists of multimodal Twitter data, including users’ social information, posted content, and an-notated labels. The PHEME dataset is particularly well-suited for fake news detection tasks, as it not only offers rich textual content but also provides sentiment annotations and propagation path information for each tweet. In our experimental design, we pro-portionally divide the training samples within each subset to comprehensively test model performance under varying sample sizes and across diverse news scenarios. In addition to the event-specific evaluation on PHEME, we further conduct a cross-benchmark study (Group B) on two widely used misinformation benchmarks, LIAR (Wang, 2017) and Twitter15/16 (Ma et al., 2017a,b), to assess the robustness of SharP across datasets with different data characteristics. LIAR consists of short, fact-checked politi-cal claims annotated with fine-grained veracity labels, which are commonly consolidated into binary labels for fake news detection. Twitter15/16 are event-centric rumor de-tection benchmarks built from Twitter conversations with reply structures, providing complementary evaluation beyond the PHEME breaking-news threads. Experimental Details: To comprehensively evaluate the model’s performance under varying resource conditions, we divide the experimental dataset into three subsets: the first group is the labeled data pool used for pre-training, the second group is a fixed-size unlabeled data pool, and the third group is the test data pool. During the pre-training phase, the model is trained on the labeled data pool to acquire fundamental text classification capabilities. Subsequently, the model proceeds to the fine-tuning phase, where it undergoes further optimization using the unlabeled data pool. In this phase, an active learning strategy is employed to select samples near the decision boundary for labeling and training, thereby enhancing the model’s performance through targeted sample selection. In the experiment, we operationalize low resource fake news detection by constraining the size of the labeled training pool. Concretely, for each dataset, we construct a series of label budgets by randomly sampling {1%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%, 35%, 40%, 45%, 50%, 70%} of instances from the labeled pool for training. To reduce sampling bias under small budgets, we adopt stratified sampling to preserve the fake/real ratio (and keep the split as balanced as possible). We then evaluate performance across these budget levels to assess robustness under varying degrees of supervision scarcity, with particular attention to whether the proposed augmentation strategy yields consistent gains when labeled data are limited. Unless otherwise specified, all other settings are kept unchanged across budget levels. 

4.2. Baseline 

In the baseline experiments, we select a series of classical and advanced fake news detection methods as baseline models. We evaluate and compare their performance with the proposed method under the same datasets and experimental settings, with a pri-mary focus on assessing the model robustness and generalization ability in low resource environments. Here are the baseline models we selected: 

HAN (2016) : A fake news detection method based on the Hierarchical Attention Network (HAN). This method utilizes a hierarchical attention mechanism to model dif-ferent levels of information in news text (such as words, sentences, and paragraphs), thereby capturing essential semantic features. HAN effectively focuses on critical infor-mation within the text and combines structural features of long texts, enhancing the model’s classification performance in fake news detection tasks (Yang et al., 2016). 

dEFEND (2019) : A multimodal fake news detection model that integrates textual content and user comments. dEFEND uses explainable attention mechanisms to identify 17 suspicious words and responses, enhancing model interpretability and robustness (Shu et al., 2019). 

HMCAN (2021) : A fake news detection method based on a hierarchical multimodal contextual attention network, which integrates text and social network comment informa-tion. It leverages CNNs and hierarchical attention to extract features from multimodal data, aiming to improve classification accuracy (Qian et al., 2021). 

DDT (2025) : A fake news detection method based on active learning, which en-hances the model’s discrimination ability by combining textual features and emotional features. In addition, DDT employs an active learning strategy, dynamically selecting uncertain samples near the decision boundary for manual annotation and fine-tuning during training. This approach maximizes the model’s learning efficiency under limited labeled data conditions. (Hu et al., 2025) 

DeepSeek (2024) : A general-purpose language model based on the Transformer ar-chitecture, incorporating various structural optimization techniques, such as RMSNorm, SwiGLU activation function, and RoPE (Rotary Positional Embedding), to improve per-formance in long-text modeling and reasoning efficiency. The model is pre-trained on large-scale bilingual (Chinese and English) corpora, demonstrating strong cross-linguistic understanding and generalization capabilities. In this study’s baseline experiments, we fine-tune DeepSeek-7B-Base using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on data with differ-ent resource proportions to evaluate its performance and adaptability in the fake news detection task (Liu et al., 2024).

4.4. Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis   

> (a) Dual Objective Optimization Weight (b) Hard Prompt Selection
> (c) Number of Generated Text (d) Soft Prompt Length Figure 7: Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis

To further verify the robustness and stability of our model, we conducted a detailed hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, aiming to evaluate the impact of different hyperpa-rameter settings on model performance. Hyperparameters play a crucial role in training deep learning models as they directly determine the convergence speed, generalization ability, and final detection performance. Therefore, selecting and adjusting hyperpa-22 rameters appropriately not only enhances the model’s performance but also ensures its effectiveness across different tasks and datasets. In this experiment, we focused on the following key hyperparameters: the choice of hard prompt categories, the size of soft prompt initialization, the number of generated data augmentation texts, and the weight settings of dual objective optimization. Ad-justing each hyperparameter can influence the training dynamics of the model, thereby affecting the final detection outcomes. By systematically analyzing these key hyperparameters, we identified which hyper-parameters have the most significant impact on model performance and gained deeper insights into their functional mechanisms. This analysis provides a scientific basis and optimization direction for hyperparameter selection in practical applications. Ultimately, the hyperparameter sensitivity analysis enabled us to identify the most suitable hyper-parameter combination for this task, further enhancing the model’s overall performance and generalization ability. Figure 7 presents the experimental results of the hyperparameter analysis. In the anal-ysis of hard prompt category selection, we observed that performance differences between different hard prompt categories were minimal when the number of training samples was large. However, when the number of training samples was small, the performance of certain hard prompt categories was significantly lower. We hypothesize that this discrepancy may result from the classifier’s preference for specific text styles. For example, hard prompt 3 (which uses a more aggressive tone) performed better than hard prompt 4 (which uses a more declarative tone), likely because fake news often employs a more provocative and attention-grabbing style, making such texts easier to identify during classification. As the amount of training data increases, the influence of this preference on model performance gradually diminishes, leading to a reduction in performance differences. Across the evaluated sample pool proportions, hard prompt 1, which uses simpler and more straightforward expressions, achieved the best or joint-best accuracy in most settings. We therefore adopted hard prompt 1 as the default hard prompt in our experi-ments. In the hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, we evaluated the hyperparameters under varying training data proportions (specifically 5%, 10%, 30%, and 50%) to assess their impact on model performance across different resource levels. The experimental results indicate that as the initialization length of the soft prompt increases, the model performance gradually improves, reaching a peak before subsequently declining. This phenomenon can be attributed to the following reasons: when the soft prompt length is too short, it fails to adequately encode guiding information for the large model, resulting in lower performance. On the other hand, when the soft prompt length is too long, it occupies a substantial proportion of the input after concatenation with the model’s textual input. This excessive additional information hinders the model’s ability to effectively understand the task objective, leading to a performance decline. In the hyperparameter analysis of the number of generated data augmentation texts, we observed that as the generation number increases, the model performance initially rises briefly, reaches a peak, and then continuously declines. Notably, when the training data volume is relatively low, the performance peak occurs with a delay. This phenomenon can be attributed to the following reasons: when the training data is relatively sufficient, an excessive number of augmented texts can result in a surplus of semantically similar training data in the next training round, thereby inhibiting per-23 formance improvement. In contrast, when the training data volume is low, the increase in generated text quantity enhances data diversity. In this case, the addition of more training data primarily contributes to performance improvement, resulting in a delayed arrival of the performance peak. Finally, in the hyperparameter analysis of dual objective optimization weight set-tings, we found that models using only a single loss function performed worse under all three sample conditions compared to models that combined both loss functions. When appropriate weights were set and both loss functions were jointly adjusted, the model performance improved significantly. This result demonstrates the effectiveness of dual objective functions in optimizing the quality of generated samples and enhancing the overall model performance. 

5. Conclusion and Future Work 

In this paper, we proposed an LLM-based text data augmentation technique guided by dual prompt engineering, which combines structured hard prompts with learnable soft prompts. This approach was successfully applied to the task of fake news detection under low resource settings. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly improves the classifier’s discriminative performance by generating diverse samples that are consistent with the domain and preserve the original labels. Particularly in low resource environments, our framework significantly enhances detection precision and overall classification accuracy by supplying diverse, high-quality augmented training samples. Beyond empirical performance, our method provides a flexible and model independent solution that can be adapted to various LLM architectures and training setups, making it applicable to a wide range of real world misinformation detection scenarios. In addition, the sample selection strategy, which focuses on uncertain prediction regions, enables more efficient use of annotation resources in active learning settings, further demonstrating the method’s practical value. In future work, we plan to extend this augmentation framework to a wider range of text classification tasks, including sentiment analysis, topic classification, and intent de-tection. We are also interested in evaluating the method’s effectiveness across multilingual datasets and under distribution shifts. In addition, we aim to explore the integration of multimodal data, such as images and user metadata, to further enhance the model’s con-textual understanding and scalability in practical applications like social media content moderation and news verification systems.
