# A Fast and High-Accuracy Finite-Time Sliding-Mode Algorithm for Computing Local Stackelberg Equilibria in Nonlinear Bilevel Games* 

Abdelhafid Zenati 1,2, Dave D Muir 1, Kamal Youcef-Toumi 2

Abstract — Computing local Stackelberg equilibria in non-linear sequential games is challenging because gradient-based best-response methods may diverge, cycle, or converge to stationary points that do not satisfy the required equilibrium conditions. These failures can result from curvature asymmetry, unstable response mappings, and lack of invariance with respect to the follower’s best-response manifold. This paper introduces the Stackelberg Sliding-Mode Algorithm (SSMA), a control-theoretic framework that recasts Stackelberg equilibrium com-putation as a manifold-stabilization problem. The follower and leader first-order optimality conditions define Stackelberg sliding manifolds, and sliding-mode dynamics drive the iterates to these manifolds in finite time. The follower curvature and leader reduced-curvature conditions determine manifold attractivity, making valid local Stackelberg equilibria attractive while rejecting stationary points that fail the second-order equilibrium conditions. The resulting approach integrates finite-time manifold stabilization, Stackelberg-consistent dynamics, and curvature-based equilibrium selection within a unified framework. Across 500 nonconvex Monte Carlo trials, SSMA achieved 100% success, reached the prescribed tolerance in a median of 26 iterations, compared with 456 for the nearest fully successful baselines, and attained terminal residuals near 10 −16 ,while converging to a valid local Stackelberg equilibrium. 

I. INTRODUCTION Stackelberg games provide a foundational framework for modelling hierarchical decision-making in economics, se-curity, biology, energy systems, and engineering [1]–[3]. The defining feature is the leader’s ability to anticipate the follower’s reaction and optimise accordingly. This strategic asymmetry has been widely exploited in applications ranging from security and allocation problems to cyber-physical systems and energy markets [4], [5]. Recent studies highlight the importance of accurate equilibrium computation in these settings, particularly when the interaction between decision makers involves nonconvex objectives, implicit constraints, and potentially unstable responses [6], [7]. Despite this breadth of application, computing Stackelberg equilibria remains difficult, especially in nonlinear or non-convex settings [7], [8]. Classical reformulation approaches treat the follower’s optimality conditions as complementarity constraints embedded in the leader’s problem. These meth-ods can be effective in structured environments, such as linear–quadratic or convex models, but they often rely on

When these assumptions fail, reformulation-based algorithms can converge to poor solutions or become computationally intractable, especially when the feasible reaction set is complex or multi-valued [9]. Further, the computational hardness of robust or constrained variants of Stackelberg equilibria has been demonstrated in several recent studies, revealing the sensitivity of leader strategies to small deviations in follower behaviour [10]. The limitations of gradient-based best-response dynam-ics have also been extensively documented: these methods may diverge, produce oscillations, or converge to stationary points that do not correspond to valid Stackelberg equi-libria [11]. The underlying instability is linked to curva-ture asymmetry, ill-conditioned response mappings, and the general lack of invariance of gradient iterates to the true best-response manifold [12]. First-order schemes, including gradient-based, optimistic-gradient (OGDA) and nested best-response methods (BR-GD), seek to improve the under-lying descent dynamics but may remain sensitive to the geometry and conditioning of the follower response [13]. More structured approaches, such as Follow-the-Ridge (FR), incorporate the local response geometry to track the follower solution manifold, while curvature-aware variants such as HessianFR further exploit second-order information [14]. Attempts to circumvent these issues using differentiation through equilibria and implicit-function-theorem (IFT) meth-ods provide direct estimates of the leader gradient through the follower response, but can require solving or regular-izing Hessian systems and may become sensitive to poor conditioning [15]. Higher-order approaches, including cubic-regularized Newton schemes (CRN), further exploit curva-ture to improve local equilibrium computation [16]. More generally, differentiation through equilibria, implicit-function methods, and end-to-end learning offer greater flexibility but still depend on uniqueness assumptions or high-quality equilibrium-selection oracles, which are rarely available in general nonconvex settings [17], [18]. Addressing these challenges requires a framework that directly stabilises the conditions defining a local Stackelberg equilibrium. Motivated by this need, we propose a control-theoretic perspective grounded in sliding-mode concepts [19]. A local Stackelberg equilibrium is characterised by two first-order conditions, Follower Stationarity and Leader Stationarity, together with two second-order conditions, Fol-lower Curvature and Leader Reduced Curvature, see Def-inition 2. Here, Leader Stationarity is defined by the total arXiv:2610.11718v1 [math.OC] 8 Oct 2026 derivative of the reduced leader objective along the follower’s response, rather than by the partial gradient with respect to the leader variable. We formulate the two stationarity conditions as Stackelberg sliding manifolds, whose joint zero set defines the first-order Stackelberg critical set. The update law is then designed so that these manifolds become attractive only when the corresponding Follower Curvature and Leader Reduced Curvature conditions hold. In this way, the dynamics are driven towards points satisfying both the first- and second-order requirements of a local Stackelberg equilibrium, while non-equilibrium stationary points are ex-cluded. This sliding-mode formulation provides fast finite-time reaching of the Stackelberg manifolds together with robust invariance once they are reached. Numerical results on representative bilevel problems demonstrate that SSMA does not merely reach a stationary point; it rapidly and reliably converges to a valid local Stackelberg equilibrium with near-machine-precision accuracy. VI. C ONCLUSION 

This paper introduced the Stackelberg Sliding Mode Al-gorithm (SSMA), a control-theoretic approach that refor-mulates the computation of local Stackelberg equilibria in nonlinear leader–follower games as a manifold-stabilization problem. SSMA constructs sliding manifolds from the fol-lower and leader first-order optimality conditions and em-ploys a discrete-time reaching law to drive the iterates toward these manifolds. The follower-curvature and leader reduced-curvature conditions determine manifold attractivity, enabling valid local Stackelberg equilibria to be selected while stationary points that fail the second-order equilibrium conditions are rejected. The theoretical analysis establishes finite-time reaching of the Stackelberg sliding manifolds un-der the stated assumptions. In 500 Monte Carlo trials, SSMA was the only evaluated method to simultaneously achieve 

100% success, near-machine-precision terminal residuals, rapid convergence, and satisfaction of the second-order con-ditions for a valid local Stackelberg equilibrium.
