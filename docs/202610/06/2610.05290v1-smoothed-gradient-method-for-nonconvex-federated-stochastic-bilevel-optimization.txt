Abstract 

In recent years, federated stochastic bilevel optimization has attracted increasing attention due to its wide range of applications in machine learning. To reduce the computational overhead associated with second-order Hessian and Jacobian matrices, several first-order methods have been proposed. However, existing methods typically impose restrictive assumptions on the lower-level function, suffer from a strong dependence on the condition number in their convergence rates, and require different learning-rate scales for variables across the upper- and lower-level problems, limiting their practical applicability and com-plicating hyperparameter tuning. To address these challenges, we propose a stochastic doubly smoothed gradient method for nonconvex federated stochastic bilevel optimization problems, which decouples the learning rates of upper- and lower-level variables and does not require a strongly-convex lower-level loss function. We establish rigorous theoretical guarantees for the proposed algorithm, demonstrating an im-proved convergence rate of O(κ15 /2/ϵ 5) and a communication complexity of O(κ4/ϵ 3), where κ denotes the condition number and ϵ represents the solution accuracy. Notably, these bounds exhibit significantly better dependence on the condition number κ than those of existing methods. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of our algorithm.

How-ever, stochastic smoothing in federated bilevel optimization, particularly doubly smoothed schemes, remains largely unexplored. This raises the following question: 

Can stochastic smoothing be effectively incorporated into federated bilevel optimization to simultaneously improve theoretical guarantees and practical performance? 

However, incorporating smoothing into federated bilevel optimization is nontrivial. Unlike standard minimax problems, federated bilevel optimization involves tightly coupled upper- and lower-level variables together with a global lower-level problem. Moreover, the penalty-based first-order reformulation introduces additional interactions between the penalty and smoothing parameters, making the appropriate smooth-ing strategy unclear. Stochastic gradient estimation and periodic communication further complicate the algorithm design. Theoretically, establishing convergence under the relaxed PL condition while improving condition-number dependence poses additional challenges. To address these challenges, we propose a stochastic doubly smoothed framework for federated bilevel optimization. Our method integrates smoothing into a penalty-based first-order reformulation for both the upper- and lower-level variables , yielding a fully first-order algorithm for solving Eq. (1.1). This formulation enables unified learning-rate scaling without explicit Jacobian or Hessian computations. • Algorithmic contribution: We propose a stochastic doubly smoothed framework for federated bilevel optimization and develop a fully first-order algorithm, termed FedDS-BVRFO . By smoothing both the upper- and lower-level updates, our method enables the same order of learning rates with respect to the condition number, simplifying hyperparameter tuning. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first stochastic doubly smoothed method for federated bilevel optimization with unified learning-rate scaling. 

• Theoretical contribution: We establish convergence guarantees under the PL condition on the lower-level objective, relaxing the strong-convexity assumption of existing federated bilevel methods. 2.1 Federated and Stochastic Bilevel Optimization 

Federated stochastic bilevel optimization has recently attracted growing attention due to its applications in privacy-preserving meta-learning and hyperparameter optimization [1, 15, 6, 34]. Compared with classical single-machine bilevel optimization [10, 14, 3], the federated setting introduces additional challenges due to the coupling between local upper-level objectives and a global lower-level problem. A primary line of work is based on approximate implicit differentiation techniques, such as Neumann-series-based estimators, leading to representative methods including FedNEST and its variants [32, 13, 35]. These approaches are further improved by incorporating momentum or variance-reduction strategies [9, 18] to enhance communication efficiency and training stability. More recently, fully first-order methods have been proposed via penalty-based Lagrangian reformulations [36, 40], which enable gradient-based updates without explicit second-order computations. In addition, several works have investigated nonconvex lower-level problems, primarily in the single-machine setting [29, 17, 2, 23, 24, 25]. These studies relax strong convexity assumptions on the lower-level problem, but their extension to federated settings remains largely unexplored. 

2.2 Smoothed Techniques 

Stochastic smoothing has been widely studied for stabilizing optimization dynamics and improving conver-gence behavior. [37] first introduced it into stochastic minimax optimization to mitigate gradient oscillations and ensure convergence to stationary solutions. Under the PL condition, [33] further showed that smoothing improves the condition-number dependence from O(κ3) to O(κ2). Subsequent works extend smoothing to more complex settings. In particular, [42] proposed doubly smoothed schemes for nonconvex–nonconcave problems, while [31] incorporated smoothing into federated stochastic minimax optimization, achieving an 

O(κ2) dependence. More recently, [39] extended smoothing to stochastic multi-level compositional mini-max problems, achieving an improved O(κ3/2) dependence when combined with variance reduction. Beyond minimax optimization, [24, 25] incorporated smoothing into Lagrangian methods for constrained bilevel op-timization. Note that our reformulation differs from those in recent works [24, 25]. Specifically, [24, 25] apply the smoothing technique only for the primal variable, while our method apply it to both the primal and dual variables in the reformulated minimax problem. Despite these advances, the role of stochastic smoothing in federated bilevel optimization, particularly the use of doubly smoothed schemes, remains largely unexplored.

4.1 Assumptions 

We adopt the following standard assumptions commonly used in federated stochastic bilevel optimization [16, 17, 2, 40, 36]. 

Assumption 4.1. For k ∈ { 1, · · · , K }, f (k)(·, ·) satisfies the mean-squared smoothness with the constant 

Lf > 0 and its second-order gradient satisfies the Lipschitz continuity with the constant ℓf > 0. In addition, the partial gradient with respect to the second argument is bounded as ∥∇ 2f (k)(·, ·)∥ ≤ Cf , where Cf > 0 is a constant. 

Assumption 4.2. For k ∈ { 1, · · · , K }, g(k)(·, ·) satisfies the mean-squared smoothness with the constant 

Lg > 0, and its second-order gradient satisfies the Lipschitz continuity with the constant ℓg > 0.

Assumption 4.3. For k ∈ { 1, · · · , K }, g(k)(·, ·) satisfies the μ-PL condition with respect to the second argument with the constant μ > 0. In addition, hρ(·, ·) = ρf (·, ·) + g(·, ·) also satisfies the μ-PL condition with respect to the second argument. 

Note that most federated bilevel optimization methods in the literature [32, 13, 9, 18, 40, 36] rely on the strong convexity of g(k)(·, ·) with respect to the second argument. In contrast, our federated analysis assumes only a μ-PL condition on the lower-level objective, which has recently been studied in single-machine bilevel optimization. Since the PL condition is strictly weaker than strong convexity, our framework applies to a broader range of practical scenarios. 

Assumption 4.4. For k ∈ { 1, · · · , K }, the stochastic gradients of f (k)(·, ·) and g(k)(·, ·) have variances upper bounded by σ2 where σ > 0 is a constant.

By contrast, the proposed smoothed formulation enables the updates of x, y, and z to operate under the same order of step sizes with respect to κ. This unified scaling substantially simplifies hyperparameter tuning and improves practical robustness.

# 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we proposed a stochastic doubly smoothed framework for federated bilevel optimization, leading to a fully first-order algorithm with unified learning-rate scaling. By integrating smoothing into a Lagrangian reformulation, the proposed method effectively stabilizes the coupled dynamics across variables and avoids expensive second-order computations. We further established convergence guarantees under relaxed nonconvex lower-level conditions and derived improved sample and communication complexities with favorable dependence on the condition number. Extensive experiments on benchmark tasks, including hyperparameter optimization and model pruning, demonstrate the efficiency and robustness of our approach compared to existing methods.
