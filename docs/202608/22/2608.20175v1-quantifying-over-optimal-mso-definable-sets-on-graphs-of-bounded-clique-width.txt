# Quantifying over Optimal MSO-Definable Sets on Graphs of Bounded Clique-Width 

Tatsuya Gima Hokkaido University gima@ist.hokudai.ac.jp 

Abstract 

We introduce AmCMSO , an extension of counting monadic second-order logic ( CMSO ) with predicates that refer to minimum- and maximum-value satisfying assignments. We establish fixed-parameter tractable model-checking meta-theorems for AmCMSO 1 on graphs of bounded clique-width and for AmCMSO 2 on graphs of bounded treewidth. These meta-theorems yield fixed-parameter tractable algorithms for several bilevel graph optimization problems, including interdiction and preassignment problems for solution uniquification, as well as algorithms for maximizing the diversity of optimal solutions without parameterizing by the optimum value. In contrast, allowing an optimality predicate to depend on an external set variable makes model checking hard for every level of the polynomial hierarchy, even on trees of fixed depth. 

# 1 Introduction 

Courcelle’s celebrated theorem [CMR00; Cou90] states that a decision problem whose feasibility can be expressed in monadic second-order logic with cardinality-modulo predicates ( CMSO ) can be solved in linear time on graphs of bounded treewidth or clique-width. This theorem has been extended to optimization problems through the LinECMSO framework, which solves problems of the following form in linear time on graphs of bounded clique-width when f is a linear function and ϕ(S) is a CMSO formula. Minimize (Maximize) f (|S|); Subject to ϕ(S).

This framework captures many combinatorial optimization problems, such as Minimum Ver-tex Cover , Minimum Dominating Set and its connected variants, vertex-deletion distance to a property definable by forbidden fixed minors, and many other problems [ALS91]. More-over, the framework extends to additive set functions that decompose into element-wise sums

Several variants of the framework capture optimization objectives other than cardinality minimization, including par-titioning [Rao07], K-best solutions [EK18], diversity maximization [Bas+22; DM26], and Lips-chitz continuity [GKY25]. On graphs of bounded treewidth, these frameworks can be extended to CMSO 2, which can describe properties involving edges and edge sets. In contrast, there is a fixed CMSO 2 property whose model-checking problem cannot be solved in polynomial time even on cliques, which have clique-width at most 2, unless E = NE [CMR00]. On the other hand, several types of combinatorial optimization problems that interfere with optimal solutions have been studied actively, including robust optimization [KZ16], network inter-diction [SPG13], uniquification problems [An+25; Hor+24], quantified integer programming [CH17; 1arXiv:2608.20175v1 [cs.DS] 20 Aug 2026 NP22], and other multilevel optimization problems. 1.1 Techniques 

We sketch a high-level overview of our algorithmic results. Our algorithm is based on the Feferman– Vaught theorem [FV59], a key tool for obtaining dynamic-programming algorithms for MSO model checking on graphs of bounded clique-width or treewidth. An extension of the Feferman–Vaught theorem to MSO states that the truth value of any MSO sentence ϕ on the disjoint union of two graphs G and H can be determined from the truth values, on G and H, of MSO formulas whose quantifier rank is at most that of ϕ. Here, the quantifier rank is a complexity measure of a formula, defined as the maximum depth of nested quantifiers. A fundamental fact of finite model theory is that the number of MSO formulas of quantifier rank at most q is finite up to logical equivalence. Thus, Feferman–Vaught theorem implies that the truth value of ϕ on the disjoint union of two graphs can be determined by a finite number of truth values of MSO formulas on the two graphs. We extend the Feferman–Vaught theorem of MSO to our AmMSO logic, and show that the model checking problem of AmMSO can be solved by dynamic programming on a decomposition tree of the input graph. The strategy of our extension is based on the following observation. 3.2.1 MSO 2 and treewidth 

We remark that our algorithm can be applied to CMSO 2 formulas on graphs of bounded treewidth. An incidence graph of a graph G is a bipartite graph GI with the vertex set V (G) ∪ E(G) and the edge set {{ v, e } : v ∈ V (G), e ∈ E(G), v ∈ e}. Here, an incidence graph can be seen as a graph with 2 colors, where one color is assigned to vertices corresponding to vertices of G and the other color is assigned to vertices corresponding to edges of G. It is folklore that any CMSO 2 formula ϕ on a graph G is equivalent to a CMSO 1 formula ϕ′ on the incidence graph GI . Thus, if the clique-width of the incidence graph GI is small, we can apply our algorithm to AmCMSO 2 formulas. On graphs of bounded treewidth, the treewidth of the incidence graph GI is at most the treewidth of G (see e.g. [Kre11]). Moreover, the clique-width of a 2-colored graph with treewidth 

k is at most f (k) [CE12], where f is a computable function, and such an expression tree can be 10 constructed from a tree decomposition of the graph. Combined with the fact that the clique-width of a graph can be bounded by a function of its treewidth [CR05], we can apply our algorithm for 

AmCMSO 2 formulas on graphs of bounded treewidth. 

Theorem 1.2. Let G be a n-vertex graph with treewidth tw . Let ϕ be an AmCMSO 2 formula. Then, a tuple of sets ⃗ S satisfying G |= ϕ[⃗S] can be found in time f (ϕ, tw ) · n, where f is a computable function.

4.2 Interdiction Problems 

In this subsection, we consider the following types of problems. 

Definition 4.3 (Interdiction Problems [GW25] on Graphs) . Let ϕ(X) be a graph property of vertex-sets or edge-sets. Given a graph G, two weight functions w, w ′, and thresholds t1, t 2 ∈ Z,

ϕ-Interdiction asks whether either of the following formulas holds: 

∃Xw(X) ≤ t1 ∧ ∀ Y  (ϕ(Y ) ∧ w′(Y ) ≤ t2) → X ∩ Y̸ = ∅ , or 

∃Xw(X) ≤ t1 ∧ ∀ Y  (ϕ(Y ) ∧ w′(Y ) ≥ t2) → X ∩ Y̸ = ∅ .

Intuitively, the task of ϕ-Interdiction is to find a small set X such that any “nearly optimal” solution of ϕ must intersect X. Interdiction problems are also known as most vital nodes problems. If t2 is the optimum value of w′(Y ) such that ϕ(Y ) holds, we call ϕ-Interdiction the Optimal-

ϕ-Interdiction problem. These combinatorial optimization problems have been widely investi-gated [Bai+10; BGV89; DK18; FS99; GW25; Zen10]. Recently, it was shown that for many proper-ties ϕ(X) whose decision version is NP-hard, the interdiction variant becomes Σ P2 -complete [GW25]. 17 Here, the constraint |Y | ≤ b can be expressed by an FO -formula of length O(b). Thus, if ϕ is definable in CMSO 1 (or CMSO 2), this type of problem is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by t2 + cw (or t2 + tw ), where cw is clique-width (or tw is treewidth), using an algorithm for 

LinECMSO [ALS91; CM93; CMR00]. Our method improves the dependency on the parameter t2

to |t2 − αϕ(G, w ′)|, where αϕ(G, w ′) is the optimum value of w′(Y ) over all Y such that ϕ(Y ) holds. More formally, we obtain the following by Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2. 

Definition 4.4 (Most vital nodes of δ-approximate CMSO solutions) . Let ϕ(X) be a CMSO formula with one free variable X. Given a graph G, a weight function w : V (G) → Z, and a parameter 

δ ≥ 0, Most Vital Nodes of δ-Approximate ϕ-Solutions asks for a minimum-weight vertex set X such that 

∀Y ⊆ V (G) (( ϕ(Y ) ∧ w(Y ) ≤ opt + δ) → X ∩ Y̸ = ∅),

where opt is the minimum w(Y ) such that ϕ(Y ) holds. After determining δ′ as described at the beginning of this section, the condition ϕ(Y ) ∧ w(Y ) ≤

opt + δ can be handled by an Am δCMSO formula, and we obtain the following corollaries. 

Corollary 4.5. Let ϕ(X) be a CMSO 1 formula with one free variable X. Let G be a graph with clique-width cw and δ ≥ 0. Then, Most Vital Nodes of δ-Approximate ϕ-Solutions is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by cw + δ + |ϕ|.

Corollary 4.6. Let ϕ(X) be a CMSO 2 formula with one free variable X. Let G be a graph with treewidth tw and δ ≥ 0. Then, Most Vital Nodes of δ-Approximate ϕ-Solutions is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by tw + δ + |ϕ|.

Examples The Length-Bounded Cut problem is a well-studied interdiction variant of the 

Shortest Path problem. In Length-Bounded Cut , given a graph G, vertices s, t ∈ V (G), and a positive integer λ, the task is to find a minimum-cardinality edge set F ⊆ E(G) such that there is no s-t path of length at most λ in G − F . The decision version of Length-Bounded Cut is NP-complete [Bai+10] even for λ = 4, W[1]-hard parameterized by the pathwidth plus the maximum degree [DK18], and can be solved in λO(tw 2)n time [DK18] where tw is the treewidth of the input graph. Let ds,t be the length of the shortest s-t path in G and δ(λ) be λ − ds,t . It is known [Cou97] that the property that an edge set Y forms an s-t path can be expressed by an MSO 2 formula Path s,t (Y ). Moreover, for an edge set F , an edge set Y forms an s-t path on G − F if and only if Y ∩ F = ∅ and Y is an s-t path on G. Therefore, the desired edge set X can be defined by an 

Am δ(λ)MSO 2-formula. Thus, we obtain an FPT-algorithm parameterized by δ(λ) plus treewidth by Theorem 1.2. 

Minimum Spanning Tree Interdiction is the interdiction variant of Minimum Spanning Tree . Recall that a spanning tree is an acyclic edge set that connects all vertices; this property is definable in MSO 2. Minimum Spanning Tree Interdiction is NP-hard in general [BGV89], and W[1]-hard parameterized by the weight t2 of the spanning tree [GS14], but Corollary 4.6 applies. Interdiction variants of Matching [GS14; Zen10], Independent Set [BTT11], and Vertex Cover [BTT11] have also been studied. These problems are NP-hard in general but we can apply Corollary 4.5 or Corollary 4.6. 

4.3 Unique Optimum 

A forcing set F for perfect matchings is a subset of edges such that there is exactly one perfect matching M with F ⊆ M . The forcing number for perfect matchings of a graph G is the smallest 18 cardinality of a forcing set, and it is NP-hard to compute the forcing number [AHM04]. Note that the property of having a unique perfect matching is definable in MSO 2, and thus by Courcelle’s theorem [ALS91; CM93] computing the forcing number is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by treewidth. # 6 Conclusion 

We have shown that extensions of MSO with Argmin and Argmax operators remain tractable on graphs of bounded clique-width. This contrasts with MSO extended by cardinality comparisons, which is known to be intractable on graphs of bounded clique-width [DGH25; Sze11]. For many optimization problems over optimal feasible solutions, our theorem removes the dependence on a cardinality parameter k.From the viewpoint of combinatorial optimization, it is natural to ask whether width parameters are useful for solving other Σ P 

> 2

-type problems, such as Stackelberg games and minimization of maximum regret. Another interesting open question is the complexity of min-max-min-type MSO problems. By the result of LinEMSO , we can compute ∃X(|X| ≤ k ∧ ϕ(X)) in FPT time [CMR00], and we also know that ∃X(|X| ≤ kx ∧ ∀ Y (|Y | ≤ ky → ϕ(X, Y ))) type problems are XP [DGH25], when parameterized by the clique-width of the input graph and an MSO formula ϕ. However, to the best of our knowledge, it is not known whether problems of the form ∃X(|X| ≤ kx ∧ ∀ Y (|Y | ≤ 

ky → ∃ Z(|Z| ≤ kz ∧ ϕ(X, Y, Z )))) are in XP or not when parameterized by the clique-width of the input graph and ϕ. In the context of integer linear programming, it is known that ∃⃗x : A⃗ x ≤ b and 

∀⃗y ∃x : A⃗ x + B⃗ y ≤ b are polynomial-time solvable when the number of variables is fixed [Kan90], but ∃⃗x ∀⃗y ∃⃗z : A⃗ x + B⃗ y + C⃗ z ≤ b is NP-hard even when the number of variables is fixed [NP22]. Thus, it is natural to ask whether a similar result holds for max-min-max-type problems, even when the clique-width of the input graph and ϕ are fixed.
